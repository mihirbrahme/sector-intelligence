In 90 seconds

What it does

Information management and compliance platform dominating commercial tanker questionnaires, oil major vetting, and crew matrix qualification

Where it is used

Dry bulk ocean logistics and chartering

What it does against the unit of work

Q88 executes operations against the vessel technical specification, tanker questionnaire (Intertanko Standard Form), officer experience matrix, statutory certificate, and oil major vetting inspection report.

In wet bulk shipping (Model 11), a tanker cannot lift a cargo without passing rigorous commercial vetting by the oil company or commodity trader. Q88 digitizes this compliance gate: - Standardized Questionnaires: Maintains the global standard electronic questionnaire (Q88 for oil/chemical tankers, VPQ for gas carriers), managing over 1,000 vessel engineering parameters (manifold dimensions, pump capacities, segregated ballast layouts). - Officer Matrix Compliance: Tracks the sea-time experience of vessel masters, chief engineers, and senior watchkeepers to verify compliance with oil major minimum experience rules (e.g. combined officer experience with company and on vessel type). - Vetting and Inspection Tracking: Aggregates Port State Control (PSC) records, Ship Inspection Report Programme (SIRE) reports, and Chemical Distribution Institute (CDI) audit findings. - Certificate Governance: Monitors expiration and renewal milestones for statutory international maritime certificates (Class, SOLAS, MARPOL, ISM, ISPS).

Models and sub-sectors

  • Operating models: Model 11 (Tanker Shipping).
  • Sub-sectors: Crude oil tankers (VLCC, Suezmax, Aframax), clean product tankers (MR1, MR2, LR1, LR2), chemical parcel tankers, and LPG/LNG gas carriers.
  • Commercial model: Annual subscription fee per registered vessel paid by shipowners and technical managers, alongside subscription access fees for charterers, terminal operators, and shipbrokers.

Market position and customer profile

Q88 holds an absolute global monopoly in liquid bulk questionnaire administration and commercial vetting preparation.

  • Market share: Over 75% of the world's commercial tanker fleet and more than 1,000 shipowners and technical managers use Q88.
  • Customer profile: Independent tanker owners (Euronav, Frontline, Teekay, Scorpio Tankers), oil majors (Shell, ExxonMobil, BP, Chevron, TotalEnergies), and global commodity trading houses.
  • Network standard: The Q88 format, developed in collaboration with INTERTANKO, is the universal questionnaire format across global wet freight chartering.

Where it is weak

Q88 is a specialized compliance and technical specification repository, not an operational execution ERP: 1. No technical maintenance engine: Q88 stores vessel specifications and certificates; it does not direct onboard planned maintenance systems (PMS), manage spare parts inventory, or handle dry-dock work orders (which require Danaos or ABS Nautical Systems). 2. SIRE 2.0 transition challenges: The shift by OCIMF to SIRE 2.0—a risk-based, tablet-driven physical inspection model—has required extensive updates to questionnaire mapping and operator reporting routines. 3. Manual officer matrix inputs: While integrating with some crew management systems, updates to officer sea-time matrices frequently require manual human resource validation, creating potential compliance gaps during crew changes.

Data model and integration

  • Integration with Veson: Following the 2022 acquisition, Q88's vessel master database is natively integrated into the Veson IMOS Platform, allowing chartering desks to verify vetting status directly inside voyage estimation screens.
  • API framework: RESTful APIs allowing technical ship managers to sync certificate renewal dates and crew change data with internal ERPs.

Ownership and consolidation

  • Founded in 2001 by Fritz Heidenreich in Norwalk, Connecticut.
  • Maintained an exclusive software partnership with INTERTANKO to govern the standard questionnaire.
  • Acquired in May 2022 by Veson Nautical, consolidating the dominant tanker compliance database with the dominant ocean chartering software.

What tends to go wrong

  • off-hire and technical downtime convert owned fleet into idle capital: In tanker shipping, an oil major vetting rejection (e.g. BP or Shell refusing a tanker due to an unresolved SIRE deficiency or lapsed certificate in Q88) immediately renders a vessel commercially unfixable. The vessel sits at anchor off-hire, incurring operating costs of $8,000–$12,000/day without revenue.
  • carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity (Carrier-selection liability): Vetting in tanker carriage is the ultimate defense against casualty liability. Commodity traders and oil companies use Q88 to verify that a tanker has no open structural deficiencies or sanctions violations before executing a fixture.
  • charter counterparty default interrupts cash and voyage control (Charter counterparty default): Ensures vessels maintain continuous class and statutory certifications required to keep charterparty contracts valid, preventing counterparty contract cancellations.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: Q88 claims to eliminate vetting delays, streamline maritime compliance, and automate tanker marketing globally.
  • Industry bodies and maritime standards: INTERTANKO and OCIMF guidelines identify the Q88 and VPQ standards as the indispensable operational protocols underpinning tanker chartering and port terminal clearance worldwide.
