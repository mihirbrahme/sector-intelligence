What it does

Bank-chartered financial institution and enterprise software provider dominating North American freight audit, payment processing, and carrier disbursement

Where it is used

Inland liquid-bulk marine / tank barge

What it does against the unit of work

Cass Information Systems processes the carrier freight bill, rate schedule line, proof of delivery, and electronic disbursement remittance.

The firm operates a federally chartered commercial bank (Cass Commercial Bank). This enables it to securely hold shipper freight funds and disburse payments directly to motor carriers, railroads, ocean shipping lines, and airlines.

Cass ingests freight bills electronically (EDI 210, API, scanned PDF). Its audit engine matches invoices three-way against client bill-of-lading records and executed carrier contracts.

The system validates mileage tables, fuel surcharge escalators, and accessorial fees. Discrepancies are flagged for automated adjustment or client review.

Once approved, Cass issues consolidated billing to the shipper and executes daily payments to carriers via automated clearing house (ACH) transfers or checks.

Cass compiles and publishes the monthly Cass Freight Index, an industry benchmark tracking North American shipment volumes and expenditures.

Work it carries

  • Freight audit and payment automation: Executing automated three-way document matching and contractual freight bill validation.
  • Detention and demurrage dispute recovery: Verifying driver detention charges against signed electronic bills of lading.
  • Benchmarking data consortium: Publishing aggregated macroeconomic freight expenditure indices.

Models and geographies

  • Operating models: shipper / BCO in-house transportation management — a buyer type, not one of the 13 operating models; secondarily enterprise 3PLs managing freight settlement.
  • Geographies: Concentrated in North America (United States, Canada, Mexico), with specialized cross-border audit capabilities.
  • Commercial model: Transaction processing fees ($0.50 to $1.50 per invoice), software portal subscription fees, and net interest margin earned on freight payment float balances held during settlement.

Where it is weak

Cass enforces strict banking and audit controls. When client transportation contracts contain informal agreements or unratified accessorial rules, the audit engine rejects substantial invoice volume.

Exception queues overwhelm the shipper's internal logistics analysts. If the shipper fails to resolve billing exceptions promptly, motor carriers place accounts on credit hold.

The platform's user interfaces reflect conservative banking heritage, lacking the modular API flexibility of modern audit software.

Its international footprint outside North America is smaller. Multinational shippers must integrate regional audit providers for European VAT or Asian billing.

This exception bottleneck is documented in exception resolution requires commercial authority not workflow software.

Who owns the preparation

Cass Information Systems explicitly refuses carrier contract renegotiation and rate sheet digitisation.

Cass mandates that the client provide executed, structured rate schedules, fuel matrices, and carrier remit-to profiles.

Cass will not resolve commercial disputes between a shipper and a carrier regarding service failures or contested accessorial charges.

Shippers must either deploy internal transportation resources or engage third-party freight consultancies to establish clean contract baselines prior to onboarding.

This dynamic reflects logistics automation preconditions are data and contract alignment.

Who it displaces

  • Fortune 500 Industrial & CPG Shippers: Entrenched standard for large enterprise shippers with annual freight spend exceeding $100 million.
  • Heavy Rail and Multimodal Shippers: Market leader in complex North American Class I rail freight audit and contract administration.
  • Mid-Market Shippers (<$50M spend): Often underserved due to minimum transaction volume thresholds; mid-market clients favor regional audit bureaus.

What tends to go wrong

  • billing exception leakage: Cass is the primary external firewall against billing exception leakage for enterprise shippers. By enforcing mathematical contract validation against rate schedules, it identifies overcharges, duplicate billings, and unauthorized accessorials before cash disbursement.
  • purchased-transportation accrual failure: Ingests hundreds of thousands of carrier bills, providing weekly unvouchered accrual files to corporate general ledgers to prevent transportation liability understatements.
  • detention and demurrage under-recovery: Audits rail demurrage and driver detention invoices against contractual free-time thresholds and gate logs.
  • avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty: Cass sits on the enterprise shipper side of the working capital float, managing invoice audit and disbursement schedules while motor carriers seek accelerated settlement.
  • escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation (Escalator and surcharge recovery lag): Automatically recalculates carrier fuel surcharges weekly against the Department of Energy (DOE) diesel index, preventing inaccurate fuel billing.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: Cass marketing claims total payment visibility, elimination of duplicate billing, and strict treasury compliance.
  • Audited corporate filings: Cass Information Systems SEC Form 10-K filings disclose that the company processes approximately $40 billion in freight expenditures annually, with transportation invoice processing driving the core of corporate fee income.
  • Financial industry benchmarks: Standard & Poor's and banking equity research recognize Cass as the dominant institutional payment processor for North American corporate logistics spend.
