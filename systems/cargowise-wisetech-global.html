In 90 seconds

What it does

Enterprise freight forwarding and customs execution platform with dominant market share across global freight forwarders

Where it is used

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

What it does against the unit of work

WiseTech Global (CargoWise) processes the international forwarding shipment file, house bill of lading, master bill of lading, and national customs declaration.

The platform combines forwarding operations, customs clearance, container tracking, terminal cartage, and branch financial accounting in a single shared database.

CargoWise's core engineering strategy differs from competitors: it avoids loose API integrations. It systematically rewrites acquired regional customs engines into its centralized codebase.

Work it carries

  • Document processing and customs entry automation: Generating electronic customs declarations and automated compliance filings.
  • Detention and demurrage dispute recovery: Logging container gate events and tracking ocean carrier detention free-time.
  • Carrier and customer master data consolidation: Maintaining centralized agent and customer entity records globally.

Models and geographies

  • Operating models: Model 2 (Freight Forwarding / NVOCC).
  • Geographies: Global footprint across 180+ countries. Universal operational standard across Asia-Europe, Transpacific, and Latin American freight forwarding corridors.
  • Commercial model: Transactional usage licensing. Forwarders pay per registered shipment file, customs entry, or transport document under "On-Demand Licensing" (ODL) contracts. Contracts feature structured volume commitments and steep commercial penalties for platform departure.

Where it is weak

CargoWise exhibits extreme implementation rigidity. The system requires forwarders to align their operational workflows strictly to the CargoWise process hierarchy.

The user interface is dense, data-heavy, and unforgiving. Frontline forwarding operators require months of training to reach baseline efficiency.

Implementations routinely stall during global branch rollouts. Regional branch managers accustomed to autonomous local billing resist centralized corporate charge codes.

Switching costs are virtually prohibitive. Extracting historical customs filings, job profitability archives, and tax records from CargoWise requires multi-year IT migrations.

Who owns the preparation

WiseTech Global explicitly refuses master data cleansing, customer tariff digitization, and branch process alignment.

WiseTech does not provide primary professional implementation services. Instead, it directs customers to independent "CargoWise Certified Service Partners."

The forwarder must clean decades of dirty master data, reconcile disparate agent profiles, and standardize global chart of accounts before deployment.

Clients that under-invest in master data preparation experience prolonged implementation overruns and billing exception chaos.

This dynamic confirms logistics automation preconditions are data and contract alignment.

Who it displaces

  • Top 25 Global Freight Forwarders: CargoWise is deployed across major business units in 24 of the top 25 global freight forwarders (including DSV, Kuehne+Nagel, DHL Global Forwarding, Bolloré, Geodis, and Schenker).
  • Mid-Market Forwarders ($50M–$500M gross revenue): High enterprise penetration; viewed as the default platform required to handle multinational tender business.
  • Small Forwarders (<$20M gross revenue): Penetration is limited by transactional minimum spend commitments and high certified-partner consulting costs.

What tends to go wrong

  • forwarding cost pass-through lag on the open shipment file (Forwarding cost pass-through lag on open file): In CargoWise, shipment files accumulate estimated cost accruals. If final carrier invoices or destination terminal charges arrive after the forwarder issues the final customer invoice, the cost variances must be absorbed into job P&L unless the file is reopened and rebilled.
  • forwarding receivable extension funded on gross turnover: Forwarders using CargoWise to disburse customs duties and VAT advance substantial capital relative to their net service fees, extending working capital exposure on gross turnover.
  • systems programme trailing the forwarding process it was bought to fix (Systems programme trailing operational process): Enterprise CargoWise implementations average 3 to 5 years. While branches wait for centralized system cutover, regional forwarding teams continue running parallel localized billing systems.
  • billing exception leakage: Discrepancies between expected ocean carrier rates and billed invoices require manual charge-code reconciliation in the job accounting module.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: WiseTech claims 90%+ straight-through file processing, dramatic labor productivity improvements, and zero-touch customs filings.
  • Audited corporate filings: WiseTech Global (ASX: WTC) FY2025 audited financial statements demonstrate >99% annual customer retention among large forwarders, EBITDA margins exceeding 50%, and >95% recurring revenue, proving extraordinary commercial lock-in.
  • Trade press and industry reports: Reporting in The Loadstar and Journal of Commerce confirms that enterprise CargoWise implementations at Tier 1 forwarders average three to five years and cost tens of millions of dollars in external partner fees.
