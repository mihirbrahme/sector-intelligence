In 90 seconds

What it does

Financial technology and freight factoring platform providing working capital financing and automated carrier payments for freight brokerages

Where it is used

Truckload Brokerage

What it does against the unit of work

Denim executes operations against the broker freight invoice, shipper receivable ledger, carrier quick-pay disbursement, and working capital cash advance.

The platform provides an integrated financial operating layer for small to mid-sized freight brokerages: - Freight Factoring & Working Capital: Advances cash on uncollected shipper invoices, allowing the broker to pay motor carriers immediately without drawing on senior bank credit lines. - Carrier Quick-Pay: Executes next-day ACH or digital payments to carriers upon receipt of delivery paperwork, charging a financing discount fee. - Automated Invoicing & Collections: Generates branded freight invoices to shippers, monitors accounts receivable aging, and manages customer payment collection. - Credit Risk Assessment: Evaluates shipper creditworthiness in real time, setting maximum credit limits for brokerage accounts to prevent bad debt write-offs.

Models and sub-sectors

  • Operating models: Model 1 (Truckload Brokerage).
  • Sub-sectors: Boutique freight brokers, specialized truckload intermediaries, flatbed/refrigerated brokers, and fast-growing startup brokerages.
  • Commercial model: Factoring discount fee charged as a percentage of gross freight invoice value (typically 1.5% to 3.5% depending on volume and credit risk), plus platform transaction fees.

Market position and customer profile

Denim targets the substantial long tail of North American freight brokerages that lack access to traditional commercial bank credit lines.

  • Market position: Fast-growing fintech alternative to traditional freight factoring firms (Triumph Business Capital, RTS Financial, Apex Capital) and legacy bank factoring facilities.
  • Customer profile: Small to mid-market freight brokerages generating $1 million to $50 million in annual gross freight revenue.
  • Differentiator: Modern self-service software interface, automated QuickBooks/TMS integrations, and flexible recourse/non-recourse financing options.

Where it is weak

Denim's financial model carries operational boundaries and margin friction: 1. High cost of capital: Factoring fees (1.5% to 3.5% of gross revenue) consume a substantial share of a broker's net revenue spread (12% to 16% gross margin), making it expensive compared to asset-based bank lines. 2. Strict shipper credit caps: Denim limits funding on unrated, financially troubled, or slow-paying shippers. Brokers must decline customer loads if Denim refuses to underwrite the credit line. 3. Customer relationship friction: Shippers receiving collection calls or revised remit-to instructions from a third-party fintech factor can push back against the intermediary relationship. 4. Not designed for enterprise brokers: Brokers scaling past $50 million gross volume typically graduate from Denim to secure cheaper commercial bank revolvers.

Data model and integration

  • Architecture: Modern cloud fintech web application with real-time financial dashboards and carrier payment portals.
  • TMS Integrations: Pre-built API connectors to freight brokerage TMS platforms, including Revenova, Tai TMS, AscendTMS, and McLeod PowerBroker.
  • Accounting Connectors: Bi-directional data sync with QuickBooks Online, NetSuite, and Xero.
  • Banking Rails: Modern payment APIs connecting directly to Plaid, ACH networks, and debit card push rails.

Ownership and consolidation

  • Founded in 2019 in New York by Bharath Krishnamoorthy and Shawn Vo as Axle Payments.
  • Rebranded to Denim in September 2022.
  • Backed by venture capital and warehouse debt facilities, raising over $150 million in equity and debt led by Pelion Venture Partners, Tribeca Venture Partners, and Silicon Valley Bank.

What tends to go wrong

  • avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty: Denim exists entirely because of avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty. Brokers must pay truckers within 2 to 15 days to secure capacity, but corporate shippers pay in 45 to 60 days. Because working capital requirements scale with gross freight spend rather than net revenue, a growing broker rapidly burns through cash. Denim finances this gap by advancing up to 95% of invoice value.
  • billing exception leakage: Denim audits customer invoice packets before dispatching bills, ensuring all rate confirmation terms, signed PODs, and billable accessorials are attached.
  • purchased-transportation accrual failure: Integrates payment disbursement directly with load execution, creating a clean audit trail between broker accounts payable and carrier payments.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: Denim claims to eliminate broker cash-flow worries, automate 90% of back-office financial tasks, and unlock unlimited brokerage growth.
  • Fintech industry and venture disclosures: Reporting in TechCrunch and FreightWaves documents Denim's deployment across hundreds of freight brokerages transacting over $500 million in cumulative freight volume, while confirming that factoring remains an interim financing bridge for brokers prior to securing traditional bank debt.
