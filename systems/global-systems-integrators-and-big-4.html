What it does

Global Systems Integrators (GSIs) and accounting advisory practices that implement enterprise supply chain software, execute IT migrations, and provide business process outsourcing

What it does against the unit of work

Global Systems Integrators and Big 4 firms process the consulting billing day, billable developer hour, project milestone, and business process outsourcing (BPO) transaction.

These organizations deploy large project teams to configure enterprise applications (SAP, Oracle, Blue Yonder, Manhattan), build custom middleware, and oversee technical go-lives.

They manage post-merger IT integrations, unifying disparate ERP, TMS, and WMS databases across newly acquired operating divisions.

Their business process outsourcing (BPO) divisions deliver offshore human-in-the-loop processing for paper freight invoices, manual customs entry indexing, and track-and-trace monitoring.

The sector separates into two service structures:

  • Global Systems Integrators and IT Majors: Accenture, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), Infosys, Cognizant, Genpact, Wipro. They provide enterprise systems integration, software engineering, and offshore transactional BPO operations.

  • Big 4 Accounting and Advisory Firms: Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG. They lead supply chain target operating model design, package evaluation, tax and customs advisory, and enterprise program governance.

Work it carries

  • Carrier and customer master data consolidation: Constructing enterprise data lakes, master data management (MDM) platforms, and data migration pipelines.
  • Document processing and customs entry automation: Staffing offshore shared service centers to key customs entries and parse shipping documents.
  • Warehouse labour productivity instrumentation: Integrating warehouse execution metrics into enterprise ERP financial modules.

Models and geographies

  • Operating models: Applied across all models, with concentration in Model 1 (Brokerage), Model 2 (Forwarding), Model 3 (Contract Logistics), and shipper / BCO in-house transportation management — a buyer type, not one of the 13 operating models.
  • Geographies: Global delivery model. Onshore advisory, program management, and client leadership sit in North America and Western Europe; offshore engineering and operations centers operate in India, the Philippines, and Poland.
  • Commercial model: Primarily structured under time and materials billing against tiered rate cards. Major software cutovers use milestone-based fixed-fee arrangements. Managed services use per-transaction BPO or monthly FTE pricing.

Where it is weak

GSI economic incentives conflict with rapid project resolution. Under time-and-materials contracts, protracted client data chaos expands billable hours and project scope.

High staff turnover in offshore development centers dilutes institutional memory. Junior delivery analysts often lack basic operational understanding of freight forwarding files, drayage queues, or driver hours-of-service rules.

Firms excel at coding software to written specifications. They falter when system failure stems from operational ambiguity, unwritten carrier agreements, or decentralized branch resistance.

When custom code proliferates to accommodate regional branch exceptions, the client inherits unsustainable technical debt that blocks future software updates.

For major historical implementation collapses, see monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace.

Who owns the preparation

Global SIs willingly accept precondition work because data cleansing and process mapping generate massive consulting billings.

However, they treat preconditions as a brute-force manual exercise. They assign junior analysts to re-key data into staging tables without resolving underlying contractual ambiguities.

They document current-state operational chaos rather than forcing standardisation across operating divisions.

Consequently, clients spend tens of millions of dollars on data normalization without eliminating the root operational failure mechanisms.

This dynamic reflects logistics automation preconditions are data and contract alignment.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: Firms market enterprise digital modernization, 30% operational cost reduction through labor arbitrage, and accelerated business value.
  • Court filings and public write-downs: Public legal disputes and corporate disclosures document catastrophic supply chain IT failures managed by major integrators (e.g., DHL writing off €345 million in 2015 after abandoning its SAP-based New Forwarding Environment project).
  • Analyst benchmarks: Everest Group and Gartner IT research show that over 45% of enterprise supply chain system integration programs experience scope overruns requiring formal budget revisions.
