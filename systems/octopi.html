In 90 seconds

What it does

Cloud-native terminal operating system (TOS) designed for small to mid-sized container, bulk, and mixed-cargo maritime terminals

Where it is used

Ports & Terminals

What it does against the unit of work

Octopi executes operations against the container lift, reachstacker move, breakbulk tally, vessel hatch sequence, and gate ticket.

The platform was built as a lightweight, cloud-hosted alternative to legacy on-premise TOS platforms: - Vessel Planning: Ingests ocean carrier EDIFACT (BAPLIE) stowage files, allowing vessel planners to drag-and-drop container sequences via a web browser. - Yard Management: Provides 3D yard graphical mapping. Reachstacker and top-pick operators use mobile tablets mounted in vehicle cabs to confirm container moves in real time. - Gate Operations: Replaces manual paper gate passes with mobile gate inspection apps that record container numbers, seal integrity, and chassis damage photos. - Mixed Cargo Tracking: Manages non-containerized breakbulk, bulk commodities, and project cargo alongside container inventories in a unified ledger.

Models and sub-sectors

  • Operating models: Model 6 (Ports & Terminals).
  • Sub-sectors: Regional feeder ports, river terminals, Caribbean/Pacific island ports, mixed-cargo breakbulk terminals, and private industrial wharves.
  • Commercial model: Subscription SaaS model based on annual container and cargo throughput volume. Deployment involves low upfront setup fees and remote onboarding completed in 8 to 16 weeks.

Market position and customer profile

Octopi targets the vast tier of regional terminals historically priced out of enterprise systems like Navis N4.

  • Market position: Leading modern cloud-native TOS for regional and mid-market terminal operations.
  • Customer profile: Terminals handling between 20,000 TEU and 300,000 TEU annually (with upper limits around 500,000 TEU). Deployed across terminals in the Caribbean (Port of Kingston feeder berths, Bahamas), Latin America, Southeast Asian river ports, and regional European wharves.
  • Competitive alternatives: Terminals at this tier typically run Microsoft Excel spreadsheets, paper tally sheets, or aging in-house legacy software.

Where it is weak

Octopi is deliberately scoped for smaller facilities and hits hard boundaries when operational complexity escalates: 1. No equipment automation support: Octopi cannot orchestrate automated stacking cranes (ASCs) or automated guided vehicles (AGVs). It lacks a low-level equipment control layer (TLS). 2. Simplified decking algorithms: Stacking logic is primarily manual or semi-automated. It lacks the mathematical yard-reshuffle optimization algorithms of N4 Expert Decking. 4. National customs integrations: Regional ports require custom electronic data interfaces with national customs administrations (e.g. ASYCUDA World, Ceisa 4.0). Building custom EDI links extends deployment timelines.

Data model and integration

Octopi runs on a modern SaaS stack built on Amazon Web Services (AWS).

  • Architecture: Multi-tenant cloud application accessed via modern web browsers and iOS/Android tablet applications.
  • API framework: RESTful APIs allowing direct integration with third-party truck appointment systems, accounting software (QuickBooks, SAP), and smart gate cameras.
  • EDI engine: Pre-configured parsers for maritime EDI standards: BAPLIE, MOVINS, COARRI, CODECO, and EDI 315 status messages.
  • Hardware footprint: Requires zero on-site servers, database administrators, or local IT infrastructure; relies on standard commercial cellular tablets and ruggedized vehicle mounts.

Ownership and consolidation

  • Founded in 2015 in Miami, Florida by software entrepreneur Luc Castera.
  • Acquired by Navis (then part of Cargotec) in March 2019 to capture the underserved mid-market and small-port segment.
  • Transferred to private equity firm Accel-KKR in 2021 as part of the Navis sale, operating as a cloud product line within Kaleris.

What tends to go wrong

  • berth, yard and landside constraints turn terminal density into delay (Berth, yard and landside constraints): In regional terminals, container yards easily choke when dwell exceeds 10 days. Octopi provides visual heat maps of dwell time, helping planners quarantine aged import containers before they block active loading lanes.
  • detention and demurrage under-recovery: Mid-market terminals lose significant revenue failing to bill container storage demurrage. Octopi automates demurrage invoicing by locking gate release permissions until accumulated storage fees are paid.
  • systems programme trailing the forwarding process it was bought to fix (Systems programme trailing operational process): Octopi was engineered to prevent systems programme trailing the forwarding process it was bought to fix in terminal operations. By deploying in 8 to 12 weeks via SaaS, it avoids the multi-year IT implementation failures typical of legacy enterprise TOS migrations.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: Kaleris marketing claims Octopi can be deployed in under 90 days, increases crane moves per hour by 25%, and eliminates paperwork completely.
  • Port operator case studies: Deployments at regional Caribbean and African ports confirm rapid cutover times (2 to 3 months) and dramatic reductions in lost container incidents, balanced against limitations when handling high-density container volumes over 500,000 TEU.
