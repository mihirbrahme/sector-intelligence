In 90 seconds

What it does

Cloud-native multi-enterprise collaborative transportation management system (TMS) connecting shippers, logistics providers, and motor carriers

Where it is used

Truckload Brokerage, Contract Logistics / 3PL Warehousing, Inland liquid-bulk marine / tank barge

What it does against the unit of work

Turvo executes operations against the shared shipment record, contextual message thread, digital proof of delivery (ePOD), driver tracking geofence, and billing packet.

Its core thesis is "collaborative logistics." Instead of siloed databases emailing updates back and forth, all supply chain participants view and interact with the same digital shipment record: - Collaborative Network: Shippers, brokers, carriers, and drivers share real-time visibility, chat messages, and documents in a single contextual feed. - Automated Exception Management: Geofencing and predictive telematics trigger automated alerts when drivers encounter transit delays or extended loading dwell. - Digital Document Ingestion: Mobile driver app captures digital signatures and photo PODs at delivery, immediately indexing them to the settlement record. - Order-to-Cash Workflow: Automates load booking, carrier dispatch, tracking check-calls, and customer billing.

Models and sub-sectors

  • Operating models: Model 1 (Truckload Brokerage), Model 3 (Contract Logistics), and shipper / BCO in-house transportation management — a buyer type, not one of the 13 operating models.
  • Sub-sectors: Temperature-controlled freight (cold chain), food and beverage distribution, CPG, and collaborative 3PL/brokerage networks.
  • Commercial model: Subscription SaaS model based on annual shipment volume or freight spend tiers, with professional onboarding fees.

Market position and customer profile

Turvo is viewed as one of the most technologically advanced and design-forward cloud TMS platforms in North America.

  • Market position: Leading collaborative TMS challenger, winning market share among mid-market and enterprise 3PLs looking to replace legacy systems.
  • Customer profile: 3PLs and freight brokerages managing $50 million to over $1 billion in freight spend, cold chain logistics operators, and enterprise shippers (Lineage Logistics, Ryder, Oberman, Port X Logistics).
  • Flagship backer: Anchored by parent company Lineage, the world's largest temperature-controlled warehousing and logistics REIT.

Where it is weak

Turvo's collaboration model hits friction when external counterparties refuse to adopt its workflows: 1. Counterparty portal resistance: The collaboration promise degrades when small motor carriers and independent owner-operators refuse to download the Turvo mobile app or log into carrier portals, forcing coordinators back to phone calls and emails. 2. Premium software pricing: Software subscription pricing is positioned at a premium compared to legacy systems, requiring strong executive justification on coordinator labor savings. 3. Not built for asset fleet maintenance: Lacks heavy mechanical shop maintenance modules, parts inventory tracking, and complex driver mileage payroll engines found in McLeod or Trimble. 4. Integration maturity with legacy ERPs: While modern API integration is clean, connecting Turvo to legacy on-premise ERPs (AS/400, older SAP releases) requires custom middleware development.

Data model and integration

Turvo was built from inception as a cloud-native, API-first microservices platform.

  • Architecture: Multi-tenant cloud application running on Amazon Web Services (AWS) with modern web interfaces and native iOS/Android mobile apps.
  • API & Webhooks: Comprehensive RESTful and GraphQL APIs with real-time webhooks broadcasting shipment milestones and tracking telemetry.
  • EDI engine: Pre-built integration with standard logistics EDI transaction sets (204, 990, 214, 210).
  • Partner ecosystem: Direct pre-configured connectors to digital freight matching platforms, telematics providers (Samsara, Omnitracs), visibility aggregators (project44, FourKites), and payment clearinghouses (TriumphPay).

Ownership and consolidation

  • Founded in 2014 by Eric Fuller, Matt Ramsey, Sai Warang, and Jeff Graan in Sunnyvale, California.
  • Raised approximately $85 million in venture capital from Activant Capital, Next47 (Siemens), and Mubadala Capital.
  • Acquired in June 2022 by Lineage Logistics (now Lineage, Inc.) for an estimated enterprise valuation exceeding $200 million.
  • Continues to operate as an independent commercial software business unit within Lineage.

What tends to go wrong

By consolidating tracking check-calls, transit delays, and status queries into an automated, shared activity feed, it reduces coordinator phone and email touches per load. - detention and demurrage under-recovery: Turvo uses automated mobile geofencing to log precise driver arrival and departure timestamps at shipper and receiver docks, establishing incontrovertible audit records for detention billing. - quote-to-book manual handoff: Provides collaborative customer portal booking, reducing manual re-keying of spot tenders into the execution engine.

What is claimed, and what is corroborated

  • Vendor claims: Turvo claims up to 70% reduction in manual check-calls, 40% improvement in operational productivity, and 100% supply chain transparency.
  • Trade press and acquisition coverage: Reporting in FreightWaves and The Wall Street Journal surrounding Lineage's acquisition highlights Turvo's exceptional UI/UX and collaborative architecture, while noting the industry-wide challenge of driving independent motor carrier app compliance.
