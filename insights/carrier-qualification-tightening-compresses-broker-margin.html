Carrier qualification tightening compresses broker margin

Restricting approved motor carrier capacity to defend against negligent selection liability compresses brokerage gross margin independent of carrier procurement skill.

Core takeaway

Restricting approved motor carrier capacity to defend against negligent selection liability compresses brokerage gross margin independent of carrier procurement skill.

Operating models affected

Truckload Brokerage

Where observed

knight-swift, landstar, ch-robinson, rxo

The distinction

Brokerage gross margin compression is traditionally diagnosed as manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility manual carrier sourcing: reps buying spot capacity inefficiently against volatile market conditions.

There is a distinct legal driver that produces an identical financial signature.

Following adverse legal rulings on broker liability, major brokers aggressively tightened safety vetting algorithms and pruned unrated, high-risk, or non-compliant motor carriers.

The cheapest available truckload capacity is disproportionately provided by unrated, newly formed, or marginal operators.

Foreclosing access to cheap, high-risk capacity forces brokerage desks to buy higher-priced qualified capacity. This compresses gross margin as a deliberate risk-management policy, not a failure of commercial execution.

Where it was observed

At Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc. in 2025-Q4, Logistics gross margin fell 180 basis points to 15.5%. Management explicitly attributed the decline to "enforcing rigorous carrier qualification rules that limited access to discounted third-party capacity."

At Landstar System, Inc., the approved carrier pool was cut by 35% over four years following the Montgomery decision and massive nuclear verdicts (including the $604m Lipe verdict against C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc.).

RXO, Inc. similarly highlighted continuous safety monitoring as a competitive differentiator, knowingly sacrificing access to bottom-tier spot rates.

How to spot it

When evaluating margin compression in operating model 1, check whether approved carrier counts fell or compliance screening criteria tightened.

Review 10-K Item 1A Risk Factors and litigation disclosures for carrier selection scrutiny. If margin compression coincides with lower cargo theft rates and reduced claims reserves, the driver is carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity carrier-selection liability, not procurement weakness in net revenue per load.
