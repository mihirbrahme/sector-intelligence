The trap

This aggregate count is mathematically misleading.

Operating models differ fundamentally in their unit of work, cost structure, and balance sheet. An operational failure inherent to brokerage cannot occur at an asset-heavy LTL carrier or a port terminal operator.

  • A brokerage failure in 5 of 8 companies where 5 are brokers is present in 100% of applicable targets, not 62.5%.
  • A forwarding failure in 2 of 8 companies where only 2 forwarders exist is present in 100% of applicable targets, not 25%.

Where it was observed

Carrier-selection liability (carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity) appeared in five of eight companies. This was not a broad sector finding; it was a 5-of-5 finding within brokerage.

Conversely, ocean spread compression appeared in two of eight companies. That was not weak evidence; it was observed in 2 of the 2 forwarders in the sample (C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc. and Expeditors International of Washington, Inc.). The count reflected the sampling limit of holding only two forwarders.

How to spot it

State prevalence as: numerator / denominator within operating model.

Check the target's classification in operating models. Never publish a cross-model prevalence fraction when evaluating failure patterns in the mechanism library or citing benchmarks in the metric dictionary.
