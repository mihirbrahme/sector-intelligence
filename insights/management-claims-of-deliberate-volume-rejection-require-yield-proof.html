Management claims of deliberate volume rejection require yield proof

Management assertions of walking away from unprofitable volume are untestable anti-patterns without retained-book yield analysis, often disguising market share loss.

In 90 seconds

Core takeaway

Management assertions of walking away from unprofitable volume are untestable anti-patterns without retained-book yield analysis, often disguising market share loss.

Operating models affected

Truckload Brokerage, Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet, LTL, Domestic Intermodal

Where observed

old-dominion, knight-swift, hub-group

The trap

When freight volumes drop, corporate commentary routinely frames the contraction as a strategic choice: "we prioritised yield over volume chasing."

Treat this phrase as an anti-pattern, not an operational finding.

An operator losing customer accounts to competitors and an operator selectively purging low-margin freight produce identical top-line profiles: - Volumes contract. - Average revenue per load appears flat or slightly higher, because losing the cheapest freight mechanically raises the arithmetic average of whatever remains.

Management commentary framing volume loss as disciplined shedding is self-reported.

Where it was observed

  • Old Dominion Freight Line, Inc. ("prioritized profitability over volume chasing").
  • Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc. ("turned away unprofitable freight" in Logistics).
  • Hub Group, Inc. ("volume declined intentionally" in Q1 2026 brokerage).
  • Knight-Swift LTL (shipments −3.7%, weight +7.9%).

At Hub Group, the claim was contradicted by the numbers: in 2025-Q4, brokerage volume fell 10% while gross revenue per load also fell 4%. Freight was lost, not pruned.

How to spot it

Apply the retained-book test: 1. Did total dollar contribution expand? If total margin dollars fell, volume was lost, not shed. 2. Did unit rates rise within retained accounts, or did the average rise solely because the bottom fell out? 3. Was capacity released, or did overhead per unit rise?

Never accept intentional volume rejection in operating models 1, 4, 5, or 7 without verifying retained yield in the metric dictionary.
