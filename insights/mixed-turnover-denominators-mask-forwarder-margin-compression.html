Mixed turnover denominators mask forwarder margin compression

Mixing statutory turnover and ex-duty turnover across disclosure tables masks core margin compression and invalidates peer margin comparisons.

Core takeaway

Mixing statutory turnover and ex-duty turnover across disclosure tables masks core margin compression and invalidates peer margin comparisons.

Operating models affected

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

Where observed

supreme-freight-services

The trap

Freight forwarders handling customs clearance can present revenue under two definitions: statutory gross turnover inclusive of customer duty and VAT disbursements, or net turnover excluding disbursements.

Statutory turnover passes substantial third-party tax payments through the top line. This gross-up dilutes the gross margin percentage by 5 to 6 percentage points compared to operators reporting purely ex-duty turnover.

The hazard intensifies when a company's internal strategic report mixes denominators across adjacent rows of its own financial disclosures. Quoting gross margin derived from statutory turnover beneath an ex-duty turnover figure masks true underlying margin contraction.

Where it was observed

At Supreme Freight Services Limited in FY2025, the directors reported turnover excluding duty and VAT of £57.7m (FY2024: £43.1m) alongside statutory filed turnover of £87.6m (FY2024: £74.5m).

In the strategic report KPI table, the directors reported "Gross margin percentage - 8.6%". This 8.6% was computed against statutory turnover (£87.6m). It sat directly beneath the ex-duty turnover line (£57.7m).

Against statutory turnover, gross margin appeared flat (8.7% in FY2024 to 8.6% in FY2025). On the ex-duty denominator, gross margin contracted 2.0 percentage points, from 15.0% to 13.0%. The mixed presentation masked a significant margin decline during a 33.9% ex-duty revenue expansion.

How to spot it

Compare stated turnover in the strategic report against the audited Profit and Loss statement. Confirm whether duty and VAT are included in revenue and cost of sales.

Recalculate gross margins across both bases consistently. Never compare margins across operating model 2 peers without verifying revenue treatment.
