Accessorial dispute recovery requires contemporaneous operational evidence

Contesting logistics accessorials, detention, and demurrage cannot be solved retroactively because evidentiary standards demand data captured at the exact moment the operational failure occurs.

Core takeaway

Contesting logistics accessorials, detention, and demurrage cannot be solved retroactively because evidentiary standards demand data captured at the exact moment the operational failure occurs.

Operating models affected

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC, Ports & Terminals, Domestic Intermodal

Where observed

the solution domainsdetention-and-demurrage-dispute-recovery, 05-solutions/failure history/consortium-blockchain-trade-documentation

Why it is true

Under regulatory dispute frameworks (such as the FMC Demurrage and Detention Billing Rule under 46 CFR Part 541), defeating carrier per-diem charges requires affirmative proof that cargo was inaccessible.

Cargo interests must prove that marine terminal appointments were unavailable, gates were closed, or chassis were defective on specific dates during free time.

Terminal appointment portals and gate systems do not retain public historical logs of appointment blackouts. Once a time slot passes, the unavailability screen disappears from the portal.

If the drayage operator or forwarder did not capture an automated, time-stamped screenshot and an Equipment Interchange Receipt (EIR) when the failure occurred, retroactive dispute assembly is impossible.

Accounting teams cannot negotiate away accessorial charges post-invoice without contemporaneous operational records.

What was seen

Documented in detention and demurrage dispute recovery and evidenced across Federal Maritime Commission charge complaint dockets.

Shippers and forwarders routinely lose formal OSRA 22 dispute claims despite legitimate port congestion because they submit general carrier delay notices rather than time-stamped portal reservation denial logs.

As noted in consortium blockchain trade documentation, TradeLens failed partly because immutable ledger timestamps could not substitute for specific gate and appointment evidence required under maritime law.

What it changes

Never treat detention, demurrage, and accessorial leakage under detention and demurrage under-recovery as an accounts payable or legal negotiation problem.

When diagnosing accessorial recovery: 1. Audit the physical data capture process at the terminal gate and appointment portal. 2. Require drayage drivers and dispatchers to capture mobile EIR scans and portal blackout screenshots contemporaneously. 3. If an operator lacks automated scraping or driver mobile capture at the physical interface, back-office dispute automation software will produce zero recoveries.
