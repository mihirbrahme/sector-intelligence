Happy path automation leaves unit costs scaling with exceptions

Automating only standard, non-deviating transactions leaves operating unit costs scaling with the operational exception rate, causing productivity gains to plateau.

The distinction

When logistics operators implement automation (such as automated EDI dispatch or electronic rate engines), development resources target the "happy path" — standard transactions that proceed without error.

Standard transactions automate rapidly. However, transactions that deviate — missed pick-up appointments, document mismatches, rate disputes, or damaged freight — cannot complete automatically.

These deviating transactions drop back into manual queues.

Because exceptions require cross-system investigation and manual chasing, a single deviating transaction consumes 5 to 10 times the operational labour of a standard transaction.

When volume expands, the automated path scales efficiently, but exceptions expand alongside it. If the deviation rate is unaddressed, operating costs stop falling. Unit costs plateau despite rising technology spend.

Where it was observed

Across logistics operations, companies frequently report high transaction automation while loads per rep per day or files per operator plateau.

The plateaus occur because operations teams spend upwards of 60% of their working hours manually chasing the 15% of transactions that deviate from the plan.

How to spot it

Apply the exception lens across all operating models: 1. Identify who touches a transaction when an appointment is missed or an invoice rejected. 2. Determine how many times an exception returns to the same desk. 3. Assess whether operating costs scale with overall volume or with the exception rate.

If headcount per unit of work fails to decline following automation, diagnose an exception routing bottleneck.
