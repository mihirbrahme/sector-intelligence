A carrier that discloses no operating statistics cannot carry per-mile benchmarks

Core takeaway

A peer set assembled from operating ratios will contain carriers that publish no miles, no tractor count and no empty-mile percentage. Those carriers belong in the ratio distribution and nowhere else.

Operating models affected

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Where observed

heartland-express

The trap

Sub-sector 4A is benchmarked on four primary measures: operating ratio, revenue per loaded mile, empty-mile percentage and driver turnover. Only the first comes from the income statement. The other three come from an operating statistics table, and that table is voluntary. There is no US GAAP or SEC requirement to publish miles, tractor counts or turnover.

Most large truckload carriers publish them, which makes the exceptions easy to miss. The peer set gets built once from the ratio — the measure everyone has — and then the per-mile columns get filled from whoever happens to disclose, with the peer label left unchanged. The resulting range reads as a distribution across the named peers. It is a distribution across a subset, and the subset is selected by disclosure behaviour, which is not random: carriers stop publishing utilisation and pricing statistics when those statistics are deteriorating.

So the bias runs one way. Absent disclosers are more likely to sit at the weak end, and the range understates the spread.

Where it was observed

At Heartland Express, the FY2025 Form 10-K discloses average tractor age of 2.6 years and average trailer age of 7.3 years. It discloses no tractor count, no trailer count, no loaded miles, no total miles, no empty-mile percentage, no revenue per loaded mile, no revenue per tractor per week and no driver turnover figure. On turnover it states only that "Driver pay, home time, and other amenities have allowed us to maintain driver turnover rates lower than the industry average" — a comparative claim with neither a value nor a comparator. The company also holds no quarterly earnings call, which FreightWaves noted on 2026-07-31: "Heartland does not host a quarterly call, nor does it provide operating metrics for utilization and pricing."

The single volume-adjacent disclosure is that approximately 77% of FY2025 loads ran under 500 miles.

Heartland cannot be inside either range.

It is the worst company in the sample to use for any per-mile reference, because none of those figures exist.

How to spot it

  • Build the peer set per metric, not per company. A company belongs to the operating ratio distribution, the empty-mile distribution and the turnover distribution independently, and the membership lists differ.
  • Record the disclosure, not just the value. Where a peer publishes nothing for a metric, the cell reads not disclosed, never blank and never interpolated. A blank cell is read as zero or as unexceptional.
  • State the population under every range. "13.5%–16.0% across four of six named peers" is usable; "13.5%–16.0%" over a six-name peer list is not.
  • Fleet age is not fleet size. Average tractor age tells you about the capex cycle and says nothing about capacity. the record's nearest mechanism, fleet age capex cliff raises maintenance before replacement is funded, is scoped to marine models 8–13 and does not extend to a road fleet; there is no Model 4 equivalent written.
  • Where the disclosure gap matters, the external substitute is regulatory, not financial: FMCSA SAFER and SMS publish power units and driver counts per USDOT number, and are because they originate outside management. Use them for capacity, and say so.
  • Treat a carrier that stops publishing operating statistics as a dated observation in its own right. The decision is management's, it is reversible, and the period in which it changed is informative.
