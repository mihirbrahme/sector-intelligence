Operating models affected

Where observed

matson

Claim. In vessel operations, crew headcount is set by the sailing schedule and the rotation pattern, not by management discretion. A hiring freeze or a headcount reduction reaches only the shoreside remainder, and changes when a cost lands rather than what the work costs.

Why. A billet is a crew position on an active vessel. The vessel cannot sail without it filled. Each billet is filled by more than one person, because seagoing staff rotate between sea duty and time ashore. Headcount is therefore an arithmetic output: active vessels x positions x rotation multiplier. Three levers reach it — laying up a vessel, shortening a string, or chartering in foreign-flag tonnage where the owner carries the crew.

What was seen. Matson, Inc. FY2025 Form 10-K, filed 2026-02-27, grade ``. The active fleet requires 329 billets; "each billet corresponds to a position on a vessel that typically is filled by two or more employees because seagoing personnel rotate between active sea-duty and time ashore." Total workforce 4,170, of whom 2,843 — 68% — are under collective bargaining agreements. The billet count excludes foreign-flag chartered vessels, where the owner is responsible for seagoing personnel.

Against that structure, the disclosed FY2025 cost response was a hiring freeze, deferred capital spending, and $20m cut from maintenance capital guidance. Headcount fell 4.3%. In the same year cash dry-docking payments rose from $30.2m to $49.4m.

The parallel exists in inland marine on a different constraint. Kirby Corporation discloses inland crew rotations of 20 days on / 10 off or 30 on / 15 off, and coastal rotations of 14/14, 21/21 or 30/30. It also names an industry mariner shortage. Same arithmetic, same conclusion.

What it changes. Before crediting a headcount action as a cost reduction in any crewed-asset model, establish what share of the workforce is billet-driven, and what the rotation multiplier is. Never propose anything that reads as running the crew harder. In a market short of licensed mariners it fails on contact, and at Matson, Inc. it fails at a bargaining table covering two thirds of the workforce.
