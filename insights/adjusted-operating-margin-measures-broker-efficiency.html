Adjusted operating margin measures broker efficiency

Adjusted operating margin, defined as operating income divided by adjusted gross profit, is the model-correct efficiency measure for truckload brokerage.

Core takeaway

Adjusted operating margin, defined as operating income divided by adjusted gross profit, is the model-correct efficiency measure for truckload brokerage.

Operating models affected

Truckload Brokerage

Where observed

ch-robinson

The distinction

Truckload brokers in operating model 1 act as non-asset intermediaries. Gross revenue moves with freight spot rates and fuel surcharges, reflecting pass-through transportation costs rather than internal company execution.

Standard GAAP operating margin computes operating income over gross revenue. This denominator distorts brokerage performance: a broker earning a flat dollar spread per load will show declining GAAP operating margins during rate spikes and expanding margins during market downturns.

Adjusted operating margin divides operating income by adjusted gross profit (net revenue, or gross revenue less purchased transportation). Net revenue represents the actual spread the broker captures. It is the correct denominator for measuring how efficiently brokerage overhead converts gross profit into operating earnings.

Where it was observed

At C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc., the company defines and discloses adjusted operating margin in its Form 10-K non-GAAP reconciliations.

In FY2025, C.H. Robinson reported operating income of $794,961 thousand against adjusted gross profit of $2,729,410 thousand, producing an adjusted operating margin of 29.1%.

On GAAP gross revenue ($16,211,102 thousand), reported operating margin was only 4.9%. Financial screeners evaluating the 4.9% figure miss the operator's operational leverage and overhead productivity.

How to spot it

Never evaluate truckload brokers on GAAP operating margins or gross revenue multiples.

Extract purchased transportation from cost of sales to derive net revenue. Calculate adjusted operating margin and compare it against SG&A as a percentage of net revenue. This separation isolates pricing discipline from back-office productivity, aligning with the brokerage value tree.
