Engine durability deficits elongate rotable MRO turnaround times

Premature durability shortfalls on modern geared turbofans extend off-wing MRO turnaround times from 90 to over 250 days, transforming rotable engine pools into multi-billion dollar capital traps.

Core takeaway

Premature durability shortfalls on modern geared turbofans extend off-wing MRO turnaround times from 90 to over 250 days, transforming rotable engine pools into multi-billion dollar capital traps.

Operating models affected

Passenger Network Legacy Carrier, Point-to-Point Low-Cost Carrier, ACMI & Heavy Air Charter Carrier

Where observed

the systems recordaviation-mro-and-engineering-platforms

Why it is true

Next-generation aircraft engines (Pratt & Whitney PW1100G-JM Geared Turbofan, CFM LEAP-1A/1B) deliver 15% fuel burn reductions by operating at unprecedented turbine temperatures and compression ratios.

These mechanical designs have suffered severe early-life durability defects. Powder-metal disk contamination in Pratt GTF engines and high-pressure turbine blade degradation in CFM LEAP powerplants have cut time-on-wing from 10,000+ cycles to under 3,000 cycles in demanding operating environments.

Global Part 145 engine overhaul facilities lack the specialized hot-section rotable components, titanium forgings, and certified technicians required to service premature shop-visit waves.

Consequently, engine shop visit Turnaround Times (TAT) have stretched from historical 75-to-90 day standards to between 250 and 320 days.

Airlines running lean spare engine reserves (historically 8% to 10% of installed fleet) run out of spare engines, forcing airframes into indefinite Aircraft-On-Ground (AOG) storage while lease payments and depreciation continue unabated.

What was seen

Documented in aviation MRO and engineering platforms and engine rotable durability deficit.

RTX Corporation (Pratt & Whitney's parent company) recorded over $3 billion in pre-tax charges in 2023–2024 to compensate airline customers for grounded aircraft and mandated accelerated fleet teardowns across 600 to 700 engines through 2026.

Ultra-low-cost carriers Spirit Airlines and Wizz Air were forced to ground between 15% and 25% of their active Airbus A320neo fleets simultaneously awaiting engine overhauls.

Secondary market spare engine lease rates jumped from $60,000 per month to over $150,000 per month, wiping out the operating fuel efficiency benefits of the new engine technology.

What it changes

When conducting airline due diligence, aircraft appraisals, or fleet procurement advisory: 1. Never evaluate next-generation aircraft economics based purely on airframe manufacturer fuel-burn guarantees. 2. Require airlines operating geared turbofans or high-compression engines to maintain spare engine ratios of 15% to 20%, rather than historical 8% to 10% baselines. 3. Structure long-term Power-by-the-Hour (PBH) maintenance contracts with strict, enforceable OEM daily cash liquidated damages for parked aircraft exceeding contracted shop-visit turnaround times.
