Visibility platforms fail at carrier data capture not software layer

Real-time supply chain visibility platforms stall because long-tail carrier telematics and batch EDI feeds fail to provide clean, timely data, not because tracking software or predictive algorithms are defective.

In 90 seconds

Core takeaway

Real-time supply chain visibility platforms stall because long-tail carrier telematics and batch EDI feeds fail to provide clean, timely data, not because tracking software or predictive algorithms are defective.

Operating models affected

Truckload Brokerage, Freight Forwarding / NVOCC, Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Where observed

05-solutions/failure history/passive-visibility-and-control-towers

Why it is true

Visibility platforms depend on continuous, high-fidelity location data from carrier electronic logging devices (ELDs) or driver mobile applications.

In fragmented freight markets, long-tail carriers actively resist continuous tracking. Drivers disable mobile GPS tracking to protect battery and personal privacy. Small motor carriers refuse to provide open ELD credentials, fearing brokers will aggregate their lane capacity data.

For ocean, rail, and LTL shipments, platforms rely on legacy EDI 214 messages that carriers transmit in batch windows 4 to 12 hours after physical events occur.

Ingesting stale, intermittent, or missing telemetry corrupts predictive ETA machine-learning models.

The software generates false-positive delay alerts and erratic arrival windows. Operations teams lose confidence in the dashboard and revert to manual phone calls.

What was seen

Documented in passive visibility control towers.

Gartner research and industry benchmarks show enterprise RTTVP tracking compliance routinely plateauing at 60% to 75% for full truckload and under 50% for LTL and intermodal.

Over 80% of automated alerts generated by visibility platforms proved un-actionable or false, producing widespread alert fatigue while detention fees and demurrage under detention and demurrage under-recovery continued to escalate.

What it changes

When evaluating a visibility or control-tower proposal, ignore software UI features, map visualization, and algorithmic ETA claims.

Evaluate the carrier data capture layer instead: 1. Demand verified, audit-backed tracking compliance percentages by mode (truckload, LTL, ocean) rather than vendor network connection counts. 2. Require contractual carrier compliance mechanisms before purchasing visibility software. 3. Treat visibility as a data ingestion challenge rather than a predictive analytics feature.
