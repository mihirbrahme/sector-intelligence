Equity income from an affiliate inside cost of sales distorts segment margin

Some companies book their share of a joint venture's result inside segment costs rather than below operating income. Segment operating margin then moves with the affiliate's earnings.

Operating models affected

Ports & Terminals, Protected liner shipping / Jones Act non-contiguous trades

Where observed

Claim. Some companies book their share of a joint venture's result inside segment costs rather than below operating income. Segment operating margin then moves with the affiliate's earnings. Strip it out before reading the segment, or a recovery at the affiliate reads as performance at the company.

Why. Equity-method income normally sits below the operating line. A filer may instead record it within costs and expenses where the affiliate's operations are integral to the segment — a terminal joint venture for a liner, say. The presentation is legitimate and disclosed. It also means one line of segment operating income is not operating.

What was seen. Matson, Inc. FY2025 Form 10-K, filed 2026-02-27, grade ``. Matson holds 35% of SSA Terminals and "records its share of income from SSAT in costs and expenses... within the Ocean Transportation segment."

FY2025 FY2024
Ocean segment operating income $455.6m $500.9m
SSAT share included within it +$32.5m −$1.0m
Ex-affiliate operating income $423.1m $501.9m

Headline decline −9.0%; ex-affiliate decline −15.7%. The FY2024 figure itself carried an $18.4m impairment share, so a joint venture recovering from a write-down absorbed 43% of the reported fall.

The same counterparty is simultaneously the segment's largest supplier. Terminal services cost $341.9m in 2025, against $320.9m and $297.2m in the two prior years — up 15% across a period in which container volume fell. One relationship flatters the income statement while inflating cost per unit of work.

What it changes. Search the segment note for equity-method income recorded within costs before computing any segment margin or margin trend. Where it is there, publish the ex-affiliate series alongside the headline. Where the affiliate is also a major supplier, compute its cost per unit of work separately. The two directions of the same relationship are usually discussed as one.
