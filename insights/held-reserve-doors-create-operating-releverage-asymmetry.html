Held reserve doors create operating releverage asymmetry

Maintaining surplus service-centre door capacity through an LTL downturn enables operating margins to expand significantly faster than sequential volume growth upon recovery.

In 90 seconds

Core takeaway

Maintaining surplus service-centre door capacity through an LTL downturn enables operating margins to expand significantly faster than sequential volume growth upon recovery.

Operating models affected

LTL

Where observed

old-dominion

The distinction

In fixed-network less-than-truckload carriage (operating model 5), overhead as a share of revenue rises during volume downturns over a fixed terminal network. Analysts often treat excess real estate as an inefficient burden.

When freight demand rebounds, an operator holding reserve door capacity experiences pronounced operational releverage.

Because cross-dock facilities, linehaul doors, and terminal infrastructure are in place, incremental tonnage flows across existing facilities with minimal marginal cost.

Overhead as a share of revenue falls far faster than it rose during the contraction.

Carrying excess terminal capacity is an economic option: the operator accepts higher unit costs during the trough to capture rapid margin expansion upon inflection without service failure.

Where it was observed

Old Dominion maintained ~30% reserve door capacity across 257 service centres through the freight recession. Across 2025-Q4 to 2026-Q1, operating ratio rose 80bp to 76.7%, then another 80bp to 76.2%.

In 2026-Q2, a 4.0% sequential tonnage increase drove a 450bp recovery in operating ratio to 70.1%. Direct expenses improved 230bp and overhead improved 220bp, while maintaining 99% on-time service and a 0.1% claims ratio.

How to spot it

Quantify reserve door capacity before asserting terminal inefficiency in LTL downturns.

Decompose operating ratio movements into terminal overhead versus linehaul labour. An operator holding network doors is positioned for rapid margin expansion, distinguishing intentional reserve capacity from structural distress.

Where this appears elsewhere

This insight governs operational analysis under LTL.
