NDC and ONE Order transition shifts airline distribution economics

The IATA New Distribution Capability (NDC) and ONE Order transition shifts airline distribution economics from GDS booking fees to direct merchandising margin, but fractures corporate agency servicing and interline settlement.

In 90 seconds

Core takeaway

The IATA New Distribution Capability (NDC) and ONE Order transition shifts airline distribution economics from GDS booking fees to direct merchandising margin, but fractures corporate agency servicing and interline settlement.

Operating models affected

Passenger Network Legacy Carrier, Point-to-Point Low-Cost Carrier

Where observed

the systems recordairline-core-operating-systems

Why it is true

Legacy airline distribution relies on Global Distribution Systems (Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport) transmitting 1960s EDIFACT teletype messages.

GDS platforms charge carriers $3.50 to $7.00+ per passenger segment while restricting pricing to 26 static alphabet fare buckets.

IATA NDC and ONE Order replace EDIFACT and the Passenger Name Record (PNR) with direct XML and JSON APIs.

Airlines regain control over dynamic Offer and Order creation. Carriers can bundle seats, bags, Wi-Fi, and priority boarding dynamically at checkout, bypassing GDS booking surcharges.

However, corporate Travel Management Companies (TMCs) depend on legacy GDS workflows. When airlines remove fare content from legacy channels, corporate travel desks cannot automate ticket exchanges, split passenger itineraries, or track corporate duty-of-care.

What was seen

Observed in airline core operating systems and the American Airlines Group Inc. commercial distribution conflict of 2023–2024.

American Airlines removed 40% of its lowest fare content from legacy GDS channels, attempting to force travel agencies onto direct NDC feeds.

Corporate travel agencies responded by shifting bookings to Delta and United. American's corporate revenue dropped sharply, leading to a 300 basis point underperformance in PRASM against network peers in early 2024.

American fired its Chief Commercial Officer in May 2024 and restored fare content to legacy GDS aggregators, demonstrating that distribution technology shifts stall when agency servicing workflows are ignored.

Furthermore, US DOT fee disclosure mandates (unbundled ancillary fee friction under regulatory disclosure rules) force airlines to expose all ancillary fee components inside NDC payloads, preventing hidden drip-pricing markups.

What it changes

When advising airlines or travel tech platforms on distribution strategy: 1. Treat NDC adoption as a commercial channel conflict rather than an IT API upgrade. 2. Model the net financial trade-off: verify whether GDS fee savings exceed potential corporate passenger yield defection. 3. Require travel agency servicing automation (refunds, schedule changes, and disruption re-accommodation) before restricting GDS fare distribution.
