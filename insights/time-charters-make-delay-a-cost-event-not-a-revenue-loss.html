Operating models affected

Where observed

kirby

Claim. In marine transport the contract form decides what a lost day of availability is worth. Under a time charter the vessel earns its day rate whether it moves or not, so delay costs fuel and crew but no revenue. Under a contract of affreightment the operator is paid per movement, so a delayed tow earns nothing. Any uptime or availability value pool must be sized against the affreightment book only.

Why. A time charter sells the asset-day; an affreightment contract sells the movement. They are two different units of work billed off the same fleet, and operators disclose the mix precisely because it changes their revenue volatility.

What was seen. Kirby Corporation FY2025 Form 10-K, filed 2026-02-17, grade ``. Inland: 70% term / 30% spot, with time charters 59% of inland term revenue (61% in 2024, 63% in 2023). Coastal: 100% term, time charters approximately 100% of it. Kirby states the purpose directly — time charters "help insulate the Company from revenue fluctuations caused by weather and navigational delays and temporary market declines."

Delay-exposed revenue is therefore inland revenue x (1 minus 0.70 x 0.59) = 58.7% of inland, and effectively nil on coastal. Inland was 80% of the $1,935.3m marine segment, so approximately 47% of segment revenue carries delay as a revenue loss.

What it changes. Never size a recovered-availability pool against total marine segment revenue — on this disclosure it overstates the base by roughly two. Read the term percentage and the time-charter percentage as two separate figures: "70% term" is not the insulation number, because a term contract of affreightment carries full delay exposure at a rate that cannot move. Ask which contracts still bill on a day the tow does not move.
