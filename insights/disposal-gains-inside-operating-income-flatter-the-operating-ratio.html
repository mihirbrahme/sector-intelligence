Disposal gains inside operating income flatter the operating ratio

Gains on sale of tractors, trailers and terminals are netted inside operating expenses, so they lower the operating ratio without any change in how the fleet runs. An asset carrier shrinking its fleet can report an improving operating ratio while the haulage operation loses more money.

Core takeaway

Gains on sale of tractors, trailers and terminals are netted inside operating expenses, so they lower the operating ratio without any change in how the fleet runs. An asset carrier shrinking its fleet can report an improving operating ratio while the haulage operation loses more money.

Operating models affected

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Where observed

heartland-express

Why the ratio is exposed

Operating ratio is operating expenses divided by operating revenue. Under US GAAP, a carrier's gain on disposal of revenue equipment is an operating item: it appears as a credit within operating expenses, not below the operating line. The denominator does not move. So every dollar of gain subtracts a dollar from the numerator and improves the ratio directly.

Three features of Model 4 make this more than a rounding issue:

  1. Tractors turn over on a three-to-five-year cycle, so disposal volume is large relative to operating income at any point in the cycle.
  2. Used-truck and industrial-land prices are set in markets unrelated to freight rates. The gain is not a return on carriage.
  3. Fleet downsizing raises disposal volume precisely when freight is weakest. The credit arrives in the worst quarters, which is when it most changes the reading.

The non-GAAP adjusted operating ratio does not fix this. The common definition strips fuel surcharge revenue and intangible amortisation. It leaves disposal gains in.

Where it was observed

At Heartland Express, Q2 2026 was reported as a return to profit: operating income of $16,549k against a $(12,419)k loss a year earlier, an operating ratio of 91.0% against 105.9%, and an adjusted operating ratio of 88.3%. Management described "stronger freight volumes and improved customer pricing."

The same release discloses a gain on disposal of property and equipment of $25,050k for the quarter, against $2,782k a year earlier, netted inside the $167,577k operating expense line. Adding it back:

Period Operating income (reported) Operating income (ex disposal gains) Reported OR Adjusted OR OR ex disposal gains
Q2 2026 $16,549k $(8,501)k 91.0% 88.3% 104.6% (104.8% adj.)
Q3 2026 $2,297k $(5,535)k 98.7% 97.6% 103.1% (102.9% adj.)
9M 2026 $15,551k $(24,648)k 97.1% 95.7% 104.6% (104.6% adj.)

Method: operating expenses as reported plus the disclosed gain, over operating revenue as reported — Q2 2026: ($167,577k + $25,050k) ÷ $184,126k = 104.6%; Q3 2026: ($177,207k + $7,832k) ÷ $179,504k = 103.1%; 9M 2026: ($524,336k + $40,199k) ÷ $539,887k = 104.6%. The adjusted column applies the same add-back to Heartland's own definition.

Across the first nine months of 2026 the gap is 750 basis points on GAAP OR and 890 basis points on adjusted OR, on $40,199k of disposal gains. And the pattern is not new. FY2025 carried $23.4m of equipment and real estate gains; three terminals went into sale-leasebacks in November 2023 for an expected $25m pre-tax gain, one of them at a $30.9m price confirmed in San Joaquin County property records. Guidance for the remainder of 2026 projects $9–13m of disposal gains against $2–5m of net capital expenditures.

A carrier whose stated goal is "an operating ratio of 85.0% or lower" is roughly 18 points away on the ex-gains measure in Q3 2026 and 14 points away on the reported one.

How to spot it

  • Read the gain on disposal of property and equipment line before reading the operating ratio. It is usually a parenthesised credit inside the operating expense table or a reconciling item on the cash flow statement.
  • Check the non-GAAP definition in the release's own words. If it names only fuel surcharge and amortisation, disposal gains are still inside the number.
  • Compare the gain to proceeds from equipment sales and to net capital expenditure. A carrier where proceeds exceed capex is shrinking the fleet, and the gain is a liquidation credit, not an operating result.
  • Where guidance forecasts disposal gains for future periods, the company is telling you the credit is planned. Treat forward operating-ratio targets built on it accordingly.
  • The same add-back belongs in any sub-sector 4A operating ratio comparison. A peer median that mixes ex-gains and inclusive ratios is not a distribution of anything.
