UK statutory headcount disclosures isolate extraction defects

Missing employee headcount figures for UK medium and large entities indicate extraction defects, because disclosure is mandatory under Companies Act 2006 s411.

The distinction

In financial intelligence workflows, automated parsers often report average employee numbers as missing across UK private company datasets. Analysts conclude targets operate under disclosure exemptions or withhold staffing numbers.

This misinterprets UK statutory accounting law.

Under the Companies Act 2006, Section 411 mandates that every company not qualifying as a micro-entity must disclose in the financial statement notes the average number of persons employed during the year.

For all medium, large, and group filers (MEDIUM, FULL, and GROUP), employee numbers are legally present in filed accounts.

When a database reports null headcounts for these companies, it is an extraction failure, never a corporate disclosure choice.

Where it was observed

The dataset initially contained headcount numbers for only three companies. The automated text scraper truncated the note at the boilerplate preamble ("The average number of employees during the year was as follows:"), discarding the numerical table across nine companies.

All nine complied with statutory disclosure; the intelligence pipeline failed to extract the numbers.

How to spot it

When evaluating UK filers across all operating models, check filing type (MEDIUM or FULL).

If headcount is missing from automated feeds, inspect raw statutory accounts directly. Do not abandon productivity metrics in the metric dictionary. A missing figure is a cue to repair the parser, not to treat the metric as unobservable.
