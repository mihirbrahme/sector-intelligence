Core takeaway

Public charges registers provide dated, independent evidence of forwarder invoice discounting and factoring facilities, enabling external verification of management working-capital claims.

Where observed

The distinction

In freight forwarding (operating model 2), working capital extensions force operators to finance receivables. Shippers pay in 45 to 60 days, while carriers and customs authorities require payment in 15 to 30 days.

When debtor days extend, operators frequently establish off-balance-sheet invoice discounting or factoring lines. In filed accounts, management frequently asserts that the business operates without factoring facilities.

Management claims regarding financing facilities are self-reported commentary.

However, under UK corporate law, any security granted over company assets — specifically debentures or floating charges over book debts and receivables — must be registered within 21 days with Companies House.

Where it was observed

Identified in mechanism workings uk forwarders §Part 6.

At Hillebrand Bulk Logistics Limited, management stated in its annual strategic report: "The Company has no investing, factoring or reverse factoring arrangements in place."

The UK Companies House charges register offers an immediate, external dataset to verify that assertion and test whether undisclosed financing facilities exist.

How to spot it

Pull the public charges register before accepting management statements on working capital liquidity.

Cross-reference registered charges against debtor days and interest expense notes. If charges over book debts appear alongside rising debtor days, the company is actively financing working capital extensions, corroborating receivable distress in billing exception leakage billing exception leakage.
