SIC codes fail to distinguish transport operating models

Core takeaway

Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes bundle fundamentally different transport operating models under identical numbers, making code-derived peer sets invalid.

Operating models affected

Truckload Brokerage, Freight Forwarding / NVOCC, Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

The trap

Industry classification taxonomies (US SIC, UK SIC 2007, and EU NACE) group companies by broad activity lines rather than financial operating structure.

In transport and logistics, this creates peer-group collisions: - Asset-heavy carriers with heavy capex and fleet debt share codes with asset-light brokers and non-asset forwarders. - US SIC 4412 (deep sea foreign transportation of freight) contains non-asset freight forwarders alongside capital-intensive ocean tanker owners. - US SIC 4512 contains passenger airlines alongside all-cargo air operators. - UK SIC 52290 (transportation support) contains non-asset forwarders alongside port operators and relocation contractors.

Screeners grouping companies by SIC code evaluate asset carriers and brokers in single cohorts, directly violating peer discipline.

Where it was observed

How to spot it

Use SIC codes solely as discovery filters, never as analytical classifications.

Before running financial comparisons or calculating ratios from the metric dictionary, manually verify each target's operating model: 1. Identify the unit of work (load, shipment file, or asset-hour). 2. Check balance-sheet tangible fixed assets (PP&E) to separate asset fleets from non-asset intermediaries. 3. Establish whether cost of sales represents purchased capacity or direct equipment operating expenses.
