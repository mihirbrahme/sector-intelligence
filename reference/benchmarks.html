Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Operating Model Definition: Model 4. Asset-heavy motor carriers running company tractors, trailers, and driver pools. Primary operational measure is Operating Ratio (OR) (Operating Expenses ÷ Operating Revenue).

Critical Segmentation Rule: Irregular-Route Over-the-Road (One-Way OTR) and Dedicated Contract Carriage operate on fundamentally divergent commercial models. Blending them into a single median produces an unusable metric. They are benchmarked separately below.

Sub-sector 4A: Irregular-Route Over-the-Road (One-Way OTR)

Operates on spot and annual routing guide contracts across an open network. Network density, empty miles %, and spot market rates dictate margins. - Unit of work: the loaded mile / linehaul trip - Primary metric: Operating Ratio (OR), revenue per loaded mile, empty miles % - Peer set: Heartland Express (pure-play OTR), Marten Transport (Truckload excl. Dedicated), Werner Enterprises (One-Way Truckload), Schneider National (Network Truckload), Knight-Swift (One-Way OTR), PAM Transportation.

Peer Evidence: Marten Truckload (excl. Dedicated) OR was 99.3% in FY2024 and 99.8% in FY2025 ($825k operating income on $421.7m revenue); Heartland Express OR deteriorated to 105.1% in FY2024 and 110.7% in FY2025.

Two cautions on the Heartland Express figures, both dated 2026-10-07.

  1. The 105.1% and 110.7% do not reconcile to the filings. Heartland's reported GAAP operating ratios are 101.9% (FY2024) and 107.1% (FY2025); its reported non-GAAP adjusted ratios are 101.7% and 104.7%. An ex-fuel-surcharge recomputation from the disclosed lines gives 102.2% and 108.1%, which does not match either.
  2. Heartland cannot populate the per-mile or turnover rows of this table at all. It discloses no loaded miles, no empty-mile percentage, no revenue per loaded mile, no tractor count and no driver turnover figure in any period. The empty-mile and turnover ranges above are distributions across the subset of named peers that disclose, and that subset is selected by disclosure behaviour. See a carrier that discloses no operating statistics cannot carry per-mile benchmarks.

And one methodological caution on the OR row itself. Gains on disposal of tractors, trailers and terminals are netted inside operating expenses, so they lower a reported OR with no change in fleet execution. Heartland's H1 2026 OR of 96.3% becomes 105.3% once $32.4m of disposal gains are added back; Q2 2026 moves from 91.0% to 104.6%. A median that mixes inclusive and ex-gains ratios across peers is not a distribution of anything. See disposal gains inside operating income flatter the operating ratio.
