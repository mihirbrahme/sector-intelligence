CMA CGM is buying contract-logistics scale through CEVA, not a forwarding-volume story

CMA CGM's purchase of FedEx Supply Chain is a liner company buying warehousing capacity through its forwarding subsidiary. The "preferred ocean carrier" commercial agreement attached to the deal is the smaller, softer part of it, and should not be read as the point. CMA CGM Group completed the $1.4 billion enterprise-value acquisition of FedEx Supply Chain on 2026-10-01, closing a deal first announced 2026-07-01. The deal roughly triples CEVA Logistics' North American contract-logistics footprint to about 150 warehouses, 34 million square feet and 20,000 employees across 240-plus locations, placing CEVA among the top five US warehouse operators by the parties' own characterisation (same sources).

International container liner and feeder shipping

October's transpacific rate spike is capacity withdrawal and a Golden Week rush, not a demand rebound

The early-October jump in transpacific spot rates reads as carriers cutting sailings into their lowest deployed-capacity month since April, layered on shippers front-loading ahead of China's Golden Week. It does not read as a renewed US import-demand signal. Average Far East-to-US-West-Coast and Far East-to-US-East-Coast spot rates rose 38% and 23% to $2,138 and $3,038 per FEU as of 2026-10-01. Deployed capacity into the US West Coast fell to its lowest level since April 2026, when tariff-driven demand loss last cut capacity this deep; carriers blanked roughly 13% of planned October Asia-US and Asia-Europe sailings, below the nearly 18% blanked during Golden Week a year earlier (same sources, restatement of carrier scheduling data).

LTL

Old Dominion is pricing through a volume downturn, not off a recovery

Old Dominion's 4.9% general rate increase, taking effect a month earlier than its prior cycle while its own shipment count keeps falling, is pricing discipline continuing into a soft market. It is not evidence that volume has recovered enough to justify the increase. Old Dominion announced the 4.9% increase to its 559, 670 and 550 class tariffs on 2026-09-21, effective 2026-10-05, citing higher real estate, equipment, technology and employee wage and benefit costs. Saia and ArcBest have already taken increases this cycle on earlier timelines than prior years, and FedEx Freight announced a 5.9% increase for 2027, its fourth consecutive year at that level.
