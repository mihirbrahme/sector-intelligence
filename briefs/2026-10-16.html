Domestic Intermodal

J.B. Hunt's Q3 shows intermodal doing almost all the earnings work, and the growth inside intermodal is concentrated on the rail network most exposed to the UP–NS merger outcome

J.B. Hunt's Q3 2026 print is being read in trade coverage as a clean earnings beat. The composition says something sharper: intermodal supplied nearly all of the disclosed operating-income growth, and within intermodal the growth concentrated on the half of the network whose rail partner is the subject of a pending merger application. J.B. Hunt's Q3 2026 results, released 2026-10-15 (SEC Form 8-K and press release, restated via WebSearch; for the figures, for attribution — sec.gov and investor.jbhunt.com were both unreachable this session). Intermodal segment revenue reached $1.83 billion, up 20% year over year; segment operating income was $147.5 million, up 18%.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

September's freight-volume growth is a fuel-surcharge story, not carriers regaining pricing power

Cass's second consecutive month of year-over-year shipment growth is being read as the freight recession ending. Set the expenditure side of the same index beside it, and September reads as priced growth, not demanded growth: the dollars are coming from fuel, not from carriers regaining the ability to charge more for a move. Cass Freight Index, September 2026, published 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-14 (Cass Information Systems, restated via FreightWaves, Logistics Management and The Trucker, retrieved via WebSearch; cassinfo.com returned a 403 on the proxy CONNECT tunnel, untested directly — for the underlying series by construction, for this session's restatement). Shipments rose 1.9% year over year — the second consecutive annual gain after 42 months of decline — and 2.3% month over month.
