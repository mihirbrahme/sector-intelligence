International container liner and feeder shipping

USTR's Section 301 fee suspension on Chinese-linked vessels lapses November 9, five days out, with an extension proposed but not yet confirmed

The nearer-term cost risk in ocean container shipping right now is a dated regulatory deadline, not the US-China tariff truce the trade press is covering. My view: USTR most likely extends the suspension, given it has already proposed doing so, but a lapse without a published final notice would add real cost within days, and current spot pricing shows no sign of expecting that outcome. USTR's original Section 301 action against China's shipbuilding and maritime-logistics practices set a phased per-net-ton service fee on Chinese-owned or Chinese-operated vessels calling US ports, rising from $50/net ton (phase II, from 2025-10-14) toward $140/net ton by 2028, with a separate, lower schedule for Chinese-built vessels under non-Chinese operators. That fee has been under suspension since late 2025 as part of the broader US-China negotiation track, and the current suspension period runs through 11:59pm EST on 2026-11-09.

Domestic Intermodal

Schneider made Norfolk Southern its sole primary eastern rail provider days before this earnings release, a concentration bet rather than a hedge against the pending NS–Union Pacific merger

Model 7's standing risk, per the benchmark register's Class I railroad count row, is that purchased linehaul has no competitive substitute on most lanes because six Class I railroads operate as regional duopolies. Schneider's answer to that risk, announced 2026-10-29, was to concentrate further rather than hedge: Norfolk Southern becomes its primary eastern rail provider, ending the relationship it had used with CSX. Schneider's CEO called a potential NS–Union Pacific merger "the icing on the cake" of the new partnership — on the record while that merger sits in Surface Transportation Board review, with UP's revised application accepted as complete 2026-05-28 and completion expected in 2027 (per benchmark register). That is a domestic intermodal operator stating a preference for a more concentrated eastern-western Class I network over the optionality of two separately negotiated railroads, the opposite of the hedge a captive-shipper framing would predict.
