LTL

Old Dominion and Saia show the same Q3 volume recovery, but Saia's newer terminals are the reason it isn't converting to margin

I read the Q3 2026 LTL season as confirmation that network maturity, not demand, is now the discriminator inside this model. Both carriers got the volume back. Only one got the margin. Old Dominion's adjusted operating ratio improved 280 basis points to 71.5% in Q3 2026, on revenue up 13.1% to $1.59bn and net income up 26.9% to $343.7m.

International container liner and feeder shipping

Maersk's record Q3 was a congestion story, and the correction already started one week after its quarter closed

My view is that the elevated container rates behind Maersk's Q3 came from temporary capacity absorption, not from demand, and that the unwind is already visible in the index data one week later. Maersk's Q3 2026 revenue rose 27% to $18.1bn and EBITDA reached $4.7bn, below the roughly $5.0bn analyst consensus; underlying EBIT of $3.3bn also missed. Spot rates rose about 46% quarter on quarter, but ocean volumes were roughly flat and the main shipping business's volume fell about 1% — the gain was priced, not shipped. Maersk itself named Asian typhoon disruption and the Strait of Hormuz closure as the drivers, and raised full-year EBITDA guidance to $14.0–14.5bn on that back.

Truckload Brokerage

Hub Group's harder deadline is November 30, not the Nasdaq panel, and the board now responsible for it has just been gutted

This is a weaker claim than the first two, offered because it names a date nobody else in this record's coverage has flagged. The market's attention has been on Hub Group's Nasdaq delisting appeal. The company's own lenders set a harder, earlier deadline, and nothing on the public record says whether it will be met. A Third Amendment to Hub Group's credit agreement, signed 2026-09-11, moved the delivery date for audited FY2025 and unaudited Q1–Q3 2026 financial statements to 2026-11-30, with an EBITDA add-back for restatement costs through 2026-12-31.
