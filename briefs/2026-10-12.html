Inland liquid-bulk marine / tank barge

Hurricane Isaias splits the Gulf tank-barge book into a revenue loss and a cost event, and Port Condition Yankee at Mobile decides which

General coverage is reading Hurricane Isaias as a port-closure and refinery story. For the model this record already tracks in Kirby Corporation, the operative question is not whether Gulf operations stopped — Port Condition Yankee guarantees that — but which half of the book was earning while they did. Argus Media and S&P Global Commodity Insights forecast landfall the night of 2026-10-09 into 2026-10-10 at the Florida-Alabama border, Category 2 with winds to 110 mph; a Claims Journal restatement of the same window put it at Category 3, 120 mph. Roughly 63% of Gulf of Mexico crude output, about 1.3 million barrels a day, was shut in as of 2026-10-08, diesel exports fell 700,000 b/d week over week, and the US Coast Guard set Port Condition Yankee for the Mobile, Alabama area, restricting vessel movement (same sourcing).

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

FMCSA's own rule text exempts the Mexico border zone from English-proficiency enforcement, which splits a thread this record has tracked as one mechanism into two

Four consecutive briefs, 2026-10-06 through 2026-10-09, have treated the FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse, the non-domiciled CDL rule, the English-proficiency out-of-service rule, and the Mexico B-1 visa revocations as one emerging capacity-removal mechanism. FMCSA's own proposed rule text keeps the last two pieces structurally apart. FMCSA's NPRM (docket FMCSA-2026-0826, Federal Register 51422, published 2026-08-10; comment period closed 2026-10-09) and DOT's April 2026 enforcement memo both state the English-proficiency out-of-service penalty does not apply to a driver operating solely within a defined US-Mexico border commercial zone. It applies only once that driver intends to operate, or has operated, beyond the zone.
