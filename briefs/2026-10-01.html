Truckload Brokerage

A $22,000 carrier purchase, not stolen credentials, is how a cargo-theft ring cleared broker vetting

A Los Angeles federal jury convicted Arshpreet Singh and Vikramjeet Singh on 2026-09-29 of running a cargo-theft scheme built on purchased carrier authority, not forged documents. Singh bought the established, properly licensed Z&F Transportation LLC for approximately $22,000 in March 2024. He then used it and other acquired or misused carriers to win real shipping contracts across Southern California and Texas, collect the freight, and never deliver it. Prosecutors tied the ring to at least $2 million in losses; sentencing is set for 2027-01-20.

XPO and ArcBest's August data break the two-carrier LTL story this record told four days ago

XPO's and ArcBest's own August operating data, filed within days of the other two, add two more distinct patterns, putting four carriers in four different quadrants rather than two camps. Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

Washington's trade truce is a political promise; the port-fee suspension under it is not yet law

Trade press this week read the US-China truce extension as removing the near-term risk of Section 301 port fees on China-linked vessels. The legal instrument that actually suspends those fees has not been re-filed, and the gap between the announcement and the rule is the operational fact a forwarder should be pricing against. USTR suspended Section 301 port fees on China-built and China-owned containerships for one year from 2025-11-10, following the original Trump-Xi Busan agreement; the suspension is due to expire 2026-11-09. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in the past week that the truce "is now going to be extended until 10 January."

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

Air cargo's first yield rise since April is carriers cutting capacity, not shippers returning

IATA's August data shows global air cargo yields rising month-on-month for the first time since April, which some coverage read as peak-season demand strength arriving early. The capacity side of the same release argues the move is carriers holding supply back, not shippers stepping forward. Global cargo tonne-kilometres rose 4.4% year-on-year in August, while available cargo tonne-kilometres fell 0.1%, as capacity cuts among North American and European carriers outweighed additions elsewhere. Load factor rose 2.0 points to 46.0% over the same month.
