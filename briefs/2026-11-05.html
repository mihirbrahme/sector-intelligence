LTL

ArcBest's Q3 EPS miss is a brokerage story wearing an LTL headline, and at least one outlet's framing blends two operating models the record already knows not to blend at this company

Its Q3 2026 release gives a case study in why that distinction has to hold at the reporting line, not just in the index entry. ArcBest's own Form 8-K EX-99.1, filed 2026-11-04 and retrieved the same day via WebSearch-mediated restatement of the filing, reports adjusted EBITDA of $15.6 million against $3.1 million a year earlier, asset-based tonnage per day up 0.6% and weight per shipment up 0.6% with shipments per day flat, and renewed contracts and deferred pricing agreements averaging a 5.1% increase. The company attributes the gain to disciplined pricing, productivity initiatives and organisational cost savings — the same price-led, volume-flat signature 2026-10-29 found at Old Dominion, not XPO's volume-and-price combination or Saia's volume-up-margin-down pattern 2026-11-02 already separated.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Knight-Swift's 700-basis-point truckload gain extends Werner's capacity-discipline signature to a second, larger carrier — but the One-Way/Dedicated split that confirmed it at Werner is still missing here

2026-11-03 confirmed, from Werner's own earnings call, that its Truckload margin gain was genuine One-Way capacity discipline rather than an undisclosed Dedicated mix shift. Knight-Swift's Q3 2026 shows the same shape of improvement at a much larger carrier, but this session could not locate the equivalent discriminator for Knight-Swift specifically. Knight-Swift's average truckload tractors fell 4.0% year over year to 20,470 from 21,319, while average revenue per tractor rose 10.9% to $56,419. The Truckload segment's adjusted operating ratio improved 700 basis points year over year to 89.2%, empty-mile percentage fell 170 basis points, and loaded miles per tractor rose year over year for an eighth consecutive quarter.
