Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSO

The US Navy is now disabling commercial ships in the Gulf under its own Iran blockade, a second active risk channel beside Houthi and Iranian attack

Every entry this record holds on the Hormuz war treats Iran and Houthi-aligned attackers as the threat to shipping. A second, separate risk has been live since mid-July and is escalating on its own track: the US Navy's blockade of Iran, which now disables commercial ships that do not comply, whatever flag they fly. CENTCOM says a US fighter jet struck the Panama-flagged bulker Ocean Molica in the Gulf of Oman on 2026-10-10, disabling its propulsion after the crew allegedly ignored repeated warnings not to call at an Iranian port. It is the fourth commercial vessel CENTCOM says it has disabled since enforcement resumed on 2026-07-14.

Truckload Brokerage

Reefer capacity is tightening on a fall-harvest schedule that national truckload indices do not show

2026-10-20 already logged a national truckload tender rejection index at 14.17%, one blended figure across every equipment type. Underneath it, refrigerated capacity in the Pacific Northwest has tightened far faster than dry van, on a schedule that tracks the fall produce harvest rather than the broader freight-cycle thread this record has tracked since 2026-10-16. Pacific Northwest reefer tender rejections averaged 41% through the first three weeks of October 2026, against 17% in July 2026 and 16% in the same month a year earlier (FreightWaves/SONAR, restated via WebSearch; specific publication date not located this session, though the figures themselves fix July 2026, October 2026 and October 2025 as the reference periods — for SONAR's own data by construction, for this session's restatement). Van tender rejections in the same region held under 5% over the same window.

International container liner and feeder shipping

The Suez-capacity-return forecast from two days ago is already wrong on the one lane it was supposed to hit first

2026-10-19 read returning Suez transit capacity, alongside an already-softening demand signal, as pointing toward falling container spot rates through the fourth quarter. The Asia-Europe lane — the one most directly fed by that returning Suez capacity — just posted its first weekly rate rise in roughly three months instead. Drewry's World Container Index sub-lane data shows the Shanghai-Rotterdam spot rate up 6% to $3,522 per 40ft container and Shanghai-Genoa up 7% to $3,945, both in the week covering roughly 2026-10-15. Loadstar's own coverage frames this as the first Asia-Europe rise after thirteen consecutive weekly declines, and ties it to carriers testing new freight-all-kinds rates of $4,500 to $4,800 per FEU from 2026-10-19 — Drewry itself calls successful implementation uncertain (same sourcing and grading).
