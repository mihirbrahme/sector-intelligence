LTL

LTL carriers are pricing ahead of the cycle, not catching up to it

Three national LTL carriers have moved their annual general rate increase earlier than the year before, in succession, through 2026. That is a pricing-power signal, not a carriers-playing-catch-up signal. ArcBest filed a 5.9% GRI on 2026-06-22, roughly six weeks ahead of its usual annual cadence. Saia filed 7.1% on 2026-07-06, 120 basis points higher and three months earlier than its prior-year increase.

International container liner and feeder shipping

The Suez capacity story is starting to fail its own falsifier

this record's 2026-09-22 brief argued Suez capacity was returning on the empty backhaul leg faster than the paying headhaul leg, and that the gap was propping up rates. It named its own falsifier: a sharp WCI fall against an unchanged headhaul share. That is now the closer read of the data. Drewry's World Container Index fell 1% to $4,468 per 40ft container as of 2026-09-24, its first weekly fall after three consecutive rises logged in this record ($4,465 on 09-03, $4,476 on 09-10, $4,500 on 09-17).

Truckload Brokerage

A court, not a data print, may settle the truckload capacity debate first

this record's 09-18 through 09-24 briefs have argued over whether truckload capacity is genuinely tight, reading spot rates against driver-pool removal. A live court case, not the next DAT or OTRI print, may be what actually moves that removal. The D.C. Circuit heard oral argument on the non-domiciled CDL final rule on 2026-09-15, a challenge affecting roughly 200,000 non-domiciled CDL holders, and heard a related case on 2026-09-11 over FMCSA withholding some $160 million from California for not cancelling over 17,000 non-domiciled CDLs. this record's own benchmark register already carries this rule as a live-litigation row with a one-quarter shelf life, unresolved since the rule took effect 2026-03-16.
