Domestic Intermodal

BNSF's motion to deny the UP-NS merger attacks the application's sufficiency, not its terms, and that narrows what a settlement could fix

The opposition to Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern has moved from asking for conditions to asking the STB to dismiss the case before it reaches merits review. That is a different and larger risk to the deal than anything the 2026-09-11 brief recorded. BNSF filed a motion with the STB on 2026-09-11 arguing the amended application fails to state a prima facie case, because its Committed Gateway Pricing program does not create genuine rail-to-rail competition — the specific defect the STB itself flagged in its May 2026 review. Shipper trade groups including the American Chemistry Council, the National Industrial Transportation League and the Fertilizer Institute joined the same request (Railway Age and Progressive Railroading,; for the filing's content, quoted consistently across both outlets and confirmed by the American Chemistry Council's own release; for the outlets' framing of what it means for the deal).

LTL

Cass's steepest LTL shipment decline since 2023 sits next to public LTL carriers reporting acceleration, and the discrepancy is the story

Cass's August reading describes where invoiced freight sat across many shippers' networks. It is not the same claim Old Dominion and Saia are making about their own networks this same month, and the gap between the two is worth more than either number alone. The Cass Freight Index shipments component fell 9.3% year-on-year in August 2026, its steepest year-on-year drop since October 2023, and 1.5% month-on-month; trade coverage of the report attributes the decline to LTL specifically, with truckload and intermodal volumes rising over the same month (FreightWaves,; — trade-press account of Cass Information Systems' own report, which could not be reached directly, egress blocked to cassinfo.com). In the same month, Old Dominion's own operating update showed yield growth accelerating with volumes nearing positive territory, and Saia reported accelerating year-on-year tonnage growth (Trucking Dive and FleetOwner,; — trade-press restatement of each carrier's own unaudited monthly metrics release, not independently verified).

Ports & Terminals

A terminal appointment-system outage, not a gate lockout, is the untested edge of the FMC's demurrage billing rule

The FMC's invoice-content rule was built around bad paperwork and blocked gates. It says nothing about what happens when the scheduling system itself fails, and this month gave a live test case. An eModal appointment-scheduling outage affecting the Port of Oakland and TraPac's Los Angeles-area terminal ran from roughly Tuesday 2026-09-08 into midday Wednesday 2026-09-09, compounded by the Labor Day holiday. Drayage carriers unable to book pickups during the outage face the same after-the-fact dispute process as any other detention charge (Overdrive,; — single-outlet trade reporting, not corroborated against a second source in this session).
