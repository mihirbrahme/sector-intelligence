LTL

Old Dominion's Q3 margin recovery runs on price, not the volume-driven releverage the LTL model predicts

Tonnage barely moved while yield did the work. Old Dominion reported Q3 2026 revenue up 13.1% year over year and operating ratio improving 280 basis points to 71.5%, from 74.3% a year earlier. LTL tons per day fell 0.1% year over year while revenue per hundredweight rose 13.3% including fuel and 5.1% ex-fuel, beating the company's own Q2 guidance of 4.0% to 4.5%. The benchmark register already places Old Dominion 1,681 basis points ahead of the FY2025 Model 5 median operating ratio; this quarter widens that gap on price alone.

Truckload Brokerage

Landstar's doubling claims line is the sharpest fresh evidence yet for the mechanism behind its own positive economic spread

Landstar's Q3 2026 results show adverse claims development more than doubling year over year, at the one brokerage this record's benchmark register already flags as generating positive economic value. That is direct evidence for mechanism carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity, not an unrelated bad quarter. Landstar reported Q3 2026 revenue of $1.512 billion, up 25% year over year, and truck revenue of $1.407 billion, up 29%, with earnings per share of $1.42 held back by what the company called highly elevated insurance and claims costs. Unfavorable prior-year claims development rose to $21.924 million, from $9.219 million in Q3 2025, which the company attributed to commercial trucking and brokerage claims and cargo losses linked to fraud and theft.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Schneider's Q3 beat does not yet establish the capacity-discipline signature it is being read as confirming

2026-10-27 asked whether a second large carrier would show the same fewer-trucks, higher-yield signature as Knight-Swift. Schneider's headline Q3 beat looks like confirmation, but the segment detail needed to test that specific mechanism was not available this session, and should not be assumed from the consolidated numbers. Schneider reported Q3 2026 adjusted earnings per share of $0.83, against $0.32 a year earlier, revenue up roughly 14.2% year over year to approximately $2.2 billion, and adjusted operating ratio improving by roughly 450 basis points to 89.3%. A Truckload-segment revenue-per-load figure located this session matched a figure already in the record for Knight-Swift's Intermodal segment almost exactly — more likely search-result cross-contamination between two companies than a genuine coincidence, so it is not used here.
