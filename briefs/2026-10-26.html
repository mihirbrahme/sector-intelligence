Domestic Intermodal

Knight-Swift's actual Q3 intermodal numbers confirm a variable-cost signature, not the fixed-cost mechanism this record credited it to four days ago

2026-10-22 read Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc.'s 200-basis-point intermodal margin gain against uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost and found the arithmetic did not fit: a rail-linehaul cost base that is contractually fixed should not move with volume the way the numbers implied. That reading was built on a back-computed estimate from a single restatement. Direct verification of the Q3 2026 Form 8-K (filed 2026-10-21) now gives the actual disclosed figures. Knight-Swift's Intermodal operating ratio improved 200 basis points year over year from 99.8% Adjusted OR (102.4% GAAP OR) to 97.8% ($2.8m operating income), on segment revenue up 30.6% to $122.9m, load count up 13.3% to 40,090, and revenue per load up 15.2% to $3,065.

Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSO

Hormuz tanker attacks have cooled from their October 7 peak while the US Navy's own blockade enforcement has not — Model 12's two risk channels are starting to diverge

2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22 separated two risk channels for Model 12: direct Houthi or Iranian-aligned attack on tankers, and the US Navy's own blockade enforcement against ships trading with Iran. Both were read as escalating together. The most recent reporting on the attack side shows it cooling while the enforcement side shows no matching change. Maritime security sources recorded at least 12 attacks on oil, LNG and LPG tankers around the Strait of Hormuz between 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-05, the highest weekly count since the US-Iran war began on 2026-02-28.

International container liner and feeder shipping

The Suez return is now visible service by service, and it is landing on the schedule this record forecast, not ahead of or behind it

2026-10-23 read BIMCO's aggregate Suez-transit data and noted Linerlytica expected ONE and HMM to join Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd's return next. Two named services now confirm that forecast at the individual-carrier level, which is confirmation rather than fresh acceleration. Premier Alliance (ONE, HMM, Yang Ming) is switching its FE1 service back to Suez, with the first vessel — the 8,110 TEU ONE Continuity — departing Laem Chabang in the week of 2026-10-17, its first move off the Cape of Good Hope since the January 2024 reroute. Separately, Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd's AE12 service (Hapag's SE1) makes its own first Suez-routed sailing on 2026-10-27, departing Tanjung Pelepas aboard the Umm Qarn.
