Truckload Brokerage

Hub Group's board didn't just lose its audit chair — six of roughly ten directors are gone, and the replacement slate includes a former CFO

2026-11-09 read the 2026-10-01 written-consent action as removing the audit committee chair and lead director. The turnover is wider than that framing captured, and it is a weaker signal for restatement independence than a single removal would be. Controlling shareholders acted by written consent effective 2026-10-01, removing Michael Flannery, lead director Peter McNitt and audit chair Gary Yablon without cause. They appointed Gregory Bunch, Thomas Fitzgerald, Thaddeus Malik and Thomas White, a former Hub Group CFO.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

DAT's October print reads as a capacity-exodus story, but FreightWaves' own carrier-exit data this year does not support an exodus

I read the current spot-rate spike as a seasonal demand story, not the capacity-scarcity story 2026-11-10's Knight-Swift judgment needs for its 2027 bid thesis. The rate move itself is real; the stated cause is not yet earned. DAT's October 2026 Truckload Volume Index put the national dry van spot rate, all-in, at $3.05 per mile — up 4 cents from September, 98 cents year over year, and the highest level since early 2022. DAT's weekly Dry Van Report, for the week ending 2026-11-06, put linehaul spot, ex-fuel, at $2.26 per mile, up 2.8% week over week, under the headline "Tight capacity keeps spot rates climbing".

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

Indonesia's new logistics-cost target is a four-month-old regulation still waiting on its own backers to ask for implementation

A government target this large, with no implementation push from the sector's own research body four months after signing, reads as aspiration ahead of execution. The target itself is credible; the pace behind it is not yet visible. Presidential Regulation 41/2026, signed 2026-07-16, replaces the 2012 National Logistics System blueprint. It sets a 2026–2029 National Logistics Strengthening Action Plan, targeting logistics cost down from roughly 23–24% of GDP to 12.5% by 2029 and 8% by 2045.
