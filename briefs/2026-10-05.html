Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Trucking's first positive year-on-year month in 42 months is carriers leaving, not shippers returning

The headline reading on the Cass Freight Index looks like the freight recession ending. The more likely story is that enough capacity left the market for the same or weaker demand to register as growth. Cass Freight Index — Shipments rose to 1.038 in August 2026, up 2.1% year-on-year and 5.6% month-on-month, the first year-on-year gain since January 2023 and the end of a 42-month downturn. Expenditures rose 18.7% year-on-year to 3.722 over the same month.

International container liner and feeder shipping

Container schedule reliability has collapsed to a four-year low, and congestion — not blanked sailings — is the bigger capacity story

2026-10-02 read the early-October transpacific rate spike as carriers blanking sailings into a low-capacity month plus a Golden Week rush. A second, larger supply constraint sits underneath that one: vessels stuck in congestion rather than deliberately held back. Global schedule reliability fell to 49.9% in August 2026, the lowest since September 2022, with average delay on late arrivals at 6.81 days, the worst since March 2022. this record's benchmark register still carries Sea-Intelligence's February reading of 59.0%, eight points higher and seven months stale. Separately, roughly 12% of global container ship capacity is tied up in port congestion, the highest level in four years, concentrated in East Asia, Northern Europe and the Indian subcontinent (C.H. Robinson's October 2026 Edge Report, retrieved via search summary 2026-10-05; by construction — a commercial provider's own market note, chrobinson.com untested).

Domestic Intermodal

Intermodal's volume surge is running into a shrinking drayage driver pool, and that constraint arrived before the rail capacity one

Domestic intermodal is posting its strongest volumes in years at the same time as its drayage labour pool keeps shrinking. The growth looks likely to hit the truck-side constraint before it hits a rail-side one. The seven-day moving average of loaded domestic intermodal containers reached an annual high of 21,697 on 2026-09-28, up roughly 8% year-on-year; AAR's weekly data put US intermodal volume up 6.3% and total North American rail traffic up 3.5% in week 38. Against that, the pool of drivers registered to haul 53-foot domestic containers fell from 531,000 at its 2022 peak to 467,000 as of July 2026, a decline of roughly 64,000.
