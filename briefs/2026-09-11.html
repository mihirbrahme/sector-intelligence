Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Diesel's run toward $6 a gallon is a supply shock, and it will show up as escalator lag before it shows up as demand

Diesel's spike toward $6 a gallon is a supply shock, not a demand signal. The first place it should surface is escalator lag — the gap between an input cost moving and a surcharge formula catching up. Fleet operating ratios and broker buy-rates should absorb the spike for one to several weeks before recovering it. Crude crossed $100 a barrel on 2026-09-09, the first time since July.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Spot truckload rates are holding while volume falls — a capacity signature, not a demand one

Spot rates holding steady while volume drops is the signature of a carrier-exit market, not a demand rebound. If shippers were simply pulling freight forward and pulling back after, price would fall with volume. It has not. FreightWaves' SONAR Truckload Volume Index fell roughly 15% on 2026-09-09, the post-Labor Day pullback from a pre-holiday surge.

Domestic Intermodal

Political pressure on the UP-NS rail merger is now multi-front, and that raises the odds of conditions, not a clean approval or denial

Congressional and multi-state opposition to the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger has widened past the standard competitive-effects review. That raises the odds the STB attaches labor or service conditions, rather than approving or denying the deal outright. 102 House members, coordinated with the Teamsters Rail Conference and led by Rep. Val Hoyle, wrote to the STB on 2026-09-03 demanding the merger be judged first on jobs, safety and service. Separately, attorneys general from seven states, including Montana and Tennessee, filed on 2026-09-01 asking the STB to deny the deal outright (same sources; ).

International container liner and feeder shipping

Panama Canal is tightening transit slots and easing draft at the same time — two different constraints, not one

The Panama Canal Authority (ACP) moved on two separate levers this month, and conflating them overstates the squeeze on cargo already booked. Cutting transit slots rations which vessels get through at all. Cutting draft rations how much cargo a vessel that gets through can carry. Daily transit slots fell from 34 to 32 effective 2026-09-03.
