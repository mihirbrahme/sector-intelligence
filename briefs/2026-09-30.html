Truckload Brokerage

Truckload capacity looks supply-driven again, undercutting this record's "cycle at the top" call from two days ago

The 2026-09-28 brief read an accelerating Cass linehaul index against rising Class 8 orders as demand pulling a freight cycle to its top. Fresh capacity and volume data argue the tightening is still carriers leaving the market, not shippers moving more freight into it. Accepted truckload volumes ran roughly 3-4% below year-ago levels through August, continuing a decline that began in late July, while the national Outbound Tender Rejection Index climbed to 14.32% after Labor Day before settling back near 13%. DAT's dry van spot linehaul rate, excluding fuel, held at $2.17 per mile for the week ending 2026-09-25, up 31.9% year-on-year and 18.5% above its nine-year seasonal average.

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

A closed FMC case supplies the first real evidence for a mechanism this record has flagged as unwritten — and the forwarder won it

detention and demurrage under-recovery scopes itself to a company as the payer of detention and demurrage, and separately notes the reverse case — a forwarder billing D&D onward to its own customer — as observed but not written. A closed Federal Maritime Commission case is the first hard evidence for that unwritten direction, and it is one case, not yet a pattern. Peloton Interactive sought $33.7 million from Model 2 forwarder Flexport International — $13.8 million demurrage, $16.2 million detention, $3.7 million yard and warehouse storage — alleging Flexport charged D&D without verifying fault or cause per container, on charges dating to the pandemic-era congestion of 2020-22. FMC Administrative Law Judge Mary Apostolakos Hervey rejected the claim in full in a 70-page initial decision issued 2026-09-09, finding Peloton's evidence relied on consolidated invoices rather than the container-by-container, day-by-day analysis FMC precedent requires.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

UK haulage's September failures are a cost-inflation story across every vintage, not a young-company problem

The 2026-09-24 brief read September's UK haulage failures as an entry-cost problem landing on young operators. A second long-established failure this month argues the pattern is broader: rising input costs are now taking down operators regardless of how long they cleared the entry bar. VG Mathers Limited, an Aberdeenshire haulier founded in 1968, entered liquidation on 2026-09-02 after director Colin Mathers cited cumulative pressure from fuel, insurance premiums, regulatory compliance costs and vehicle maintenance expenses, with no single shock named. That sits beside CVH Transport Services, 49 years old at its 2026-09-16 liquidation and already logged in the 2026-09-28 brief — two of five dated September UK haulage failures across both briefs now run 49 and 58 years, against three under six.
