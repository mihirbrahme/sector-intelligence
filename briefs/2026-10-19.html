Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

DAT's own September contract data confirms last week's fuel-surcharge read — then the first October weekly print breaks it

2026-10-16 read September's Cass volume growth as fuel-surcharge arithmetic rather than carriers regaining pricing power, and set the test explicitly: a meaningful ex-fuel increase in DAT's contract rate, not located that session, would weaken the read. DAT's own September release has since confirmed it in the stated direction — and the first full October weekly print already looks like a different mechanism taking over. DAT's September 2026 national averages: dry van contract linehaul ex-fuel fell 1 cent to $2.40/mile; dry van spot linehaul ex-fuel fell 2 cents to $2.17/mile; DAT's estimated fuel-surcharge component rose 14 cents to $0.84/mile, a record for all three equipment types it tracks. Both figures roll forward cleanly from benchmark register's August row.

International container liner and feeder shipping

The Suez Canal's return past half of pre-crisis transit levels adds capacity just as the container rate cycle was already softening

Capacity returning to the Suez Canal functions as a supply addition arriving on top of an already-softening demand signal. Together they point toward falling container spot rates through the fourth quarter rather than a holding pattern at summer's elevated levels. BIMCO's Niels Rasmussen, citing Clarksons Research data, reports that weekly container capacity transiting Suez averaged 753,000 TEU across the first two weeks of October 2026, against 1,457,000 TEU in the same weeks of 2023. That is the first time since the Houthi attacks began that transits have exceeded half of pre-disruption levels.
