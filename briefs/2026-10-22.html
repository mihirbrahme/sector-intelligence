Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Knight-Swift's 700bp truckload margin gain came from a shrinking fleet, which is a capacity choice as much as a market read

Knight-Swift's Q3 2026 Truckload segment adjusted operating ratio improved 700 basis points year over year, to 89.2% from 96.2%. Management names "pricing and network efficiency," but the operating detail points at a capacity choice: average tractors fell 4.0% to 20,470 while average revenue per tractor rose 10.9% to $56,419 and miles per tractor eased 0.9% (same sourcing and grading). That combination — fewer trucks earning more each — reads as the carrier cutting its own marginal capacity into a tightening market, not simply collecting a higher rate on an unchanged fleet. Model 4's OTR benchmark put the FY2025 trough median adjusted operating ratio near 99.8%; Knight-Swift's own series ran 93.9% (FY2024) to 92.8% (FY2025) to 91.5% (2026-Q2) before this quarter's further gain.

Domestic Intermodal

Knight-Swift's intermodal margin gain does not actually fit the fixed-cost mechanism this record credits it to

Knight-Swift's intermodal segment posted a 200-basis-point adjusted operating ratio improvement in Q3 2026, on load count up 13.3% and revenue per load up 15.2% year over year. Read against uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost — rail linehaul committed by contract and insensitive to volume — a surge this size in both volume and price should swing margin by far more than 200 basis points. The arithmetic does not support that read. Segment revenue rose 30.6% year over year to roughly $122.9m; back-computing operating expense from the two disclosed ratios gives an expense increase of roughly 28% over the same period [MODELLED: Q3'25 revenue ≈ $122.9m ÷ 1.306; expense = revenue × operating ratio in each period].

Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSO

CENTCOM's blockade tally now includes destroyed vessels, but its own wording does not separate ordinary shipping from Iran's sanctions-evasion fleet

2026-10-21 read the US Navy's Gulf enforcement as a second active risk channel for Model 12, beyond Houthi and Iranian attack. CENTCOM's own releases now report destroying vessels outright, a step beyond the disabling and turning-back already tracked — but the releases do not let that risk be assigned cleanly to the compliant, insured tonnage Model 12 actually charters. An October 5 release states US forces "destroyed 13 commercial vessels that were violating the blockade or part of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' multibillion-dollar shadow network" over the prior 12 weeks; an October 10 release instead gives "10 tankers linked to" that shadow network over the same period from the 2026-07-14 restart. The two releases do not reconcile, and neither names which destroyed vessels were ordinary commercial ships and which were the aging, unflagged "dark fleet" tonnage built for sanctions evasion.
