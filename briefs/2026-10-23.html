Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Werner's pre-announced Q3 miss is a cost story, not a capacity story, which cuts against the fleet-discipline reading this record gave Knight-Swift two days ago

2026-10-22 read Knight-Swift's Q3 margin gain as a carrier choosing to shrink its own fleet into a tightening market, and asked whether Werner or Schneider would show the same signature when they reported. Werner answered first, four days before its scheduled call, and the answer is the opposite pattern: rising cost, not falling capacity. Werner pre-announced preliminary Q3 2026 results on 2026-10-19: revenue of approximately $946m to $951m and adjusted diluted EPS of $0.15 to $0.16, against a prior consensus of $0.36. Werner attributes roughly $0.18 per share of the sequential deterioration versus Q2 2026 to three items: rising fuel cost, driver recruitment and retention spending, and a late-quarter adjustment to insurance reserves for past claims.

International container liner and feeder shipping

Suez transits just crossed 50% of pre-crisis levels, which is real but still far short of the flood this record's capacity-return forecast assumed

2026-10-19 forecast falling container spot rates on returning Suez capacity; 2026-10-21 found the Asia-Europe lane rising instead and called the forecast "already wrong." The gap between those two reads narrows once the actual scale of the return is counted, and it explains why rates have not yet broken: the return is real but still partial. BIMCO, citing Clarksons Research, reports weekly container capacity transiting Suez averaged 753,000 TEU in the first two weeks of October 2026, against 1,457,000 TEU in the same period of 2023 — the first time transits have exceeded half of pre-disruption levels since the Houthi campaign began. The same release puts the share of container capacity on the Suez-or-Cape corridor actually using Suez at 36% in early October, up from an 8% average across 2024 to mid-2026, but still well below the roughly 80% share that ran before the attacks.

Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSO

CENTCOM's own vessel-redirection counts do not form a consistent series, and this record should stop reading any single figure as evidence of escalation

2026-10-21 flagged that wire reports put CENTCOM's cumulative blockade count anywhere from 72 to 135 and could not resolve it to one figure. Pulling the figures CENTCOM itself has released across the year shows the problem is not a reporting spread around one true number — the releases are not monotonic, so no single count can be read as a running total. CENTCOM's own cited figures, in the order reported: 97 vessels redirected and 4 disabled as of 2026-05-21; 45 redirected, 2 disabled as of 2026-08-04; 53 redirected as of roughly 2026-08-08; 82 redirected, 3 disabled as of 2026-08-28; 84 redirected as of 2026-09-01; 67 redirected and 15 humanitarian passages separately counted in another release. A count that runs 97 in May and 45 in August is not the same cumulative series — each figure almost certainly answers a differently scoped question ("since blockade start," "this month," "this phase") that the releases do not state.
