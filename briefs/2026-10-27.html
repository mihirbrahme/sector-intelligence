Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSO

Hormuz attacks accelerated through October instead of cooling, reversing a read this record made four days ago

2026-10-26 read the attack channel as cooling from its October 7 peak, resting on one outlet's phrasing that the pace had "slowed from the first half of the month." A comparable, counted series now exists, and it points the other way: the cumulative count climbed through October rather than levelling off. Maritime-security sources cited by Reuters counted at least 12 attacks on oil, LNG and LPG tankers in the week of 2026-09-28 to 2026-10-05, the highest single week since the war began on 2026-02-28. UKMTO-sourced counts then rose through the month: at least 18 attacks since October 1 by 2026-10-13, 22 vessels struck between October 1 and 13, and 23 attacks since October 1 by roughly 2026-10-15, "almost double" the average monthly pace since February, with six ships hit in a single day — the highest daily count of the war.

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

Trucking's capacity exit is now a sector-wide supply story, not just Knight-Swift's own choice to shrink its fleet

2026-10-22 read Knight-Swift's Q3 margin gain as a capacity choice — fewer tractors, higher revenue per tractor — and asked whether a second large carrier would show the same signature. The capacity data arriving since says this is not one carrier's choice: the whole sector is losing trucks to bankruptcy while the usual response to elevated tender rejections is not showing up. FreightWaves's own review of federal bankruptcy filings counted at least 16 carriers filing Chapter 7 or Chapter 11 between 2026-09-18 and 2026-10-21, spanning general freight, petroleum transport and timber haulage, from single-truck operators up to fleets of several dozen power units — among them American Petroleum Carriers (McAllen, Texas, Chapter 11) and Midwest Expedited (petition filed 2026-10-06). A separate FreightWaves analysis of SONAR data shows new-for-hire fleet tractors falling to 19,520 in August 2026, roughly 14% below the first-half average, while the SONAR Truckload Rejection Index held near 14% — about three times its 2023–2025 range, after rising roughly 268% over three years against volume growth of roughly 9%.

Domestic Intermodal

AAR's own weekly count shows intermodal volume accelerating nationally, which weakens the case that Knight-Swift's Q3 numbers are a company-specific signature

2026-10-22 and 2026-10-26 found that Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc.'s Q3 intermodal margin gain did not fit uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost's fixed-cost prediction: operating expense grew nearly as fast as a 30.6% revenue surge, a variable-cost signature rather than a fixed one. The Association of American Railroads' own weekly traffic count, read for the first time this session, points at why: intermodal volume is accelerating across the whole network, not only at Knight-Swift. AAR reported intermodal units up 7.4% year over year in the week ending 2026-10-03, 10.4% in the week ending 2026-10-10, and 12.6% in the week ending 2026-10-17 — each week faster than the last — against carload growth of only 2.4% to 3.5% over the same three weeks. Cumulative intermodal units are up 4.6% year to date across the first 41 weeks of 2026, ahead of carloads at 2.7%.
