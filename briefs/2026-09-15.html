International container liner and feeder shipping

Gemini’s phased return to Suez trades Red Sea risk for transit time, eroding the capacity cushion supporting Asia-Europe spot rates

Returning major liner loops through the Suez Canal reverses the vessel capacity absorption that has supported container freight rates since early 2024. For forwarders, it turns a carrier capacity bottleneck into an immediate risk of margin compression on contracted container slots. On 2026-09-14, Maersk announced Gemini will return four mainline strings—AE5, AE11, AE12, ME2—to Suez (Maersk advisory, 2026-09-14; for carrier advisory; Seatrade Maritime, 2026-09-14; for trade reporting). Westbound sailings begin between 2026-09-19 and 2026-09-24.

The UP-NS rivals are no longer only attacking the merger's substance — they are trying to freeze it

BNSF, CPKC and CSX's joint letter to the STB is a different kind of opposition than BNSF's own dismissal motion three days earlier. Ports & Terminals

Hapag-Lloyd’s long-term terminal partnership with DP World uses contracted capacity to de-risk African feeder growth without terminal capex

Ocean carriers expanding into African trades face severe port congestion and berthing queues. Securing multi-terminal priority agreements provides operational schedule certainty without the heavy capital expenditure of acquiring port concessions. On 2026-09-14, Hapag-Lloyd announced an agreement with DP World securing long-term capacity at Dakar, Luanda and Dar es Salaam (Hapag-Lloyd release and Splash247, 2026-09-14; for release; for press). The deal also includes infrastructure cooperation at Banana and Maputo.
