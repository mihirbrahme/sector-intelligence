Per-transaction managed service or BPO

In 90 seconds

What it is

A commercial model where the provider takes operational ownership of a discrete process, charging a fixed fee per completed unit of work rather than selling software licenses or consulting hours

Where it applies

Truckload Brokerage, Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

How it works mechanically

The provider contracts to deliver an end-to-end operational function (e.g., customs entry creation, freight audit clearing, or carrier safety verification).

The contract sets a fixed price per processed unit of work: - $18 per completed import customs entry. - $1.75 per audited and reconciled carrier freight invoice. - $5 per fully verified carrier onboarding record.

The provider furnishes all software tooling, integration connectors, and operational personnel required to clear the workflow.

The buyer passes raw transaction inputs (scanned commercial invoices, EDI 210 feeds, or carrier applications) to the provider.

The provider resolves exceptions and returns completed, compliant transactions to the client's TMS.

Contracts include minimum monthly transaction commitments and strict Service Level Agreements (SLAs) on turnaround speed and error rates.

What it suits

  • Document processing and customs entry automation addressing quote-to-book manual handoff: Managing customs declarations for freight forwarders.
  • Freight audit and payment automation addressing billing exception leakage: Processing complex carrier freight bills.
  • Carrier onboarding and compliance automation addressing carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity: Running 24/7 carrier safety vetting and insurance verification.

Risk allocation

  • Buyer risk: Low. The buyer converts fixed overhead (back-office salaries) into variable cost that flexes with shipment volume. If freight volume declines, costs decline proportionally.
  • Provider risk: High. The provider absorbs all technical and operational execution risk. If the provider's automation fails and human clerks must manually process transactions, the provider's profit margin turns negative.

What has to be measurable

  • Unambiguous transaction unit counts (cleared customs entries, settled invoices).
  • Turnaround time metrics (e.g., 98% of customs filings completed within 2 hours of document receipt).
  • Quality/accuracy thresholds (e.g., invoice error dispute rate below 0.5%).

Because the unit of work matches the invoice line, commercial disputes over billing are rare.

Where it fails

  1. Volume collapse below minimums: If market freight volumes decline sharply, the buyer attempts to terminate the contract or renegotiate minimum fee commitments.
  2. Extreme input fragmentation: If the buyer passes corrupted, unreadable documents or non-standard freight that requires extensive manual research, provider operational costs explode.

Buyer size and maturity

  • Best fit: Mid-market freight forwarders and brokers ($50M to $300M revenue) struggling to recruit and retain specialized back-office staff (e.g., customs entry clerks).
  • Poor fit: Mega-forwarders (Expeditors International of Washington, Inc., Kuehne+Nagel) that operate massive in-house offshore shared service centers.

Does this structure pay for precondition work?

Yes. It absorbs and internalizes the precondition work completely.

In a managed service, the provider does not ask the buyer to pay an upfront fee for master data cleansing or tariff digitization.

Instead, the provider performs the prerequisite data cleansing as an internal capital investment: - The provider cleans the client's SKU catalog and builds custom HTS cross-reference tables during onboarding. - The provider absorbs this upfront cost because its long-term gross margin depends on driving automation up to 80%+. - Once automated, the provider earns software gross margins (70%+) on a fixed per-transaction fee, rapidly recovering the initial data investment.

The buyer experiences zero friction because they never see a line item for "data cleaning."

Boutique positioning against Tier 1 SIs

Boutiques use per-transaction managed services to displace legacy Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) conglomerates:

  • Technology-enabled unit pricing vs FTE "body-shop" leasing: Legacy BPOs (Genpact, Wipro, Infosys) sell FTE staffing models ("10 offshore clerks at $2,500/month each"). When volume drops, the client still pays. A boutique charges strictly per transaction, using modern intelligent document processing to run the workflow with two engineers rather than ten clerks.
  • Guaranteed turnaround SLAs with financial penalties: Boutiques win contracts by putting skin in the game, offering 15-minute customs entry turnaround SLAs backed by fee rebates, which legacy BPOs refuse to match.

Where this appears elsewhere

It applies directly to Truckload Brokerage, Freight Forwarding / NVOCC.

Systems implementing this include Blue Yonder (formerly JDA Software / Manugistics), Boutiques and specialist advisories, CargoWise (WiseTech Global), Cass Information Systems, Denim (formerly Axle Payments), Descartes Systems Group.
