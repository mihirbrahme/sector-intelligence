Outcome-based and gainshare commercial structures

In 90 seconds

What it is

A commercial structure where provider compensation is tied directly to verified operational cost reductions or margin improvements measured against an agreed baseline

Where it applies

Truckload Brokerage, Contract Logistics / 3PL Warehousing

How it works mechanically

The provider contracts to deploy technology or operational consulting with low upfront fees, taking payment as a percentage of verified financial improvement (typically 15% to 30% of audited savings or margin expansion).

Before work begins, the parties agree on a baseline metric: - Cost per order line in a contract warehouse. - Net revenue per load in truckload brokerage. - Direct overtime labor hours per 1,000 cases handled.

The contract establishes an audit protocol: savings are calculated quarterly by comparing actual operational performance against the baseline, adjusted for volume and macro rate indices.

The buyer remits the agreed gainshare percentage to the provider 30 days after the quarterly audit is reconciled.

What it suits

  • Dynamic pricing and buy-rate guidance addressing manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility: Earning a percentage of gross profit per load expansion.
  • Warehouse labour productivity instrumentation addressing contract vintage margin decay: Earning a share of direct labor expense reductions per order line.

Risk allocation

  • Buyer risk: Very Low. The buyer pays negligible fees unless the operational metric demonstrably improves.
  • Provider risk: Extreme. The provider invests capital, software, and labor upfront. If the client fails to adopt the system, or if macro market shifts distort the baseline, the provider receives zero compensation.

What has to be measurable: the baseline trap

Gainshare requires an independently verifiable, unalterable baseline.

In transport and logistics, this is where gainshare almost always fails.

  • In truckload brokerage, a falling freight market widens gross margin percentages automatically as purchased transportation drops faster than shipper contract rates. A provider claiming a gainshare on margin percentage is taking credit for the market cycle.
  • In contract logistics, client SKU proliferation or changes in order profiles alter units-per-hour benchmarks without any operational failure.

A gainshare struck on a metric neither party can compute independently is a dispute with a signature on it.

Where it fails

  1. Attribution warfare: When performance improves, the client argues that savings resulted from internal management actions, carrier renegotiations, or fuel price drops rather than the provider's tool.
  2. Accounting manipulation: The client reallocates overhead or shifts volume between facilities, changing the denominator of the gainshare metric.

Buyer size and maturity

  • Best fit: Sophisticated enterprise operators with dedicated industrial engineering teams, clean ERP cost accounting, and established baseline reporting.
  • Poor fit: Mid-market forwarders and brokers who do not track unit-of-work costs independently.

Does this structure pay for precondition work?

No. Gainshare completely fails to fund precondition work.

As proven in logistics automation preconditions are data and contract alignment, six of ten patterns require extensive master data cleansing and contract standardization before any software can function.

Under pure gainshare: - The provider must spend months cleaning carrier master files or building Engineered Labour Standards with zero compensation. - The buyer refuses to pay milestone fees because "the contract is outcome-based." - The provider runs out of working capital during the precondition phase before any operational gain can be generated.

Gainshare structures guarantee that implementations stall during the prerequisite data cleanup.

Boutique positioning against Tier 1 SIs

Boutiques succeed with gainshare only by violently narrowing its scope:

  • Single-metric physical gainshare vs EBITDA transformation: Tier 1 consultancies propose broad "EBITDA margin gainshares" that inevitably collapse in audit disputes. A boutique structures gainshare around a single physical metric that cannot be gamed: e.g., "30% of verified direct forklift travel hours saved via task interleaving," measured directly by machine telematics.
  • Hybrid "Floor-and-Gainshare": The boutique charges a base fee that fully covers its labor cost for the precondition master-data work, with a secondary gainshare kicker on subsequent operational throughput gains.

Where this appears elsewhere

It applies directly to Truckload Brokerage, Contract Logistics / 3PL Warehousing.
