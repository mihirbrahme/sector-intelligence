In 90 seconds

What it is

A purely performance-contingent commercial model where provider compensation is paid as a percentage of actual cash recovered or billing credits issued from disputed transportation charges

Where it applies

Truckload Brokerage, Freight Forwarding / NVOCC, Ports & Terminals, Domestic Intermodal

How it works mechanically

The provider contracts to audit historical and active transportation transactions with zero upfront implementation fees or software subscriptions.

The provider earns an agreed contingency fee (typically 20% to 35%) on verified financial recoveries: - Cash refunds deposited into the client's bank account from carrier over-billings. - Formal billing credit memos issued by ocean liners, railroads, or motor carriers canceling detention, demurrage, or incorrect freight invoices. - Cash settlements won via Federal Maritime Commission (FMC) Part 502 formal charge complaints.

The client remits payment to the provider only after the refund check clears or the credit memo is applied against outstanding payables.

If zero dollars are recovered, the client owes zero dollars.

What it suits

  • Detention and demurrage dispute recovery addressing detention and demurrage under-recovery: Recovering improper ocean container per-diem penalties.
  • Freight audit and payment automation addressing billing exception leakage: Clawing back duplicate freight payments and uncontracted accessorial charges.

Risk allocation

  • Buyer risk: Zero. The buyer invests no balance-sheet capital, incurs no software license commitments, and pays only out of realized cash windfalls.
  • Provider risk: Moderate. The provider performs audit and legal filing labor upfront. However, because transport billing error rates are structurally persistent (2% to 5% across the sector), recovery yields are highly predictable.

What has to be measurable

Measurability is absolute and binary: - Bank transaction settlement records. - Written carrier credit memos referencing disputed invoice numbers. - Formal FMC or court settlement orders.

Unlike gainshare, there is no dispute over baseline definitions, weather delays, or market inflections. The metric is cash received.

Where it fails

  1. Commercial relationship intervention: The client's procurement or carrier relations team blocks dispute filings, fearing ocean liners or key motor carriers will retaliate by cutting capacity allocations.
  2. The "Diminishing Returns" plateau: As automated pre-payment controls improve upstream data quality, historical recovery pools dry up, eroding the provider's recurring contingency revenue.

Buyer size and maturity

  • Best fit: All buyer tiers, particularly margin-constrained freight forwarders (Model 2) and drayage carriers facing aggressive ocean carrier per-diem assessments.

Does this structure pay for precondition work?

Yes. It is the ultimate self-funding mechanism for prerequisite data work.

As demonstrated in detention and demurrage recovery, automating dispute filing requires building continuous terminal portal scraping and mobile gate-receipt pipelines—precondition work buyers refuse to fund out of pocket.

The contingency structure solves this via a two-stage "Self-Funding Precondition" model: 1. The Historical Cash Haul: The provider runs an initial 180-day historical audit of paid carrier invoices, recovering $200,000 to $500,000 in improper detention or audit fees. 2. Reinvestment Escrow: The client and provider agree in the contract that 50% of the provider's earned contingency fees are credited into an implementation escrow. 3. Funded Preconditions: This escrow funds the technical integration of terminal scraping tools and clean carrier master files.

The client gets its master data cleansed and automated pipelines built using "found money" from ocean carrier over-billings, requiring zero capital budget appropriation.

Boutique positioning against Tier 1 SIs

This is one of the most defensible commercial positions a boutique can adopt against Tier 1 firms:

  • The Independence and Risk Bar: Tier 1 accounting and consulting firms (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG) are strictly barred by internal risk management and auditor independence regulations (Sarbanes-Oxley) from accepting contingency fee arrangements for audited corporate clients. They cannot legally offer this structure.
  • Pure Alignment vs Retainer Billing: While Tier 1 SIs demand $250,000 upfront retainers to "study accessorial billing processes," a boutique offers to audit the files for free and take 25% of the cash recovered. Clients overwhelmingly sign the contingency contract.
