What the work is

The granular, activity-based calculation of the exact operational costs incurred to service specific customer accounts, distribution lanes, facilities, consignees, and shipment profiles across a transport or logistics enterprise.

Operationally, the pattern replaces blunt company-wide overhead allocations (e.g. allocating SG&A as a flat percentage of gross revenue) with direct causality-based cost assignment. It tracks operational cost drivers—driver detention hours, facility dwell times, delivery appointment strictness, pick density, packaging exceptions, order cancellation rates, and billing dispute processing hours—and assigns them directly to the generating customer account. This exposes the "whale curve" of profitability, separating high-volume margin-destroying accounts from high-margin accounts.

What it is aimed at

  • contract vintage margin decay Contract vintage margin decay: Pinpoints legacy accounts whose operational complexity has outpaced contractual rate tables, providing empirical evidence for targeted contract renegotiations.
  • billing exception leakage Billing exception leakage: Quantifies the exact administrative and operational cost of resolving custom invoice formatting, billing disputes, and non-standard documentation demanded by difficult shippers.
  • uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost: Exposes accounts where uncompensated facility dwell and circuitous routing drive up driver payroll and fuel spend without corresponding revenue recovery.
  • forwarding cost pass-through lag on the open shipment file Forwarding cost pass-through lag: Tests file-level cost against the quote before margin leakage becomes a period-end gross-profit movement.
  • forwarding operations establishment fixed against falling file count Forwarding operations establishment fixed against falling file count: Separates desk workload from entity-level admin cost so capacity can be removed where work has actually left.
  • volume-tender dilution of forwarding portfolio yield Volume-tender dilution of forwarding portfolio yield: Prices tenders against full file cost rather than buy-rate markup alone.

Models, sub-sectors and geographies

  • Model 1 (Truckload Brokerage): Identifying shipper accounts that require 5+ manual check-calls per load, reject electronic tracking, or cause severe destination driver dwell, turning 15% paper gross margins into negative net operating income.
  • Model 2 (Freight Forwarding): Measuring administrative file-handling cost per shipment; tracking customs entry amendments, demurrage dispute handling, and non-standard documentation touches per importer account.
  • Model 3 (Contract Logistics): Calculating exact warehouse storage cube utilization, piece vs carton pick labor, kitting touches, and customer return handling costs per client account to eliminate unbilled warehouse operational drag.
  • Model 4 (Asset Carriage TL) & Model 5 (LTL): Profiling consignee facilities; isolating congested receiver distribution centers that burn driver on-duty driving hours and destroy asset utilization.
  • Model 6 (Ports & Terminals): Calculating real cost-per-move across shipping line calls, accounting for yard re-handling counts, empty box dwell times, and gate peak-hour labor staffing.
  • Model 7 (Domestic Intermodal): Tracking chassis street dwell and rail terminal staging delays per drayage customer.

Unit of work and its economics

A customer account, shipment file, warehouse order line, or lane origin-destination pair.

Activity-based account segmentation exposes the bottom 10% to 20% of customer accounts that destroy 40% to 80% of net operating profits. Enforcing accessorial minimums and re-pricing unprofitable freight expands overall segment operating margins by 70 to 140 basis points.

Precondition and difficulty

Activity telemetry ingestion and commercial sales resistance dominate implementation difficulty.

Cost-to-serve models cannot produce defensible results without granular operational data: 1. Telemetry integration: Capturing true cost requires marrying telematics/geofence dwell data, warehouse management pick timestamps, and accounting payroll data into a single analytical layer. 2. Sales commission resistance: Sales leaders aggressively resist account-level profitability transparency because it exposes prominent "trophy" accounts as unprofitable, threatening volume-based compensation plans. 3. Operational granularity: If driver wait time or invoice dispute administrative hours are not logged against specific shipment IDs, cost allocation defaults back to arbitrary averages, invalidating the model.

Commercial structures

Delivered via fixed-price diagnostic then build for initial baseline calculation and account portfolio segmentation, or subscription SaaS for continuous lane and customer profitability monitoring.

What has already failed here

Cost models that lack commercial enforcement mechanisms become shelf-ware.

As noted in exception resolution requires commercial authority, identifying an unprofitable operational pattern yields zero financial return unless commercial leadership possesses the contractual mandate and executive backing to re-price or shed the offending business.

Where it applies

Commercial enforceability varies across geographies: US and European shippers operate under strict, contractually codified accessorial fee schedules (detention, TONU, liftgate). In Southeast Asian emerging markets, cutthroat broker competition makes enforcing facility detention fees commercially difficult without risking entire account loss.

Where it has worked

-: Council of Supply Chain Management Professionals (CSCMP) Cost-to-Serve Benchmarking: Multi-operator implementations demonstrate that the top 20% of customer accounts typically generate 160% to 220% of net operating profit, while the bottom 10% to 20% destroy 40% to 80% of enterprise earnings through unrecovered operational exceptions and facility dwell. -: Public 3PL & Contract Logistics Filings (e.g. GXO Logistics, RXO FY2024–FY2025 10-K): Disclosed that systematic account-level profitability reviews and enforcing contractual accessorial minimums enabled contract logistics and managed transportation units to expand operating margins by 70 to 140 basis points while voluntarily pruning unprofitable shipment volume.
