What the work is

The service manages the order-to-cash lifecycle for freight revenue across shippers, freight forwarders, and vessel charterers.

The workflow executes across five operational stages:

  1. Packet assembly: Merges rate confirmations, delivery receipts, customs documents, and EDI 210 files.
  2. Electronic submission: Transmits billing packets through shipper portals, audit networks, and EDI.
  3. Dispute clearing: Resolves invoice rejections, auditing accessorial backup and correcting PO numbers.
  4. Cash application: Reconciles electronic remittances to invoice lines, chasing aging receivables.
  5. Credit and lien execution: Assesses counterparty solvency, requires escrow deposits, and executes possessory freight liens during defaults.

How it differs by model

In Model 1, the desk accelerates collections to bridge cash gaps against carrier settlements.

In Model 2, the desk invoices international freight, reconciling ocean legs, customs fees, and currency shifts.

In Model 4 and Model 5, the desk audits weight tickets, billing terminal corrections.

In Model 7, the desk bills intermodal loads, reconciling rail fuel and chassis per-diem fees.

In Model 10 and Model 11, the desk collects ocean freight. Under charter counterparty default interrupts cash and voyage control, it monitors credit risk, enforces escrow deposits, and executes maritime liens during defaults.

What it is aimed at

  • billing exception leakage: Matches invoice line items against shipper contract rules, preventing short-payments and billing rejections. Read what follows as a lens applied to the mechanisms above, not as a mechanism this pattern arrests: Automates standard billing dispute resolution, removing invoice correction friction from frontline sales desks.
  • avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty (Working capital gap): Compresses Days Sales Outstanding by accelerating billing, closing the cash deficit against carrier payables.
  • charter counterparty default interrupts cash and voyage control (Charter counterparty default): Mitigates credit default exposure by requiring escrow funding and executing maritime liens when customers default.
  • forwarding receivable extension funded on gross turnover: Moves file billing earlier by clearing cost, document and accessorial holds before the customer payment clock starts.

Unit of work and its economics

The unit of work is the processed customer freight invoice or the cleared receivables transaction.

Processing costs range from $2.00 to $4.50 per invoice offshore, compared to $8.00 to $18.00 onshore.

Offshore BPO FTEs in India or the Philippines cost $2,000 to $3,200 monthly, compared to $6,000 to $8,500 onshore.

Compressing DSO by 7 to 12 days frees substantial liquidity. Rigorous dispute clearing reduces bad debt write-offs by 30% to 50%.

Preconditions

Deploying a freight receivables desk requires four prerequisites:

  1. Receipt discipline: Drivers and carriers must upload legible delivery receipts within 24 hours of delivery.
  2. Billing profiles: Shipper accounts payable portal rules, reference fields, and invoice formats must be stored electronically.
  3. Credit protocols: Treasury must define formal customer credit limits, payment terms, and automated credit hold thresholds.
  4. Ledger automation: Connect bank remittance feeds directly to transport management software for cash application.

Commercial structures

  • Per-transaction BPO: Fixed fee of $2.25 to $3.75 per collected customer invoice.
  • Build-operate-transfer (BOT): External specialists establish an offshore receivables shared service center, transferring ownership after 24 months.

What has already failed here

Offshore BPO receivables lift-and-shift programs failed without billing integration. Brokers moved billing clerks offshore without digitizing portal rules.

Clerks generated generic PDF invoices omitting mandatory reference numbers, which customer systems rejected. Receivables aged past 90 days, creating working capital deficits under avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty.

Brokers incurred millions in bad debt write-offs. Per monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace, shifting broken receivables workflows offshore destroys cash flow.

Where the hard part sits

The marketing pitch promises automated freight billing and rapid collection. Operational difficulty sits in complex payment portals and credit enforcement.

Shippers use audit portals configured to reject invoices on minor formatting technicalities. Desks must manage hundreds of customer web portals.

Sales reps resist credit enforcement, lobbying to waive credit holds and delay liens to preserve relationships.

Where it applies

In the United States, settlement relies on EDI 210 messaging, automated clearing, and UCC Article 7 liens.

In the United Kingdom and Europe, desks navigate value-added tax rules and PEPPOL standards via nearshore hubs in Poland and Romania.

In Southeast Asia, carriage requires tax documentation like Indonesian e-Faktur. Cash practices require regional desks in Manila, Jakarta, or Kuala Lumpur.
