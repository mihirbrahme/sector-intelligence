What the work is

The centralisation, predictive scoping, and execution governance of scheduled vessel dry-docking and major asset turnaround programs. It aligns statutory classification society special surveys (every 5 years under DNV, Lloyd's Register, Bureau Veritas, or ABS), intermediate surveys (at 30–36 months), shipyard dry-dock slot reservations, subcontractor staging, and mandatory retrofits.

Operationally, the pattern establishes digital work-package management that begins 12 to 18 months prior to shipyard arrival. It captures continuous condition-monitoring feeds (ultrasonic hull thickness gauging, vibration analysis, lube oil wear metals) to build definitive repair specifications. During the yard stay, it replaces static paper Gantt charts with daily critical-path tracking, managing concurrent workstreams across steel plate renewal, sea valve overhauls, tailshaft extraction, hull grit-blasting, and silicone coating application.

How it differs by model

  • Model 8 (Inland Liquid Bulk): Focuses on US Coast Guard Subchapter M inspection compliance, cargo pump overhauls, and vapor recovery line certifications across river pushboats and tank barges.
  • Model 9 (Protected Liner Shipping): Governed by strict US Jones Act shipyard requirements. With only a handful of qualified domestic commercial dry-docks available in North America, scheduling must be secured 24 months in advance, and delays immediately threaten island lifelines (e.g. Hawaii, Puerto Rico).
  • Model 10 (International Container Liner): High opportunity cost of off-hire requires dry-dock coordination around alliance trade loops; concurrent execution of environmental retrofits (bulbous bow modifications, shore-power connections) with standard hull maintenance.
  • Model 11 (Dry Bulk Ocean Logistics): Managing ballast tank corrosion and hatch cover hydraulic overhauls; timing dockings precisely during seasonal grain/ore export lulls to minimize lost charter earnings.
  • Model 12 (Energy Shipping): Demands the highest safety and inspection rigor under oil major SIRE/CDI standards; cryogenic containment insulation checks on LNG carriers and inert gas system overhauls on crude tankers.
  • Model 13 (Great Lakes & Coastal Bulk): Highly concentrated winter lay-up turnaround. Fleet overhauls must be completed during the mandatory January–March St. Lawrence Seaway ice closure, creating intense local contractor competition across Canadian and US Great Lakes yards.

What it is aimed at

  • dry-docking bunching removes revenue days Dry-docking bunching removes revenue days: Staggers fleet survey dates across multiple fiscal years to prevent multiple sister vessels from being out of service during peak seasonal rate environments.
  • off-hire and technical downtime convert owned fleet into idle capital Off-hire and technical downtime convert owned fleet into idle capital: Accelerates critical path execution in the shipyard, converting non-revenue yard days back into active trading days.
  • fleet age capex cliff raises maintenance before replacement is funded Fleet age capex cliff raises maintenance before replacement is funded: Implements rigorous structural scoping on vessels aged >15 years, preventing surprise hull plate renewal costs from exhausting annual M&R capital.

Unit of work and its economics

The unit of work is an off-hire asset-day or a dry-docking turnaround project.

A standard special survey on a commercial vessel consumes 20 to 30 scheduled days off-hire, incurring $2.0 million to $5.0 million in direct shipyard, class surveyor, and equipment fees. Unscheduled repair extensions add $15,000 to $45,000 per day in direct yard/dockage charges, plus the lost Time Charter Equivalent (TCE) revenue of $15,000 to $65,000 per day depending on the vessel segment. Predictive scoping reduces off-hire duration by 3 to 7 days per docking, saving $120,000 to $500,000 in combined direct costs and recovered trading revenue per vessel.

Preconditions

Predictive pre-docking scoping and long-lead procurement dominate total effort:

  1. Ultrasonic thickness measurement (UTM) pre-surveys: Hull gauging and cargo hold inspections must be conducted by certified riding squads 6 months prior to yard entry. Discovering wasted steel plate while on the dry-dock blocks automatically blows out schedules by weeks.
  2. Long-lead equipment procurement: OEM spare parts for main engine crankshafts, stern tube seals, and bow thruster units require 6 to 9-month procurement lead times. If spares are not staged at the shipyard prior to vessel arrival, dry-dock stays stall on parts logistics.
  3. Class surveyor alignment: Pre-agreeing repair specifications with DNV or Lloyd's Register surveyors prevents mid-docking work scope rejections.

Commercial structures

Typically procured as subscription SaaS for planned maintenance software (e.g. DNV ShipManager, Kongsberg, Star Information Systems), paired with fixed-price diagnostic then build for shipyard specification engineering. Independent marine superintendency firms are engaged on time and materials ($1,200 to $2,500 per superintendent/day) for on-site shipyard project governance.

What has already failed here

Digital dry-dock turnaround management software deployed without prior physical hull thickness testing and ultrasonic steel gauging fails to prevent massive schedule overruns.

When vessels enter the dry-dock based on paper maintenance schedules, opening cargo holds often reveals unbudgeted plate corrosion and structural fractures. Shipyard stays double from 25 days to 50 days, incurring massive off-hire losses.

Where the hard part sits

The vendor pitch describes automated Gantt charts and real-time mobile task updating.

The physical difficulty is managing uncooperative shipyard labor, class surveyor discretion, and subcontractor bottlenecks: - Shipyards frequently reassign skilled welders and pipefitters to competing navy or cruise ship projects mid-docking, causing critical path tasks to stall. - Independent class surveyors possess absolute authority to mandate additional steel renewals upon physical inspection, instantly expanding the work package. - Paint coating application requires strict ambient temperature and humidity windows; unexpected rainfall delays hull coating cycles regardless of software optimization.

Where it applies

Shipyard direct labor and steel renewal rates diverge dramatically across maritime repair hubs. Chinese and Vietnamese shipyards offer dry-dock labor and hull grit-blasting/coating at 30% to 50% lower daily tariffs than European (Rotterdam, Brest) or Mediterranean yards, making scheduled deviation to Asian yards accretive for international liner and bulk operators. Conversely, Jones Act vessels (Model 9) and US inland tank barges (Model 8) are legally restricted to high-cost domestic shipyards, where limited dry-dock capacity and union labor rates double direct overhaul costs, making schedule optimization an existential cash requirement.
