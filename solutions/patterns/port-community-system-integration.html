What the work is

The pattern links freight forwarder TMS platforms, carrier booking engines, and terminal operating systems directly with neutral national Port Community Systems (PCS). Examples include Portbase, DAKOSY, CrimsonLogic TradeNet, and 1-Stop.

The software replaces manual web portal entries with structured API and EDIFACT messages. Inbound import flows trigger automated pre-arrival declarations, electronic customs manifests, and veterinary inspection releases before vessels berth.

Outbound export workflows automate customs export declarations, verified gross mass (VGM) transmissions, and digital release orders. The system cross-references terminal cargo availability flags against inland truck dispatch schedules.

Commercial release replaces insecure email PIN codes with encrypted digital token handoffs. Drivers receive authorized terminal gate appointments tied to specific container release tokens.

How it differs by model

  • Model 2 (Forwarding/NVOCC): Forwarders automate import summary filings and customs clearances, cutting clerical cost per file and preventing demurrage. The per-file clerical cost is not restated here — it is a labour-rate derivation, and the sourced wage bases are in benchmark-register 5c.
  • Model 6 (Ports): Terminals ingest pre-arrival gate appointments, smoothing truck arrival peaks and maintaining yard velocity.

Unit of work and its economics

The unit of work is the electronic customs manifest, terminal release token, or gate transaction.

US import demurrage runs roughly $75 to $150 per container per day in the first tier, rising to $250 to $300 in later tiers on 3 to 7 days of free time. Automated customs pre-clearance cuts yard dwell from 5.5 days to 3.2 days.

Because the tariff is tiered, those 2.3 days are worth more than an average rate suggests. They are removed from the far end of the dwell distribution, where the later, higher tiers apply — so the saving is convex in dwell, not linear. Size it off the carrier's own tier schedule.

Manual document re-entry consumes freight-forwarding clerk labour per bill of lading; the per-document cost is a derivation from the wage bases in benchmark-register 5c, not a market price, and the figure this line once carried was destroyed. Separately, insecure PIN-based release exposes high-value cargo to fraudulent terminal collection — a claims exposure whose magnitude needs a cargo-insurer or court record to state.

Preconditions

Deployment requires four prerequisites:

  1. PCS API credentialing: Registration and security clearance with relevant port platforms.
  2. Standardised EDI mapping: Harmonising internal shipment records into UN/EDIFACT or JSON schemas.
  3. Customs broker power of attorney: Integration with customs authority interfaces for bond debiting.
  4. Drayage identity management: Enrolling approved trucking carriers into identity validation schemes.

What has already failed here

Consortium blockchain platforms attempting to replace municipal port systems have failed completely. As documented in consortium blockchain trade documentation, solutions like TradeLens collapsed after spending hundreds of millions.

Carriers refused to join a blockchain ledger controlled by a direct competitor (Maersk/IBM). In contrast, national Port Community Systems succeed because they operate as neutral, regulated public-private utilities.

Where the hard part sits

The pitch describes unified port connectivity. The operational reality sits in fragmented municipal bureaucracy: - Every national port authority operates proprietary data schemas, preventing a single global integration layer. - Customs physical inspection holds override electronic release signals without explaining root inspection causes. - Independent drayage operators resist adopting secure token apps, defaulting to unannounced gate arrivals.

Where it applies

  • North America: Port community adoption is fragmented; terminal portals lack a unified national customs interface.
  • UK/Europe: Mature ecosystems; Portbase (Rotterdam) and DAKOSY (Hamburg) mandate electronic customs and Secure Chain release.
  • Singapore: Centralised single window (TradeNet, Portnet); regulatory enforcement guarantees 100% electronic participation.
  • Malaysia/Indonesia: Port Klang Net and Inaportnet face varying compliance rates across secondary feeder ports.
  • Australia: 1-Stop platform dominates major container terminals, mandating vehicle booking system appointments.
