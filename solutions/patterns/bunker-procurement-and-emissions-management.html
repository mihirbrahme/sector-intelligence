What the work is

The pattern automates the procurement lifecycle for marine fuel while integrating live carbon accounting under regulatory regimes including EU ETS and FuelEU Maritime.

The system connects vessel telematics with procurement desks. When voyage planning generates bunker stems, software models landed energy costs across bunkering ports, factoring in fuel price, barge fees, port dues, deviations, and carbon surcharges.

Mass flow meter data logs custody transfers, cross-referencing delivered volumes against bunker delivery notes to eliminate quantity shortages.

Automated lab interfaces ingest ISO 8217 test results before fuel enters settling tanks, validating sulfur compliance, kinematic viscosity, flashpoint, and catalytic fines.

How it differs by model

  • Model 10 (Liner): Large container strings consume 100 to 250 MT/day. Fixed schedules require contractual bunker adjustment factor (BAF) formulas and transparent EU ETS surcharges per TEU.
  • Model 11 (Dry Bulk): Voyage charterers bear bunker risk, while time charterers purchase fuel directly. Operators balance redelivery bunker quantities against charterparty allowances.
  • Model 12 (Energy Shipping): Tankers operate dual-fuel engines burning boil-off gas, requiring real-time dispatch optimization between LNG and VLSFO.
  • Model 9 (Protected Liner): Jones Act vessels operate within North American Emission Control Areas, burning 0.10% sulfur MGO or LNG.

Unit of work and its economics

The unit of work is the metric tonne (MT) of bunker fuel or the executed bunker stem.

VLSFO traded at a global 4-port average of $862.00 per MT on 2026-09-15 — Singapore $895.00, Rotterdam $730.50. MGO was $911.50 in Singapore and $731.00 in Rotterdam.

The Singapore–Rotterdam spread of roughly $165/MT on VLSFO is the pattern's whole case. On a 1,500 MT stem that is about $247,000 of difference decided by where the vessel bunkers, before anyone negotiates a price. A Capesize burning 35 MT/day is spending roughly $30,000 per day at the 4-port average.

Under EU ETS, each MT of VLSFO yields 3.114 tonnes of CO2. At allowance prices of EUR 60 to EUR 85 per tCO2, carbon adds EUR 180 to EUR 260 per MT. Stem shortfalls run 1.5% to 3.0% on a 1,500 MT stem — at $862/MT that is $19,000 to $39,000 of fuel paid for and not delivered.

Preconditions

Deployment requires four prerequisites:

  1. Mass flow meter installation: Deploying Coriolis meters certified to SS 648 or ISO 22192 standards on delivery barges.
  2. Automated telemetry: Integrating onboard flow meters and noon reports to ingest continuous consumption logs.
  3. Lab testing integration: Electronic data links with testing laboratories for ISO 8217 reporting.
  4. Carbon allowance registries: Connecting trading desks to Union Registry accounts for EU ETS tracking.

What has already failed here

Digital reverse-auction bunker platforms selecting fuel solely on headline spot price have triggered operational disasters. As documented in monolithic ERP and WMS rip and replace, procurement tools uncoupling price from fuel stability caused engine seizures. Bunkers bought without pre-testing contaminated injectors with catalytic fines, causing dead-ship emergencies. Furthermore, passing generic carbon surcharges without voyage telematics provoked disputes and customer churn.

Where the hard part sits

The pitch describes automated energy procurement. The operational reality sits on bunker manifolds: - Barges aerate fuel during delivery (the cappuccino effect), inflating volumetric readings without Coriolis mass flow meters. - On-spec lab results take 48 to 72 hours, forcing vessels to sail before off-spec warnings arrive. - Carbon allowance volatility requires separate derivative trading desks that traditional bunker buyers cannot operate.

Where it applies

  • North America: Strict ECA 0.10% sulfur enforcement along US coastlines; high MGO price spreads penalise engine changeover delays.
  • UK/Europe: Mandates EU ETS compliance and FuelEU Maritime penalties; major hubs (Rotterdam, Antwerp) require mass flow meter bunkering.
  • Singapore: Largest bunkering hub; mandates MPA-approved mass flow meters and digital bunker delivery notes.
  • Malaysia/Indonesia: Anchorages (Tanjung Pelepas, Port Klang) offer lower prices but variable quality enforcement.
  • Australia: remote ports carry a premium per MT over the main bunkering hubs, which is why planners stem for the round trip rather than buying locally. For scale, the Singapore–Rotterdam VLSFO spread alone was ~$165/MT on 2026-09-15; a remote-port premium sits above that.
