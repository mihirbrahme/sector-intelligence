Commercial Contracting Structures

How the work is priced and contracted. The structure decides who carries the risk of the outcome, which is usually the live question.

  • Benchmarking and data consortium commercial modelsA cooperative commercial structure where logistics operators contribute anonymized operational transaction data in exchange for aggregated market rate intelligence, fraud alerts, or pre-cleansed master records
  • Build-operate-transfer commercial arrangementsA multi-year commercial structure where a provider designs, builds, and operates a specialized technology and operational capability under strict SLAs, subsequently transferring full ownership, staff, and intellectual property to the client
  • Fixed-price diagnostic then buildA two-phase commercial engagement that uncouples operational and data discovery from technology delivery, establishing an audited baseline before scoping the implementation build
  • Forward-deployed engineer and embedded technical arrangementsA commercial engagement embedding senior software and data engineers directly inside client operations to build, deploy, and iterate production automation workflows in live operational environments
  • Outcome-based and gainshare commercial structuresA commercial structure where provider compensation is tied directly to verified operational cost reductions or margin improvements measured against an agreed baseline
  • Per-transaction managed service or BPOA commercial model where the provider takes operational ownership of a discrete process, charging a fixed fee per completed unit of work rather than selling software licenses or consulting hours
  • Recovery fee and contingency commercial structuresA purely performance-contingent commercial model where provider compensation is paid as a percentage of actual cash recovered or billing credits issued from disputed transportation charges
  • Subscription SaaS commercial modelsA software delivery model charging fixed recurring annual or monthly subscription fees, frequently combined with consumption tiers based on load volume or active user seats
  • Time and materialsA flexible engagement structure where the buyer pays for professional engineering and advisory hours worked at agreed rate cards, typically structured in two-week sprints with budget caps

Failed Approaches & Graveyard

Approaches that were funded, deployed and did not hold. The most useful reading in this section, and the least written about elsewhere.

  • Autonomous long-haul trucking driverless commercialisationVenture-backed autonomous trucking firms failed to achieve commercial driverless operations on interstate highways because the capital cost of transfer-hub drayage and safety liability exceeded driver wage savings
  • Computer vision autonomous inventory auditing roboticsAutonomous mobile robots and computer-vision drones failed to scale for inventory auditing because detecting out-of-stock inventory does not solve the physical labor bottleneck of warehouse replenishment
  • Consortium blockchain for global trade documentation and shippingCentralized consortium blockchains failed in global container shipping because trade document friction is caused by maritime jurisdiction law and commercial carrier distrust, not by the absence of cryptographic ledgers
  • Digital freight brokerage automated matching without network effectsAlgorithmic load-matching platforms failed to replace traditional freight brokers because digital brokerage remains a working-capital-intensive credit and risk-underwriting business, not a software platform with zero marginal cost
  • Monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace in logistics and retail supply chainsMulti-year enterprise ERP and WMS "rip-and-replace" programs consistently failed because enterprise financial software cannot model physical warehouse and retail execution constraints without catastrophic operational disruption
  • Passive visibility control towers without operational executionReal-time transportation visibility platforms and control towers stalled across enterprise logistics because displaying shipment location data does not provide the contractual authority or physical capacity to resolve transit delays
