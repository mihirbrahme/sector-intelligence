Operating Models by Sector

Thirteen operating models. A company is read through the one it runs, because the model sets the unit of work, the peer set and the only denominator on which its margin means anything.

Brokers & Forwarders (Non-Asset)

Non-asset capacity intermediaries. Evaluated strictly on net revenue spread and loads or shipment files per operator. Personnel is the dominant cost.

Model 01 the load

Truckload Brokerage

buys capacity from carriers, sells it to shippers, keeps the spread.
Model 02 the shipment file

Freight Forwarding / NVOCC

buys space from ocean/air carriers, sells to shippers, manages the documentation and customs chain.

Trucking & Rail Fleets (Inland Asset)

Motor carriers, LTL terminal networks, and rail intermodal. Capital is in rolling stock and cross-docks. Evaluated on operating ratio (OR) and loaded asset productivity.

Model 04 the loaded mile, or the linehaul trip

Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleet

owns tractors, trailers, and employs drivers.
Model 05 the shipment, or the hundredweight

LTL

consolidates partial loads through a terminal network.
Model 07 the intermodal load — one container moved end to end

Domestic Intermodal

buys rail linehaul in bulk under multi-year contracts with the Class I railroads, owns or leases the container and chassis fleet, and arranges drayage at both ends.

Warehouses & Port Terminals (Facilities)

Dedicated facilities and gateway infrastructure. Multi-year contracts or concessions. Evaluated on cost per order line or crane moves per hour.

Model 03 the order line

Contract Logistics / 3PL Warehousing

operates warehouses and fulfilment under multi-year contracts, usually cost-plus or open-book with productivity commitments
Model 06 the box move

Ports & Terminals

charges for moves, storage, and berth access.

Ships & Barges (Marine)

Vessels, tank barges, and offshore fleets. Multi-decade balance sheet assets. Economics turn on revenue-days, time charter rates, and dry-dock management.

Model 08 the crewed towboat-day

Inland liquid-bulk marine / tank barge

owns tank barges and the towboats that push them, crews both, and sells liquid-bulk capacity on the US inland waterway system and coastwise.
Model 09 the container lifted

Protected liner shipping / Jones Act non-contiguous trades

operates US-flagged, US-built, US-crewed vessels on fixed day-of-week schedules between the mainland and the non-contiguous states and territories, where cabotage law bars foreign-flag competition.
Model 10 the loaded container carried, normally TEU

International container liner and feeder shipping

operates container vessels on scheduled international strings.
Model 11 the loaded voyage

Dry bulk ocean logistics and chartering

contracts to move dry bulk cargoes, then performs the voyage with owned vessels, chartered-in vessels, or spot tonnage.
Model 12 the contracted vessel-day or offshore asset-day

Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSO

owns or operates specialised energy marine assets and sells availability under long-term charters, contracts of affreightment, or offshore production and storage contracts.
Model 13 the revenue day or loaded vessel-day
