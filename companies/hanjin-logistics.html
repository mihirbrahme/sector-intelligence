How it is built

  • Parcel Express Network (Hanjin Express): Top-tier South Korean parcel delivery operator (#2/#3 market share alongside CJ Logistics and Lotte Global Logistics), anchored by the Daejeon Smart Mega Hub Terminal (opened January 2024; KRW 285bn capex; +1.2 million daily parcel sorting capacity with AI 3D auto-scanners).
  • Container Port Terminals (Model 6): Operates major container berths including Hanjin Busan Container Terminal (HJNC at Busan New Port), Hanjin Incheon Container Terminal (HJIT at Incheon New Port), and Pyeongtaek container stevedoring facilities.
  • Land Transport and Contract Logistics: Operates container drayage, industrial bulk haulage, petrol/chemical distribution, and port-side fulfilment centres across South Korea.
  • Global Forwarding and Cross-Border E-Commerce: Operates overseas subsidiaries and customs/fulfilment hubs in the US, China, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Europe, serving cross-border e-commerce platforms and Korean industrial exporters.

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Port congestion breaks liner rotation integrity. Vessels miss planned port windows, bunch at terminals, skip calls or require extra ships to keep weekly service.

Berth, yard and landside constraints turn terminal density into delay. A terminal has throughput demand, but berth productivity, yard ground slots or truck/rail evacuation cannot clear boxes at the rate vessels arrive.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.
