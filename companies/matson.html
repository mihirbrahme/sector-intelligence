What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Dry-docking bunching removes revenue days. Several scheduled dockings fall into the same period, reducing revenue days and utilisation before visible repair cost rises.

Fleet age capex cliff raises maintenance before replacement is funded. Ageing vessels show rising maintenance, dry-docking scope, off-hire and class-compliance capex before newbuild cash flow is ready.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.
