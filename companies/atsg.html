    Full-year revenue, CAM dry lease margins, Amazon and DHL contract metrics

    from EDGAR, accession 0000894081-25-000012.

    • Financial results. Total consolidated revenue was $2,075m ($2,048m prior year). Operating income was $178.5m. Adjusted EBITDA reached $562m.
    • Segment revenue and margin divergence. Form 10-K disclosures clearly separate the financial mechanics of dry aircraft leasing versus ACMI flight operations:
    • CAM (Aircraft Leasing) generated $435m in external and intersegment revenues with pre-tax earnings of $142m (~32.6% pretax margin), driven by long-term fixed monthly dry lease agreements ($220,000 to $310,000 per month per Boeing 767-300F).
    • ACMI Services (ABX Air & ATI) generated $1,525m in revenue with pre-tax earnings of $48m (~3.1% pretax margin), absorbing flight crew labor wage increases under amended collective bargaining agreements.
    • Block hour flying volume. Total flight operations block hours were 152,400 hours. Contract terms guarantee minimum monthly block hours (take-or-pay thresholds) from primary customers Amazon Air and DHL, shielding operating revenue from short-term e-commerce volume dips.
    • Customer concentration. Amazon represented 38% of consolidated revenue, and DHL represented 27% of consolidated revenue, illustrating high counterparty concentration typical of dedicated ACMI wet-lease logistics.
    • Capital expenditures and passenger-to-freighter conversions. Capital expenditures totaled $615m, reflecting acquisition of feedstock passenger aircraft and feedstock conversion costs into medium widebody cargo freighters at third-party MRO modification stations (PEMCO and Israel Aerospace Industries).

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Engine rotable durability deficit. Widespread grounding of modern narrowbody aircraft awaiting off-wing engine shop visits.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost. The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.
