Earnings disclosures confirm that between 65 and 75 aircraft were grounded (AOG) due to Pratt & Whitney PW1100G-JM engine inspection directives. To protect domestic flight schedules and high aircraft utilization, IndiGo executed damp-lease contracts for Boeing 777-300ER widebody aircraft from Turkish Airlines and ACMI leases for Airbus A320s from SmartLynx. Management confirmed receiving quarterly contract compensation from Pratt & Whitney/RTX to offset grounding and wet-lease costs.
    • High fleet utilization. Average aircraft daily utilization reached 13.8 block hours per operating aircraft day, supported by quick 30-to-35 minute turnaround protocols across Indian metro airports.

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Stage-length dilution distorting unit cost benchmarking. Short-haul carrier appears structurally uncompetitive against long-haul peers on unadjusted Cost per Available Seat Mile (CASM).

Schedule block padding masking gate and taxi dwell. Reported on-time arrival rates (A14) improve while daily aircraft utilization (block hours per aircraft day) declines.

Unbundled ancillary fee friction under regulatory disclosure rules. Decline in ancillary fee conversion rates at booking; customer churn on unbundled fee structures.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.
