How it is built

Landstar operates an asset-light freight transportation model: - Business Capacity Owners (BCOs): Independent contractors who provide truck capacity under exclusive lease (represented 42.4% of purchased transportation in 2025). - Approved third-party carriers: Non-exclusive brokers/carriers providing additional capacity (50.8% of purchased transportation in 2025).

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility. Net revenue per load compresses as spot rates rise, and does not recover while the rate move persists.

Quote-to-book manual handoff. Loads per rep per day flat or falling while volume grows.

Avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty. Cash conversion cycle extends 10 to 30 days beyond the structural float baseline (+15 to +25 days). Carrier quick-pay discounts, factoring fees, or credit line interest consume 15% to 40% of net operating income.

Carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity. Approved third-party carrier pools shrink, vetting standards tighten, and access to the cheapest available capacity is deliberately foreclosed — observed across five companies inside three quarters, following a single legal event.

Fragmented carrier master after acquisition. Onboarding cycle time grows after an acquisition closes; duplicate carrier records; compliance exceptions rise.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.
