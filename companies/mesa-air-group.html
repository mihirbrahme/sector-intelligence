    • Financial performance. Total contract revenue was $498.5m ($462.1m in prior year). Operating loss was $(18.4)m, narrowing from $(45.2)m in FY2023. Contract revenues derived 100% from capacity purchase agreements flying for United Airlines.
    • Pilot attrition signature and fleet parking. Form 10-K MD&A disclosures document the acute operational impact of post-pandemic mainline pilot hiring. Mesa experienced annualized captain attrition exceeding 30% to mainline legacy carriers in 2022–2024. Due to the 1,500-hour FAA first officer rule and simulator upgrade bottlenecks, Mesa was forced to park over 20 aircraft despite available contract flying demand.
    • American Airlines CPA termination. Disclosures confirm that American Airlines initiated early termination of its CPA agreement with Mesa in April 2023, citing inability to meet contracted block hour flying minimums. Mesa completed an emergency fleet migration, reallocating its Bombardier CRJ-900 aircraft to United Airlines.
    • Mainline partner financial rescue. To stabilize Mesa's pilot workforce, United Airlines amended the United Express CPA, increasing block hour reimbursement rates by ~35% to fund new ALPA collective bargaining pilot pay scales matching mainline regional wholly-owned carriers. United also acquired equity warrants and structured debt notes, converting operating losses into equity-backed survival.
    • Controllable completion factor recovery. Controllable completion factor rebounded from 92.4% during the peak captain shortage to 99.1% in FY2025 as the pilot pipeline stabilized.

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Crew legality cascade under irregular operations. Flight cancellations multiply non-linearly on the second and third days of an irregular operations (IROPS) weather event, continuing after weather has cleared.

Regional carrier captain attrition drain. Regional carrier parks 10–25% of operating regional jets despite strong mainline partner contract demand.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.
