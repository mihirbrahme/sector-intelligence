How it is built

  • Four acquired fleets have been collapsed toward one. Smith Transport was acquired 2022-05-31; Contract Freighters, Inc. (CFI) was acquired in August 2022. Effective 2025-12-31 the company "integrated and rebranded U.S. operations of Contract Freighters, Inc. ('CFI') into Heartland Express." Corporate and tractor rebranding completed during Q1 2026.
  • Millis Transfer continues as a named operating brand. CFI remains the brand for cross-border Mexico freight.
  • Length of haul is short. Approximately 77% of FY2025 loads ran under 500 miles.
  • Customer concentration is material and rising. The 25, 10 and 5 largest customers were approximately 63%, 47% and 32% of FY2025 operating revenue, against 60%, 40% and 26% in FY2024. One customer exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue.
  • Asset disposal is a continuing source of reported operating income, not an isolated event. Three terminals were sold into sale-leasebacks in November 2023 for an expected pre-tax gain of roughly $25m; FY2025 recorded $23.4m of gains on equipment and real estate; H1 2026 recorded $32.4m and Q3 2026 recorded $7.8m of gains on disposal of property and equipment ($40.2m across 9M 2026). See disposal gains inside operating income flatter the operating ratio.
  • Debt reduction is the stated financial objective. Acquisition-related debt and finance leases have fallen from $494.1m in 2022 to $99.9m at 2026-09-30. Smith Transport's acquisition debt and finance leases were fully retired as at 2026-03-31. Management targets a debt-free balance sheet in 2027 and an operating ratio of 85.0% or lower.
Network and fleet
  • Twenty-five terminal facilities in the contiguous US, one in Mexico, plus the North Liberty terminal and head office (FY2025 10-K).
  • Average tractor age 2.3 years and average trailer age 7.1 years at 2026-09-30, against 2.6 and 7.5 years a year earlier.
  • Tractor and trailer unit counts are not disclosed in any source held. Fleet age is given; fleet size is not.

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Contract vintage margin decay. Blended margin declines while new-business margin holds.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost. The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.
