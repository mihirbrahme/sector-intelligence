Movements

  1. 2025-06-22

    Full-year audited IFRS results, ancillary density, Boeing delivery delays, unit cost leadership

    from EDGAR, accession 0001038683-25-000021.

    • Financial results. Total operating revenue reached EUR 13,948.5m ($15,100m equivalent), up 25% YoY. Operating profit was EUR 2,058m (Operating margin 14.75%). Net profit after tax was EUR 1,920m.
    • Passenger volume and load factor. Traffic grew 9% YoY to 183.7 million booked passengers. Consolidated passenger load factor was 94.0%. Average booked passenger fare was EUR 49.80 (+21% YoY).
    • Ancillary revenue generation. Ancillary revenue totaled EUR 4,300m, representing 31.9% of total revenue. Average ancillary spend per passenger reached EUR 23.30, driven by reserved seating, priority boarding, and carry-on bag fees.
    • Unit cost leadership. Non-fuel unit costs (CASK ex-fuel) remained flat at EUR 0.0275 per seat-kilometer (~3.00 US cents/ASM), representing the lowest unit cost structure in the European airline industry. Total fuel expense was EUR 5,140m, with 75% hedged forward.
    • Distribution channel control. 20-F disclosures confirm over 99% of ticket volume originates through proprietary digital channels (Ryanair.com and mobile app). Management notes litigation against online travel agency (OTA) screen-scraping to protect direct relationship and eliminate third-party commission overhead.
    • Boeing aircraft delivery constraints. Management reports that Boeing manufacturing bottlenecks and FAA quality audits delayed delivery of 20+ Boeing 737-8200 aircraft scheduled for peak summer operations, forcing capacity trimming from 205m to 198m passenger targets.

What to look for

Failures that recur in this model and would show in this operator's numbers before they show anywhere else.

Stage-length dilution distorting unit cost benchmarking. Short-haul carrier appears structurally uncompetitive against long-haul peers on unadjusted Cost per Available Seat Mile (CASM).

Schedule block padding masking gate and taxi dwell. Reported on-time arrival rates (A14) improve while daily aircraft utilization (block hours per aircraft day) declines.

Unbundled ancillary fee friction under regulatory disclosure rules. Decline in ancillary fee conversion rates at booking; customer churn on unbundled fee structures.

Billing exception leakage. DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms.

Post-restructuring capability gap. Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Purchased-transportation accrual failure. Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.
