In 90 seconds

The business buys rail linehaul in bulk under multi-year contracts with the Class I railroads, owns or leases the container and chassis fleet, and arranges drayage at both ends. Sells door-to-door or ramp-to-ramp service to shippers and keeps the difference between the price sold and the rail, dray and equipment cost of the move. The unit of work is the intermodal load — one container moved end to end, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on net revenue — gross revenue less rail linehaul and purchased drayage. Gross revenue moves with rail rates and fuel surcharge pass-through and is not a performance read, for the same reason it is not one in brokerage. But net revenue alone is not sufficient here. The container, chassis and drayage fleet is owned capital, so a margin taken on net revenue says nothing about whether the capital earns its cost. Read the two together: margin on net revenue for execution, return on capital employed for the equipment decision. The cost base is rail linehaul first and largest (approx 50–65% of gross revenue), then drayage (approx 18–28%, depending on owned versus purchased fleet), equipment depreciation and chassis lease/maintenance (approx 4–8%), terminal and repositioning. Personnel is modest (approx 5–9% of revenue).

The valid peer set is J.B. Hunt (JBI segment: ~$6.0B revenue, ~115k 53-ft containers, ~5,900 dray tractors, 7.5% operating margin in FY2025), Schneider (Intermodal segment: ~$1.15B revenue, ~28k containers, ~1,500 tractors, ~4.5% margin), hub group (Intermodal segment: ~$2.5B–$3.0B segment revenue). Compare segment to segment, never to a parent consolidated figure. It is never compared to truckload carriers on operating ratio, or to brokers on net revenue margin. The first has no purchased rail linehaul, the second has no owned equipment fleet.

The common misreadTreating it as brokerage because the linehaul is purchased. The box and chassis fleet is owned and sized to peak directional flow, so capacity cannot be handed back when volume falls. A broker's cost base flexes with volume; this one does not.

The operating model

The model divides into 5 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little. It operates across Australia, Malaysia and Thailand.

  • Primary margin metric: operating income ÷ net revenue, paired with return on capital employed. In practice, net revenue is never disclosed across major US public peers. J.B. Hunt (JBI), Schneider (Intermodal), and Hub Group, Inc. all report gross segment revenue and operating income; none publishes a net revenue line. Segment operating income ÷ segment revenue (segment operating margin) is therefore the only available public measure: typically 6–10% in balanced freight markets, dropping to 3–5% during freight downturns.

  • Key productivity metrics: container turns per year (loads per box per year), dray cost per load, dray miles per load, street and ramp dwell days, empty repositioning as a share of total moves, chassis cost per load, revenue per load.

Financial signature

  • Capex moderate and bimodal, determined by drayage fleet ownership: approx 2–4% of revenue where drayage is mostly purchased/owner-operator, rising to 6–10% where drayage power is company-owned. A 53-foot domestic container has a 12–15 year useful life with minimal maintenance capex. In contrast, an owned dray tractor fleet (e.g. J.B. Hunt's ~5,900 tractors) forces a 4–6 year replacement cycle, embedding Model 4 asset-carriage depreciation into the cost structure.
  • Asset turnover sits between non-asset brokers and truckload carriers: approx 1.5–2.5x gross revenue over total segment assets. Balance sheet intensity is driven by the container/chassis fleet carried at cost, tractors, and working capital.
  • Working capital positive and funded on gross revenue. DSO approx 40–50 days on shippers against rail and dray settlement on shorter terms (15–30 days), giving a cash conversion cycle of roughly +15 to +30 days. Funding requirements scale on gross freight spend.
  • The rail contract behaves partly like a fixed cost, creating operating leverage. Class I rail agreements (e.g. BNSF with J.B. Hunt, Union Pacific with Schneider) incorporate contractual minimum volume commitments (MVCs) or volume-tiered rate schedules. Evidence from Surface Transportation Board dockets (STB EP 770 Urgent Issues in Freight Rail Service and STB Ex Parte 711) demonstrates that volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers or penalty charges. When volume drops, purchased transportation does not flex downward proportionately, driving severe contractual margin compression.
  • Directional network imbalance generates unbilled repositioning cash drain. Freight corridors (e.g. Los Angeles to Chicago vs. Midwest to West Coast) suffer persistent backhaul imbalances. Empty repositioning costs must be borne by the intermodal carrier without offsetting shipper revenue, depressing container turns from a target 20–22 turns/year down to 14–16 turns/year in slack markets.

Ranges are verified against SEC 10-K segment filings (J.B. Hunt, Schneider, Hub Group) and STB regulatory proceedings.

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

Container Turns per Year

Definition

Total intermodal loads handled ÷ average active domestic container fleet size over a 12-month period.

Why it matters

The master capital efficiency metric for intermodal box fleets. It measures how many times per year an expensive capital asset generates revenue.

Moves up

Fast rail ramp turnarounds; rapid consignee unloading and gate-out; balanced bi-directional corridor demand; scheduled appointment compliance.

Moves down

Rail ramp congestion and terminal dwell; shipper and consignee detention using containers as temporary storage; corridor volume imbalances forcing empty staging.

Where to find it

Calculate from SEC 10-K disclosures: segment loads ÷ average trailing container count. Label.

Common misread

Assuming low turns always mean operational incompetence. Severe rail linehaul service failures or customer-side detention directly trap containers outside the intermodal operator's control.

Diagnostic question

How many days does an average container spend sitting on the street with a customer versus moving on a train or sitting on a rail ramp?

Dray Cost per Load

Definition

Total drayage expense (origin pickup dray + destination delivery dray) ÷ total intermodal loads moved.

Why it matters

Drayage represents 20–30% of total intermodal operating costs. It is the largest controllable operational variable in an intermodal movement.

Moves up

Driver detention at shipper loading docks or congested rail ramps; long-distance drayage beyond 50–75 mile ramp catchments; reliance on spot third-party dray brokers; chassis split charges.

Moves down

High density of pickups within 30 miles of ramps; company-owned dray tractor fleets with high driver retention; drop-and-hook customer programs; automated gate systems (AGS).

Where to find it

Rarely disclosed as a line item. Estimated from purchased transportation notes, tractor fleet headcount, and dray miles per load.

Common misread

Assuming owned drayage is always cheaper than purchased drayage. In unbalanced or seasonal lanes, owned dray fleets suffer catastrophic idle driver and tractor expense.

Diagnostic question

What proportion of your dray moves involve an empty chassis split or wait longer than 90 minutes at the rail ramp gate?

Street and Ramp Dwell

Definition

Elapsed time a domestic container spends either staged inside a railroad intermodal yard (ramp dwell) or held at a customer facility before return (street dwell), measured in days or hours.

Why it matters

Dwell directly caps container velocity and turn rates. Ramp dwell triggers Class I rail storage fees, while street dwell risks unbilled customer equipment absorption.

Moves up

Delayed consignee unloading; missed dray appointments; weekend rail ramp cut-off mismatches; customer warehouse receiving backlogs.

Moves down

Strict demurrage/per diem billing enforcement; pre-cleared appointment scheduling; drop-trailer programs with strict free-time limits.

Where to find it

Rail operational scorecards (STB weekly service metrics) for terminal dwell; carrier internal telematics (solar GPS container tracking).

Common misread

Viewing customer detention charges as profitable revenue. Equipment per diem rarely compensates for the lost revenue of an un-turned box during peak demand.

Diagnostic question

How many containers in your fleet have exceeded 5 days of customer street dwell, and what percentage of that detention was invoiced and collected?

Empty Repositioning Share

Definition

Total empty container miles (or empty railcar moves) ÷ total intermodal container miles (or moves), expressed as a percentage.

Why it matters

Freight networks are structurally directional. Moving an empty container generates rail linehaul and drayage cash cost with zero offsetting revenue.

Moves up

Headhaul/backhaul freight demand imbalance (e.g. Southern California import corridor vs. Midwest consumption); seasonal agricultural demand shifts.

Moves down

Backhaul freight discounts; street-turning import boxes directly into domestic export loads; dynamic pricing balancing lane flows.

Where to find it

Proprietary carrier telematics. Industry norms synthesised from STB freight data and intermodal market surveys.

Common misread

Treating empty repositioning as avoidable waste. Running zero empty miles means refusing high-margin headhaul freight in major origin markets.

Diagnostic question

How much margin does your highest-yielding headhaul lane surrender to the cost of deadheading the empty box back?

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost

Symptom

The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Visible from outside

Financial signature

The gap between the two is the mechanism. 34.2% of direct cargo expense and 12.5% of Ocean Transportation revenue. Terminal services cost $341.9m (2025), $320.9m (2024), $297.2m (2023): +15% over three years across which total container volume fell. This is the same structure on the capital line rather than the operating line, and thinner evidence than the Recorded as a second observation, not a second proof. - Already recorded for model 7 in operating models: Class I rail agreements with contractual minimum volume commitments, where volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers, evidenced through STB EP 770 and Ex Parte 711 dockets.

Root cause

The operator's cost base contains a block priced by a counterparty whose own fixed costs it must cover. When the operator's volume falls, the counterparty's cost per unit rises and is passed through. The operator holds an equity interest or a long contract, so it cannot re-tender. In the joint-venture case it also books its share of the counterparty's earnings inside its own cost line. The same relationship therefore flatters the income statement while inflating the cost per unit.

Falsified if

The purchased input's unit cost falls with the operator's volume, or the operator demonstrates it re-tendered the work, or the cost rise is explained by scope rather than by the counterparty's own cost base.

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Purchased-transportation accrual failure

Symptom

Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Visible from outside

  • Consequence evidence, not mechanism evidence: the Nasdaq listing deficiency notice (2026-08-20, Listing (c)(1)); securities litigation filings; and the Deutsche Bank impact estimate. These show that a failure occurred, that reports were delayed, and that the market repriced the company. They do not evidence the accrual-to-invoice locus.
  • Management remediation and supporting material: the disclosed Ernst & Young re-audit engagement, carrier settlement, freight payment audit and technical accounting roles in postings.

Financial signature

Reported operating margin overstated while cash is unaffected — the tell that separates this from a revenue or collections failure. Hub Group, Inc. understated both lines by approximately $77 million across the first three quarters of 2025, and management confirmed no impact on cash or operating cash flow in any period. The consequential costs are the larger part of the signature and are externally observable: delayed 10-K, two missed 10-Qs, shares −18% (2026-02-06) and −9.2% (2026-05-12), CFO and COO departures (2026-05-28), a securities litigation investigation, and a Nasdaq listing deficiency. See evidence of consequences does not evidence mechanism cause and accrual release inverts cost-ratio trend.

Root cause

Hub Group, Inc. 2026-Q1 disclosed that transactions were "recognized prematurely, booked incorrectly, or lacked adequate supporting documentation" in the historical ledgers, with an expected conclusion of ineffective disclosure controls and ineffective ICFR for FY2023 and FY2024. In operational terms this sits on the accrual-to-invoice reconciliation for purchased transportation: the accrued cost of a move and the carrier's eventual invoice are reconciled at period end, and the reconciliation was carrying an unsupported reduction to accounts payable. The company has not stated it that way. The mapping from "lacked adequate supporting documentation" to the PT accrual process is our inference and carries no better than.

Operational systems where this surfaces

TriumphPay, HubTran, Cass Information Systems, McLeod LoadMaster/PowerBroker, Trimble TMW

Falsified if

The understatement is confined to a single acquired entity or a single period and does not widen backwards, which points to a conversion or purchase-accounting error rather than a standing reconciliation failure; or the restatement also moves cash or operating cash flow, which makes it a different failure; or the disclosed cause names revenue recognition rather than expense accrual.

Remediation

Automated three-way match between tender, accrual and carrier invoice; exception queues on unsupported accrual adjustments; continuous reconciliation rather than period-end reconciliation.

Diagnostic question

"When a carrier's invoice arrives, what does it get matched against, and who clears the difference?"

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation

Symptom

Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Visible from outside

The disclosed lag and covered share are grade ` from the 10-K; the analyst exchanges are gradeC`. Realised rates tracking the input series with a lag is the mechanism. Realised rates tracking the freight rate index instead is the cycle. That test runs on public data and has not been run.

Financial signature

Operating margin compresses in a period of rising input prices while volume and utilisation are stable, and recovers with a lag rather than with volume. The tell is the pairing of a disclosed lag with a disclosed uncovered share. escalation clauses, or the customer pays for the fuel," with "generally a 30 to 120 day delay before contracts are adjusted"; spot contracts carry no fuel escalator and spot was 30% of inland marine revenue. sell-side analysts across the 2026 Q2 and Q3 cycle and answered qualitatively both times, with management redirecting to a market-driven rather than fuel-driven pricing environment.

Root cause

The escalator is a contractual formula computed and invoiced on a cycle, not a price the commercial team sets. Recovery fails in three distinct places. The formula is indexed to a series that moves differently from the actual input. The cycle resets after the cost lands. The billing recalculates and rebills by hand. Only the third is addressable operationally, and no operator located discloses which of the three binds.

Operational systems where this surfaces

SAP TM, Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

The operator discloses realised escalator capture at or near 100% across a full inflation cycle, or the uncovered revenue share is immaterial, or realised rate movements track a freight rate index rather than the input series.
