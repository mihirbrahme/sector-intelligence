In 90 seconds

The business operates US-flagged, US-built, US-crewed vessels on fixed day-of-week schedules between the mainland and the non-contiguous states and territories, where cabotage law bars foreign-flag competition. Sells containerised capacity priced on service frequency and schedule integrity rather than on spot rate. The unit of work is the container lifted, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on total revenue. Segment operating margin is the available measure. Return on capital cannot be computed by segment where, as at matson, the filing states that segment balance sheet information is not provided to the chief operating decision maker. Say so rather than substituting a group figure for a segment one. The cost base is direct cargo expense first — terminal handling, stevedoring, wharfage and outside purchased transportation, 36.5% of Ocean revenue at matson FY2025 — then vessel operating expense (21.7%), operating overhead (12.0%), depreciation (5.6%) and general and administrative (8.6%).

The valid peer set is the protected trades are duopolies with one listed participant. Hawaii — Pasha, Aloha Marine Lines. Alaska — Totem Ocean Trailer Express, Alaska Marine Lines, Samson Tug & Barge. All private. There is no valid public margin peer for a Jones Act non-contiguous carrier, and international liner operators (Maersk, ONE, ZIM) are not one: different cabotage regime, different crewing cost, different lane economics. Where a benchmark provider's marine class contains eight firms and the subject is plausibly one of them, that is a comparison with itself — drop it rather than caveat it, per.

The common misreadTreating an exceptional rate-cycle year as the baseline. matson earned a 32.5% EBITDA margin in FY2021-22 on Transpacific rate dislocation and 19.3% in FY2025. Anchoring on the peak turns a return to normal into an operating failure — apply the cyclicality lens in value tree patterns before diagnosing anything.

The operating model

The model divides into 4 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little. It operates across Indonesia and Malaysia.

  • Primary margin metric: segment operating income / segment revenue, and cost per FEU by cost line. The second carries the diagnosis.

Labour capacity is billets, and billets do not flex

A vessel cannot sail without its crew positions filled. Each position is filled by more than one person, because seagoing staff rotate between sea duty and time ashore. Matson, Inc. FY2025: an active fleet requiring 329 billets, each "typically filled by two or more employees", inside a workforce of 4,170 of whom 2,843 — 68% — are under collective agreements.

This is why a hiring freeze is not a cost reduction in this model. Seagoing headcount is an output of the sailing schedule and the rotation multiplier, not a management discretion. A freeze reaches only the shoreside remainder, and changes what a task costs not at all. See billets make seagoing headcount a schedule output, not a cost lever.

Financial signature

  • Capex lumpy, contractually committed, and decoupled from demand. Newbuild programmes are signed years ahead against milestone schedules that do not flex with volume. Matson, Inc. FY2025: $244.3m of new vessel construction, $149.1m of other capital expenditure and $49.4m of dry-docking payments, against FY2026 guidance of approximately $425m, $150-170m and $45m respectively, on a fleet renewal programme of approximately $1.0bn. Where management states the new tonnage carries no unit-cost advantage over the existing fleet, the return on that capital has to come from utilisation — a denominator question, not a cost one.
  • A tax-deferred construction fund sits between cash and capital, and it is restricted. The Capital Construction Fund is a statutory vehicle whose balance can be applied only to qualified vessel construction without triggering tax. Matson, Inc. held $532.7m at FY2025, down from $642.6m. The movement: $237.3m of milestone payments out, against $100.7m deposited for the repurchase of assigned accounts receivable. Do not read a CCF balance as distributable cash. The receivable mechanism also entangles it with working capital rather than holding it separate.
  • Working capital near zero or negative; the liabilities carry the story. Receivables run approximately 25-30 days, there is no inventory, and the cash cycle can be negative. The distinguishing off-balance-sheet items are multiemployer pension withdrawal liability and deferred dry-docking costs. Matson, Inc. recognises $41.9m long-term plus $4.1m short-term of the first, and states its contingent share on a full withdrawal "may be material". Deferred dry-docking costs were $94.7m net.
  • Dry-docking is a regulated, deferrable, and therefore deferral-prone cost. Ocean-going vessels are dry-docked twice in every five years and the expenditure is capitalised and amortised between dockings. That accounting makes it the easiest cost in the model to move between years and among the hardest to remove: Matson, Inc. cut maintenance capital guidance by $20m in 2025 while cash dry-docking payments rose from $30.2m to $49.4m in the same year.
  • Concentration is low downstream and high upstream. Matson, Inc. FY2025: top ten Ocean customers approximately 19% of segment revenue, against a single 35%-owned, third-party-operated terminal joint venture at 34.2% of direct cargo expense and 12.5% of Ocean revenue. The economically significant counterparty is a supplier, not a customer.

Figures cited to Matson, Inc. FY2025 Form 10-K (filed 2026-02-27), grade ``. Derived per-FEU figures marked. One company's disclosure is the only clean public source for this model; that is stated rather than presented as a distribution.

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Revenue per FEU

Definition

Segment revenue divided by containers carried, in forty-foot equivalent units.

Why it matters

The yield measure for model 9, and the input to every cost-per-unit-of-work comparison.

Moves up

Lane mix shifting toward premium services; rate increases; higher accessorial and demurrage capture.

Moves down

Mix shifting toward lower-yield protected lanes; rate competition; fuel surcharge deflation.

Where to find it

Compute from the disclosed FEU volume table and segment revenue. Neither is published as a rate.

Common misread

Treating group revenue per FEU as a price. It is a mix index across lanes with very different economics.

Diagnostic question

"What is the yield on the lane that lost volume, against the lane that gained it?"

Liner Schedule Reliability

Definition

Share of scheduled vessel arrivals that arrive within the measurement provider's on-time window.

Why it matters

Scheduled liner shipping sells a weekly service promise. When reliability falls, cost moves through extra ships, skipped calls, terminal costs and customer service failures.

Moves up

Stable rotations, berth-window discipline, fewer port omissions, lower congestion and better recovery planning.

Moves down

Port congestion, vessel bunching, weather, blank sailings, terminal labour disruption and route diversions.

Where to find it

Sea-Intelligence Global Liner Performance, Drewry schedule reliability products and carrier service reports. Public releases are usually global or carrier-level, not lane-specific enough for diagnosis.

Common misread

Treating low schedule reliability as an operator failure. Use trade-lane peer performance before asserting port congestion breaks liner rotation integrity.

Diagnostic question

"Which string missed the window against the trade's peer reliability, and what cost did the recovery add?"

Billets and the Rotation Multiplier

Definition

A billet is a crew position on an active vessel. Each billet is filled by two or more employees, because seagoing personnel rotate between sea duty and time ashore.

Why it matters

It converts a sailing schedule into a headcount floor. Seagoing employment is an output of how many vessels sail on what rotation, not a discretionary cost line, so ordinary headcount levers do not reach it.

Moves up

More active vessels; longer strings; rotation patterns with more time ashore per sea day.

Moves down

Vessels laid up or moved to reserve; chartered-in foreign-flag tonnage, where the vessel owner carries the crew.

Where to find it

The human capital section of the 10-K. Matson, Inc. discloses the billet count directly; most operators do not.

Common misread

Reading a hiring freeze or a headcount reduction as a reduction in the cost of doing the work. It reaches the shoreside remainder only, and it changes when a cost lands rather than what it costs. See billets make seagoing headcount a schedule output, not a cost lever.

Diagnostic question

"How many of your people are billet-driven, and what is the rotation multiplier on each pattern?"

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

Direct Cargo Expense per FEU

Definition

Terminal handling, stevedoring, wharfage and outside purchased transportation, divided by containers carried.

Why it matters

The largest cost line in model 9 and, at Matson, Inc., the only one whose unit cost moved. It is where terminal and longshore cost inflation lands, and it is largely outside the carrier's operational control where terminals are run by a joint venture or a third party.

Moves up

Terminal operator cost inflation; longshore wage settlements; lower lift volumes over a fixed terminal cost base; congestion.

Moves down

Lift volume growth; terminal productivity; renegotiated stevedoring terms.

Where to find it

The segment cost table plus the FEU volume table. Where the terminal operator is a related party, the specific charge is disclosed separately in the related-party note.

Common misread

Reading the aggregate direct cargo line as controllable operating cost. Split the related-party terminal charge out before drawing any conclusion. At Matson, Inc. it was 34.2% of the line, and carried all of the deterioration.

Diagnostic question

"What did your cost per lift do, and how much of that is the terminal operator's cost base rather than your volume?"

Deferred Dry-docking Payments

Definition

Cash paid for regulatory dry-docking of ocean-going vessels, capitalised and amortised over the period to the next docking — generally 30 or 60 months. Regulation requires dry-docking twice in every five-year period.

Why it matters

It is the most deferrable cost in model 9 and therefore the one most likely to be presented as a saving when it has only been moved. The accounting separates cash paid from expense recognised, so the two lines can move in opposite directions in the same year.

Moves up

Dockings falling due; shipyard rate inflation; deferred work arriving; older tonnage requiring more extensive scope.

Moves down

Deferral, which is temporary by construction.

Where to find it

The cash flow statement and the liquidity discussion for payments; the balance sheet for deferred dry-docking costs, net; the D&A guidance for the amortisation component.

Common misread

Treating the guidance cut as the cost outcome. Read the cash line, then the deferred asset balance. A falling guidance number against a rising cash number and a rising deferred balance is deferral, not reduction.

Diagnostic question

"How many dockings are past their planned window, and what does the yard quote now against what it quoted then?"

Revenue per TEU

Definition

Liner revenue divided by loaded containers carried, in TEU.

Why it matters

It is the yield measure for international and regional container carriers. It must be read with lane mix and slot utilisation.

Moves up

Rate increases, premium cargo mix, higher reefer or special cargo share, and stronger headhaul demand.

Moves down

Spot-rate decline, mix toward feeder relay cargo, weak backhaul pricing, and empty repositioning.

Where to find it

Compute from freight income and liftings where both are disclosed. Some carriers disclose average freight rate per TEU directly.

Common misread

Treating revenue per TEU as price. It is a lane and cargo mix index.

Diagnostic question

"Which lane added TEU, and did its yield sit above or below the network average?"

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation

Symptom

Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Visible from outside

The disclosed lag and covered share are grade ` from the 10-K; the analyst exchanges are gradeC`. Realised rates tracking the input series with a lag is the mechanism. Realised rates tracking the freight rate index instead is the cycle. That test runs on public data and has not been run.

Financial signature

Operating margin compresses in a period of rising input prices while volume and utilisation are stable, and recovers with a lag rather than with volume. The tell is the pairing of a disclosed lag with a disclosed uncovered share. escalation clauses, or the customer pays for the fuel," with "generally a 30 to 120 day delay before contracts are adjusted"; spot contracts carry no fuel escalator and spot was 30% of inland marine revenue. The filing additionally lists the conditions under which the clauses under-recover: "specific terms of the fuel escalation formulas, fuel price volatility, navigating conditions, tow sizes, trip routing, and the location of loading

  • Matson, Inc.: the size of the fuel-cost recovery lag was asked twice by sell-side analysts across the 2026 Q2 and Q3 cycle and answered qualitatively both times, with management redirecting to a market-driven rather than fuel-driven pricing environment.

Root cause

The escalator is a contractual formula computed and invoiced on a cycle, not a price the commercial team sets. Recovery fails in three distinct places. The formula is indexed to a series that moves differently from the actual input. The cycle resets after the cost lands. The billing recalculates and rebills by hand. Only the third is addressable operationally, and no operator located discloses which of the three binds.

Operational systems where this surfaces

SAP TM, Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

The operator discloses realised escalator capture at or near 100% across a full inflation cycle, or the uncovered revenue share is immaterial, or realised rate movements track a freight rate index rather than the input series.

Dry-docking bunching removes revenue days

Symptom

Several scheduled dockings fall into the same period, reducing revenue days and utilisation before visible repair cost rises.

What it looks like from outside

Off-hire, dry-docking payments or deferred dry-docking assets rise. Revenue days fall in the affected fleet while market volume indices do not explain the fall.

Financial signature

Revenue days, shipping days or available days fall while owned fleet count is stable. Maintenance capex and deferred dry-docking assets rise. days from five planned dry-dockings against two in 2024. Matson, Inc. FY2025 cash dry-docking payments rose to $49.4m from $30.2m.

Cycle discriminator

A freight cycle changes rates and demand. This mechanism changes the denominator. It is usable only when revenue-day or off-hire loss exceeds the demand change implied by sector volume indices.

Root cause

Class, regulatory and yard windows were allowed to cluster. Deferral makes the later period look like poor utilisation even when demand is unchanged.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Danaos Management Consultants, ABS Nautical Systems

What operators do about it

Stagger dockings, reserve yard slots earlier, bundle repair scope, charter substitute tonnage, or delay non-critical work.

Falsified if

Utilisation falls with the market while docking days, off-hire days and dry-docking cash do not rise.

Fleet age capex cliff raises maintenance before replacement is funded

Symptom

Ageing vessels show rising maintenance, dry-docking scope, off-hire and class-compliance capex before newbuild cash flow is ready.

What it looks like from outside

Fleet age rises; maintenance and docking cash rise; vessel sales remove older units; newbuild commitments appear after a period of high utilisation or regulatory pressure.

Financial signature

Repair cost, off-hire days and dry-docking cash rise while depreciation remains based on old book value. Capex later jumps when replacement is ordered. constrained US inland tank barge fleet. Regional Container Lines ordered two 14,000 TEU vessels for 2028 after heavy fleet capex years.

Cycle discriminator

A cycle changes asset values and rates. The mechanism requires age, maintenance, off-hire or replacement-capex evidence moving against the company's own fleet history, not only lower vessel values.

Root cause

Replacement decisions were deferred while the old fleet still earned acceptable cash returns. Book value understated replacement cost, so reported returns looked better than economic returns.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Danaos Software, ABS Nautical Systems, Kalmar Insight

What operators do about it

Staged renewal, sale of older ships, life extension work, charter-in cover, speed limitation, or scrapping.

Falsified if

Fleet age is stable or falling, maintenance per vessel-day is stable, and capex is ordinary replacement rather than catch-up.

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Purchased-transportation accrual failure

Symptom

Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Visible from outside

  • Consequence evidence, not mechanism evidence: the Nasdaq listing deficiency notice (2026-08-20, Listing (c)(1)); securities litigation filings; and the Deutsche Bank impact estimate. These show that a failure occurred, that reports were delayed, and that the market repriced the company. They do not evidence the accrual-to-invoice locus.
  • Management remediation and supporting material: the disclosed Ernst & Young re-audit engagement, carrier settlement, freight payment audit and technical accounting roles in postings.

Financial signature

Reported operating margin overstated while cash is unaffected — the tell that separates this from a revenue or collections failure. approximately $77 million across the first three quarters of 2025, and management confirmed no impact on cash or operating cash flow in any period. The consequential costs are the larger part of the signature and are externally observable: delayed 10-K, two missed 10-Qs, shares −18% (2026-02-06) and −9.2% (2026-05-12), CFO and COO departures (2026-05-28), a securities litigation investigation, and a Nasdaq listing deficiency. See evidence of consequences does not evidence mechanism cause and accrual release inverts cost-ratio trend.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost

Symptom

The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Visible from outside

Financial signature

Recorded as a second observation, not a second proof. - Already recorded for model 7 in operating models: Class I rail agreements with contractual minimum volume commitments, where volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers, evidenced through STB EP 770 and Ex Parte 711 dockets.

Root cause

The operator's cost base contains a block priced by a counterparty whose own fixed costs it must cover. When the operator's volume falls, the counterparty's cost per unit rises and is passed through. The operator holds an equity interest or a long contract, so it cannot re-tender. In the joint-venture case it also books its share of the counterparty's earnings inside its own cost line. The same relationship therefore flatters the income statement while inflating the cost per unit.

Falsified if

The purchased input's unit cost falls with the operator's volume, or the operator demonstrates it re-tendered the work, or the cost rise is explained by scope rather than by the counterparty's own cost base.

Charter-in rate exposure locked at the wrong point in the cycle

Symptom

Charter hire per revenue day, TEU or tonne rises after market freight rates turn down. The operator carries high-cost chartered tonnage into a weaker revenue market.

What it looks like from outside

Charter hire grows faster than liftings, tonnes, revenue days or TCE. Lease liabilities rise while owned fleet is flat. Management describes capacity as flexibility while the P&L shows fixed hire.

Financial signature

Margin compresses while volume or revenue days hold up. The cost line is charter hire or lease expense, not owned-vessel depreciation. owned fleet plus chartered-in tonnage used to meet cargo and COA commitments. The release also names charter-rate and vessel-value fluctuation as risks.

Cycle discriminator

Compare charter hire per capacity day with the market freight index. A simple cycle effect shows both revenue and charter hire moving together. The mechanism is present only when the hire cost remains fixed or rises after revenue yield falls.

Root cause

The operator fixed capacity before the freight market turned. A liner did it to protect service frequency. A dry bulk operator did it to cover cargo commitments. An energy carrier did it to bridge fleet or project timing.

What operators do about it

Shorten charter tenor, layer charter maturities, sublet excess tonnage, relet cargo, blank sailings in liner trades, or accept lower utilisation to protect cash.

Falsified if

Charter-in cost per capacity day falls with the market, or the hire increase is fully explained by more operated days or larger ships.

Forensic craft insights

Specific observations on how this model behaves in practice, the preconditions for automation, and operational failure modes.

Billets make seagoing headcount a schedule output, not a cost lever — In vessel operations, crew headcount is set by the sailing schedule and the rotation pattern, not by management discretion.

Cost ratios and cost per unit of work move in opposite directions — A cost line rising as a percentage of revenue tells you nothing about whether the work got more expensive.

Depreciated book flatters return on capital in long-lived marine fleets — For fleets of 25-to-40-year assets built over decades, reported return on capital sits on a denominator well below replacement cost.

Equity income from an affiliate inside cost of sales distorts segment margin — Some companies book their share of a joint venture's result inside segment costs rather than below operating income.

Time charters make delay a cost event, not a revenue loss — In marine transport the contract form decides what a lost day of availability is worth.
