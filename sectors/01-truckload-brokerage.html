In 90 seconds

The business buys capacity from carriers, sells it to shippers, keeps the spread. Owns no tractors or trailers. The unit of work is the load, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on net revenue (also called adjusted gross profit) = gross revenue less purchased transportation. Gross revenue moves with market linehaul rates and fuel surcharge pass-through, neither of which reflects company performance. The cost base is predominantly personnel. SG&A as % of net revenue is the efficiency measure.

The valid peer set is RXO, Landstar (brokerage), Hub Group (brokerage segment), C.H. Robinson (NAST segment), TQL, Echo, Coyote. It is never compared to asset carriers, on any margin metric.

The common misreadCiting gross revenue growth as a performance signal when it is a rate-cycle artifact.

The operating model

The model divides into 6 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little. It operates across Australia and Southeast Asia.

  • Primary margin metric: operating income ÷ net revenue. C.H. Robinson discloses this as "adjusted operating margin" in its own 10-K.

  • Key productivity metrics: loads per rep per day, net revenue per load, net revenue per employee, headcount per thousand loads.

Sub-sectors

The model holds, but the economics inside it are not uniform. Each sub-sector changes what the work costs and which numbers move.

Flatbed and specialised brokerage Equipment type, permits, tarping, securement and loading windows reduce interchangeability. Capacity search is more manual. Tanker and bulk brokerage Product compatibility, washouts, hazmat qualification and contamination risk matter more than linehaul rate. Financial signature

  • Working capital intensity: high, and it scales with the wrong number. Carriers are paid on short terms (quick-pay programmes commonly 2-15 days, standard terms approx 15-30 days) while shippers pay on approx 40-50 day terms. DSO approx 40-50 days against DPO approx 20-30 days gives a cash conversion cycle of roughly +15 to +25 days. The funding requirement scales with gross revenue; the earnings that fund it scale with net revenue. A brokerage growing loads into a rising rate market consumes cash.
  • Capex negligible: approx 0.5-1.5% of net revenue (a rounding error against gross revenue), effectively all IT and capitalised software. Free cash flow tracks operating income closely.
  • Asset turnover high, but the balance sheet is receivables and goodwill. Revenue / total assets typically approx 3-5x on gross revenue; PP&E is a low single-digit % of assets. Consequence: operating cash flow is counter-cyclical to freight rates - a falling market releases working capital, so cash conversion looks best when earnings are worst. Do not read that as operational improvement.

Ranges are approximate directional norms for the operating model, not figures sourced to any company's filings.

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Net Revenue / Adjusted Gross Profit

Definition

Gross revenue less purchased transportation and related services

Why it matters

The only revenue line for a broker that reflects company performance rather than market rates

Moves up

Buying capacity below the price sold; contract mix over spot in a falling market; spot exposure in a rising market

Moves down

Manual load coverage forcing market-rate buys; customer mix shifting to low-margin contract freight

Where to find it

10-K MD&A, usually a named non-GAAP reconciliation table. Often absent from standard XBRL taxonomy — extract from filing text.

Common misread

Comparing across companies with different contract/spot mix

Diagnostic question

When a tender comes in at a rate below what you can buy that lane for today, who makes the cover-or-decline call, and what do they look at before they make it?

Net Revenue per Load

Definition

Net revenue ÷ loads handled

Why it matters

The cleanest single measure of pricing discipline

Moves up

Dynamic pricing; better carrier sourcing; freight mix to harder-to-cover lanes

Moves down

Manual sourcing at market rates; commoditised lane mix

Where to find it

Load counts sometimes disclosed in MD&A or investor decks; otherwise modelled

Common misread

Ignoring lane mix. A falling number may be mix, not execution.

Diagnostic question

Which lanes did your reps earn the most on last quarter, and how did they know to price those lanes that way?

Loads per Rep per Day

Definition

Loads handled ÷ (customer-facing headcount × working days)

Why it matters

The productivity metric brokers manage to internally. Using it signals operational fluency.

Moves up

Automation of quoting, tendering, tracking, and check calls

Moves down

Manual exception handling; fragmented systems post-acquisition

Where to find it

Rarely disclosed. Model from headcount and load counts, label.

Diagnostic question

What does a rep actually do between 7am and 9am, and which of those steps would disappear if your systems talked to each other?

Adjusted Operating Margin

Definition

Operating income ÷ adjusted gross profit

Why it matters

The company's own disclosed efficiency measure. Using it signals you read the filing rather than a screener.

Moves up

Headcount productivity gains; automation of load coverage

Moves down

Personnel cost growth outpacing net revenue; acquisition integration overhead

Common misread

Confusing with GAAP operating margin on gross revenue (4.9% for the same company, same year)

Diagnostic question

How many people touch a single load between the customer tender and the carrier invoice being paid, and what does each of them do to it?

SG&A as % of Net Revenue

Definition

SG&A ÷ net revenue

Why it matters

For an asset-light business this is the cost structure

Moves up

Headcount growth ahead of volume; integration overhead; duplicate systems

Moves down

Automation; consolidation of acquired back offices

Common misread

Computing against gross revenue, which makes every broker look efficient

Diagnostic question

Of the back offices you have acquired, how many are still running their own process on their own system, and what has kept them there?

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility

Symptom

Net revenue per load compresses as spot rates rise, and does not recover while the rate move persists

Root cause

Capacity is sourced load by load against a static carrier list, with the buy decision made at the prevailing market price rather than against a priced view of the lane. Pricing is reactive, so the company absorbs rate moves instead of positioning ahead of them.

Operational systems where this surfaces

McLeod Software (PowerBroker), Trimble Transportation (TMW.Suite), MercuryGate, Revenova

Falsified if

  • Net revenue per load recovers while spot remains elevated; or
  • the compression is fully explained by a disclosed shift in contract/spot mix or customer mix; or
  • the company discloses a tightening of carrier qualification standards or a narrowing of the approved carrier pool over the same period — added from the Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc. case above; a narrowed pool is neither a mix shift nor a customer-mix shift, so the original falsifier list did not catch it.

Remediation

Agentic carrier sourcing; dynamic buy-rate guidance. - What has been tried (failure history): algorithmic dynamic pricing engines and automated load-matching platforms (digital freight brokerage matching algorithms, Convoy, Uber Freight). - Why it failed: algorithms reacted to historical spot rate trends rather than forward capacity shifts; during market inflections, dynamic buy-rate algorithms underpriced shipper commitments and overpaid for spot cover, producing persistent negative gross margins. - Difficulty of remediation: dynamic buy-rate guidance is substantially harder than it looks. Sourcing cannot be automated purely on historical lane data during inflection points; without real-time relationship intelligence and spot negotiation capability, automated buy guidance either overbids (destroying margin) or underbids (causing tender rejections).

Diagnostic question

"When a load needs covering at 4pm, what does the rep actually do first?"

Discriminating question

"Has your approved carrier list got shorter in the last two years, and was that a decision or an outcome?"

Why quote-to-book manual handoff is not the explanation

quote-to-book manual handoff raises the cost of transacting a load and shows up in loads per rep per day and personnel expense per load, leaving net revenue per load unaffected. If the buy price is holding and only the cost to process is rising, this is quote-to-book manual handoff and not manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility. Both can run at once; the two are separated by which denominator moves.

Quote-to-book manual handoff

Symptom

Loads per rep per day flat or falling while volume grows

Financial signature

Personnel expense grows in line with volume rather than sub-linearly; SG&A as % of net revenue does not improve as volume scales

Root cause

Quoting, booking, tendering, and confirmation live in separate systems with no record passing between them, so scale is bought with headcount rather than throughput.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Revenova, McLeod PowerBroker, MercuryGate, Turvo

Falsified if

Loads per head improves as volume grows, or the flat ratio is explained by a disclosed mix shift into more complex freight

Remediation

quote-to-book workflow automation removing the re-keys. - What has been tried (failure history): automated two-sided digital matching (digital freight brokerage matching algorithms), maritime electronic documentation platforms (consortium blockchain trade documentation), and enterprise ERP re-platforming (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: automating standard booking touches saved only 3 to 5 minutes on the happy path, while operational exceptions (missed appointments, equipment failures, detention) required 30 to 45 minutes of manual rep intervention. - Difficulty of remediation: workflow automation removing re-keys is harder than it looks. Removing the initial data re-key leaves 15% to 25% of loads stranded in manual exception loops; unless exception handling itself is automated, personnel expense per load fails to decline with volume.

Diagnostic question

"How many systems does a rep touch between a customer asking for a rate and the load being tendered?"

Why manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility is not the explanation

Manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility moves the price at which capacity is bought and shows up in net revenue per load. quote-to-book manual handoff leaves the buy price alone and moves the cost of processing the load. If net revenue per load is compressing while loads per head holds, the problem is sourcing, not the handoff.

Avoidable working capital float drag and carrier settlement financing penalty

Symptom

Cash conversion cycle extends 10 to 30 days beyond the structural float baseline (+15 to +25 days). Carrier quick-pay discounts, factoring fees, or credit line interest consume 15% to 40% of net operating income.

Financial signature

  1. Direct financing cost drag: QuickPay financing discounts and freight factoring fees cost 1.5% to 2.5% of gross invoice value (evidenced by TriumphPay / Triumph Financial NASDAQ: TFIN Form 10-K, and Denim). Because a broker's net revenue is only 12% to 16% of gross billing, a 2.0% gross financing fee represents 12.5% to 16.7% of net revenue, consuming a dominant share of net operating income.
  2. Rate inflation cash starvation: working capital scales with gross purchased transportation; earnings scale with net revenue spread. When spot rates spike, an intermediary with delayed billing burns cash rapidly to settle carriers while waiting 60+ days for shipper collections.

Root cause

Disconnected workflows between dispatch, POD ingestion, and shipper billing. Invoices sit unbilled while clerks manually chase delivery receipts or resolve accessorial disputes.

Operational systems where this surfaces

TriumphPay, Denim, Cass Information Systems, HubTran, Revenova

Falsified if

The intermediary operates with zero debt or factoring, achieves automated shipper collections under 30 days, or settles carrier payables without incurring quick-pay discounts or credit facility interest.

Diagnostic question

"What is the average elapsed time between a carrier delivering a load and the shipper receiving an audit-ready invoice, and what is your effective annual financing cost on the receivables bridging that gap?"

Carrier-selection liability repricing the cost of cheap capacity

Symptom

Approved third-party carrier pools shrink, vetting standards tighten, and access to the cheapest available capacity is deliberately foreclosed — observed across five companies inside three quarters, following a single legal event.

Financial signature

Three line items move together, and the first of them moves independent of market rates,

  1. Gross margin on brokered freight compresses beyond what the rate move explains. Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc. 2025-Q4 names it directly: Logistics gross margin fell 180bp to 15.5% from "higher purchased transportation costs paid to outside carriers... coupled with Knight-Swift's policy of enforcing rigorous carrier qualification rules that limited access to discounted third-party capacity." See carrier qualification tightening compresses broker margin.
  2. Insurance, claims and self-insured retention rise. Landstar System, Inc. carries a $10 million per-occurrence SIR (2025-Q4); Knight-Swift Transportation Holdings Inc. held $602.8 million in total claims reserves at 2026-06-30; C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc. carries $155 million per occurrence over a $5 million deductible against a $604 million verdict.
  3. Legal defence cost enters SG&A. Landstar System, Inc. SG&A +5.9% to $230,548 thousand in FY2025, attributed in part to "increased legal defense costs."

Root cause

Carrier qualification and continuous safety monitoring is an evidence-assembly process run per carrier per load, and the cheapest available capacity is disproportionately the least-qualified. Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC converted selecting it from a compliance risk into a direct litigation liability; Lipe v. Lupus Superior priced that liability at $604 million on a 23% fault apportionment, reaching vicarious liability through a borrowed-employee finding. The operational failure is not the vetting decision — a tightened pool is a correct response to a repriced liability. The failure is that vetting throughput is the constraint on how much qualified capacity a broker can reach, so the pool shrinks to what the process can clear rather than to what the risk standard requires.

Operational systems where this surfaces

McLeod PowerBroker, Revenova, TriumphPay, Q88

Falsified if

The approved carrier pool is stable or growing while the margin and claims lines move — in which case the compression is manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility or contract vintage margin decay, not this; or the claims and legal-defence movement is fully explained by disclosed prior-year reserve development on accidents predating Montgomery, which is a reserving question and not a selection question; or the margin compression tracks published spot indices closely enough that no rate-independent component remains.

Remediation

Automated carrier qualification and continuous safety monitoring — raising vetting throughput so the qualified pool is bounded by the risk standard rather than by process capacity; entity verification at onboarding; automated compliance and authority tracking against FMCSA records. - What has been tried (failure history): instantaneous digital carrier onboarding in digital freight brokerages (digital freight brokerage matching algorithms) and autonomous Class 8 linehaul trucking (autonomous long-haul trucking commercialisation, TuSimple, Embark). - Why it failed: open digital onboarding opened platforms to identity fraud, cargo theft, and severe negligent selection litigation; autonomous trucking eliminated the driver but transferred all collision liability to the fleet and software vendor, confronting uninsurable nuclear verdict exposure. - Difficulty of remediation: automated qualification is harder than it looks. Fraudulent carriers continuously morph identities across new MC authorities and spoofed telematics; automated vetting rules either admit sophisticated bad actors or reject valid marginal capacity, artificially compressing broker margins.

Diagnostic question

"How many carriers cleared your qualification process last month, and how many did you need to clear?"

Fragmented carrier master after acquisition

Symptom

Onboarding cycle time grows after an acquisition closes; duplicate carrier records; compliance exceptions rise

Financial signature

SG&A per load rises after close and has not reverted by the company's own stated synergy timetable; synergy targets restated, deferred, or dropped from later disclosure. Use the timetable the company published — do not assume a recovery period.

Root cause

Each acquired entity brought its own TMS and carrier master. There is no golden record, so carrier onboarding, compliance checking, and payment run separately per brand and the cost of each is duplicated.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Trimble Transportation (TMW.Suite / TruckMate / Innovative), McLeod Software

Falsified if

Authorities and bonds were consolidated on the company's stated schedule and SG&A per load reverts to the pre-close trend

Remediation

Entity resolution, golden record, single onboarding workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): multi-year enterprise ERP and master data management (MDM) consolidations (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: enterprise software suites treated carrier master consolidation as database deduplication rather than reconciling distinct operating authorities, insurance policies, and settlement terms.

Diagnostic question

"How many carrier masters do you have today?"

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Detention and demurrage under-recovery

Symptom

Charges absorbed rather than contested or passed through, in periods when port and terminal dwell is rising

Financial signature

Gross profit per file falls as dwell rises, and the fall concentrates in the trade lanes and ports where dwell moved

Root cause

The charge is contestable, but contesting it requires assembling appointment, gate, and equipment evidence across systems inside a short window. No single owner holds the window, so claims lapse and the charge is booked as cost.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Navis N4, Tideworks (Mainsail / GateVision), Octopi, Turvo, Veson IMOS, CargoWise, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

Gross profit per file holds through a dwell increase, or the company's docket activity is in line with comparable-volume peers

Remediation

Automated evidence assembly and claim generation inside the dispute window. - What has been tried (failure history): consortium blockchain container milestone logging (consortium blockchain trade documentation, TradeLens) and real-time container visibility platforms (passive visibility control towers). - Why it failed: D&D disputes do not fail from a lack of timestamp tracking; they fail because ocean carriers and terminal operators reject claims lacking official equipment interchange receipts (EIR) and appointment cancellation logs under 46 CFR Part 541. Knowing a container is dwelling does not provide legal leverage to compel fee waivers. - Difficulty of remediation: automated claim generation is harder than it looks. Terminal operating systems actively restrict external API access to gate records and appointment logs; assembling admissible dispute evidence requires cross-system extraction from uncooperative counterparties inside short statutory windows.

Diagnostic question

"Who decides whether a demurrage charge gets contested, and how long do they have?"

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Forensic craft insights

Specific observations on how this model behaves in practice, the preconditions for automation, and operational failure modes.

Adjusted operating margin measures broker efficiency — Adjusted operating margin, defined as operating income divided by adjusted gross profit, is the model-correct efficiency measure for truckload brokerage.

Carrier qualification tightening compresses broker margin — Restricting approved motor carrier capacity to defend against negligent selection liability compresses brokerage gross margin independent of carrier procurement skill.

Counter-cyclical broker cash flow masks earnings decay — Operating cash flow in truckload brokerage is counter-cyclical to freight rates, causing cash generation to peak when earnings and margins are collapsing.

Exception resolution requires commercial authority not workflow software — Operational exception resolution cannot be automated purely through software workflows because clearing a transit deviation requires commercial authority to spend money or absorb capacity losses.

Management claims of deliberate volume rejection require yield proof — Management assertions of walking away from unprofitable volume are untestable anti-patterns without retained-book yield analysis, often disguising market share loss.

Rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage — Rising freight rates mechanically expand receivables and drain operating cash in brokerage, driving days sales outstanding higher without internal billing or collections failure.

Rate inflation mechanically dilutes broker margin percentage — Rapid freight linehaul inflation mechanically compresses a broker's gross margin percentage even while gross profit per load and absolute earnings expand.

SIC codes fail to distinguish transport operating models — Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes bundle fundamentally different transport operating models under identical numbers, making code-derived peer sets invalid.

Tag priority ladders can return stale EDGAR data — Omitting modern revenue concepts in SEC EDGAR XBRL fallback ladders causes extraction scripts to return decade-old historical figures rather than reporting missing data.

Venture-subsidised pricing masks negative broker unit economics — Venture-funded rate discounting and carrier pay premiums create an illusion of algorithmic operational efficiency that collapses the moment balance-sheet capital stops absorbing the gross-to-net spread deficit.

Visibility platforms fail at carrier data capture not software layer — Real-time supply chain visibility platforms stall because long-tail carrier telematics and batch EDI feeds fail to provide clean, timely data, not because tracking software or predictive algorithms are defective.
