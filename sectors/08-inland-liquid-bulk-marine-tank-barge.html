In 90 seconds

The business owns tank barges and the towboats that push them, crews both, and sells liquid-bulk capacity on the US inland waterway system and coastwise. Paid either per movement (contract of affreightment) or per day (time charter). Never takes ownership of the cargo. The unit of work is the crewed towboat-day, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on total revenue, read ex-fuel. Fuel is a contractual pass-through on term business and appears as a rebill in revenue. The gross line therefore moves with diesel while the business does not — the same trap gross revenue sets in brokerage. kirby FY2025: revenue rose 1% while operating income rose 3%, and the 10-K attributes part of the gap to lower fuel rebills. The cost base is crew and vessel operating cost first, then fuel (approximately 8% of marine revenue at kirby FY2025), then depreciation (approximately 11% of revenue), then shipyard maintenance and USCG inspection. SG&A is low: 7.5% of marine revenue at kirby FY2025.

The valid peer set is thin and mostly private. Listed comparators carrying a disclosed marine segment: Genesis Energy, Martin Midstream — verify segment scope before use, both mix inland and offshore. Private operators: Blessey Marine, Canal Barge (Redwood Holdings), LeBeouf Bros Towing, Florida Marine Transporters. Never compare to dry-cargo barge lines (Ingram, ACBL) on margin, or to truckload carriers on operating ratio.

The common misreadTreating barge count as capacity. Power and crew bind. Unlike the barge fleet, the power fleet flexes: kirby states it has historically chartered approximately one quarter of its horsepower requirement. It released chartered boats through H2 2025 as activity fell — average inland towboats 285 in 2024, 279 in 2025, 266 in the 2025 fourth quarter. The fixed asset is the barge; the flexible asset is the boat. That is the reverse of the LTL reading, where the network is fixed and the tractor is not. It also means a quoted towboat count must be checked for period — a fourth-quarter average is a trough, not a fleet.

The operating model

The model divides into 4 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little. It operates across Indonesia.

  • Primary margin metric: segment operating income / segment revenue, paired with return on segment assets. Margin alone misleads here because asset intensity is extreme — see the financial signature.

The contract form decides the unit of work, and it is disclosed

This distinction governs every other reading of the model and has no analogue in models 1-7.

  • Contract of affreightment — paid to move a cargo from origin to destination at a set rate. A delayed tow earns nothing while it waits. Delay is a revenue loss.
  • Time charter — paid a daily rate for the vessel. A delayed tow earns the same day rate. Delay is a cost event only: fuel, crew, repositioning.

Kirby Corporation states the purpose plainly. Time charters "help insulate the Company from revenue fluctuations caused by weather and navigational delays and temporary market declines." FY2025 disclosure: inland 70% term / 30% spot, with time charters 59% of inland term revenue; coastal 100% term and approximately 100% time charter.

Consequence for any uptime or availability argument. Roughly 41% of inland revenue (70% x 59%) and effectively all coastal revenue is delay-insulated by contract. Only the affreightment book — inland spot plus the non-time-charter share of inland term — carries delay as revenue loss. On Kirby Corporation FY2025 that is approximately 59% of inland revenue, or about 47% of marine segment revenue. Sizing a recovered-delay-day pool against total marine revenue overstates the base by roughly a factor of two.

  • Cost structure: crew and vessel operating cost first, then fuel (approximately 8% of marine revenue at Kirby Corporation FY2025), then depreciation (approximately 11% of revenue), then shipyard maintenance and USCG inspection. SG&A is low: 7.5% of marine revenue at Kirby Corporation FY2025.
  • Key productivity metrics: barge utilisation, delay days per towboat-year, time-charter share of term revenue, escalator capture and reset lag, revenue per available barge-day, chartered share of horsepower.

Capacity is priced and impaired by vessel class, not by asset

Kirby Corporation's FY2025 10-K states that recoverability is assessed on vessel classes, "because identifiable cash flows for individual marine transportation assets are not available"; that customers "generally do not specify which particular vessel is used"; and that "prices are quoted based on vessel classes not individual assets."

Two consequences. Per-asset economics are not computable from inside the company either, so any proposition resting on per-vessel profitability asks for a number that does not exist. And the correct capacity unit for analysis is the class-day: interchangeable vessels within a class, nominated day by day against geographic position. See marine capacity is priced by vessel class, not by asset.

Financial signature

  • Asset turnover the lowest of any surface transport model: approximately 0.4x. Kirby Corporation FY2025 marine segment: revenue $1,935.3m over segment assets $4,705.7m = 0.41x. That sits below LTL (1.0-1.5x) and inside the ports and terminals range (0.2-0.5x). Read this model as infrastructure, not as trucking.
  • Capex approximately equals depreciation in steady state, at 11-12% of segment revenue. Kirby Corporation FY2025: marine capex $229.1m (11.8% of segment revenue) against marine depreciation and amortisation $213.9m (11.1%).
  • Book value understates replacement cost, so reported return on capital is flattered. A new 30,000-barrel inland tank barge costs approximately $4.5m, steel sits near historical highs, and hull lives run 25-40 years. A fleet built over decades sits at depreciated cost far below replacement. This is the mirror of the LTL terminal-land distortion and it runs the opposite way: LTL land at historic cost understates an appreciating asset; here historic cost understates the replacement bill for a depreciating one that must actually be renewed. See depreciated book flatters return on capital in long-lived marine fleets.
  • Working capital light; there is essentially no inventory. Receivables run roughly 45-55 days and the operator does not own the cargo. Any cash conversion cycle computed for a group that also contains a distribution business is a blend and says nothing about the marine operation.
  • Supply is structurally constrained and the constraint is external. The US inland tank barge fleet has been approximately flat since 2019, at around 4,000 units. Industry-wide in 2025: 66 built against 65 retired. Roughly 600 units are aged 30 years or older and 350 are aged 40 or older, against a national average age of approximately 18 years. Few yards of scale build these barges, and newbuild economics do not clear at prevailing rates. Capacity is therefore a slow variable, and utilisation reads demand rather than management.
  • Lock and waterway infrastructure is a cost the operator carries and does not control. Kirby Corporation defines delay days as lost tow time from "weather, lock conditions, or other navigational factors" — externally caused by definition. The externally generated dataset that measures the lock component is USACE's Lock Performance Monitoring System, registered in benchmark register. exists for an operational metric a company reports itself.

Figures cited to Kirby Corporation FY2025 Form 10-K (filed 2026-02-17), grade ``. Derived ratios marked. One company's segment disclosure is the only clean public source for this model; that is stated rather than dressed as a peer distribution.

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Time Charter Equivalent

Definition

Gross freight income less voyage costs, usually expressed in US dollars per day. Voyage costs include bunker, port and canal charges.

Why it matters

It converts voyage charters, time charters and COA voyages into a comparable ship-day number.

Moves up

Higher freight rate, shorter ballast leg, slower fuel-efficient steaming where time cost is lower than bunker saving, and premium cargo or ice-class employment.

Moves down

Bunker spikes on voyage-risk contracts, longer ballast legs, port congestion, and lower spot market indices.

Where to find it

Dry bulk and tanker operator earnings releases, investor decks and MD&A. It is non-GAAP; use the company's reconciliation.

Common misread

Comparing TCE without checking available-day definition. Some companies use voyage days; others use available days.

Diagnostic question

"How many days in the denominator were loaded, ballast, waiting, or off hire?"

Delay Days

Definition

Lost time incurred by a tow — one towboat plus one or more barges — while stopped in transit. Kirby Corporation's FY2025 10-K scopes the causes explicitly: "weather, lock conditions, or other navigational factors."

Why it matters

The only published operational availability measure in inland marine, and the denominator of any productivity argument in model 8.

Moves up

Fog and high wind on the Gulf Coast; high or low water on the river system; lock outages and chamber closures; ice.

Moves down

Favourable seasonal weather; lock reliability; routing away from constrained chambers.

Where to find it

Disclosed annually in Kirby Corporation's 10-K MD&A. No other operator located publishes it.

Common misread

Reading a 1% annual improvement as management performance. In a metric whose disclosed drivers are all weather and infrastructure, a 1% move is within the noise of a mild winter.

Diagnostic question

"Of last year's delay days, how many chambers and how many weather events do they come from, and which of those did you route around?"

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

Barge Utilisation

Definition

The operator's own measure of the share of the tank barge fleet actively employed. Definitions are not standardised and no operator located publishes the calculation.

Why it matters

The demand read for model 8. Because fleet supply is approximately fixed industry-wide, utilisation moves with volumes, not with capacity decisions.

Moves up

Petrochemical and refinery production volumes; refinery utilisation; export demand; barges out of service industry-wide.

Moves down

Lighter feedstock mix; refinery outages and closures; new barge deliveries; cargo shifting to pipeline.

Where to find it

Disclosed as a range, by quarter, in Kirby Corporation's 10-K and earnings releases. Never as a number.

Common misread

Quoting a full-year utilisation figure. Kirby Corporation's FY2025 10-K carries two different full-year characterisations of the same number in two places — "in the 90% range" and "decreased to the high 80% range" — which is why the quarterly series is the one to use. Recorded as a source conflict in Kirby Corporation's evidence log rather than averaged.

Diagnostic question

"What is in the numerator — barges on hire, barges loaded, or barges not in the yard?"

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation

Symptom

Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Visible from outside

The disclosed lag and covered share are grade ` from the 10-K; the analyst exchanges are gradeC`. Realised rates tracking the input series with a lag is the mechanism. Realised rates tracking the freight rate index instead is the cycle. That test runs on public data and has not been run.

Financial signature

Operating margin compresses in a period of rising input prices while volume and utilisation are stable, and recovers with a lag rather than with volume. The tell is the pairing of a disclosed lag with a disclosed uncovered share. - Kirby Corporation FY2025 10-K: marine term contracts "typically include fuel escalation clauses, or the customer pays for the fuel," with "generally a 30 to 120 day delay before contracts are adjusted"; spot contracts carry no fuel escalator and spot was 30% of inland marine revenue. The filing additionally lists the conditions under which the clauses under-recover: "specific terms of the fuel escalation formulas, fuel price volatility, navigating conditions, tow sizes, trip routing, and the location of loading

sell-side analysts across the 2026 Q2 and Q3 cycle and answered qualitatively both times, with management redirecting to a market-driven rather than fuel-driven pricing environment.

Root cause

The escalator is a contractual formula computed and invoiced on a cycle, not a price the commercial team sets. Recovery fails in three distinct places. The formula is indexed to a series that moves differently from the actual input. The cycle resets after the cost lands. The billing recalculates and rebills by hand. Only the third is addressable operationally, and no operator located discloses which of the three binds.

Operational systems where this surfaces

SAP TM, Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

The operator discloses realised escalator capture at or near 100% across a full inflation cycle, or the uncovered revenue share is immaterial, or realised rate movements track a freight rate index rather than the input series.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost

Symptom

The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Visible from outside

Models 8 and 9 rest on `` related-party and cost disclosure only.

Financial signature

Cost per unit of work on the purchased line rises during a volume decline, while the operator's own controllable cost lines hold flat per unit. The gap between the two is the mechanism. 34.2% of direct cargo expense and 12.5% of Ocean Transportation revenue. Terminal services cost $341.9m (2025), $320.9m (2024), $297.2m (2023): +15% over three years across which total container volume fell. Per FEU, $875 against $800, +9.4% in a year when volume fell 2.6%. Over the same period vessel operating expense per FEU was flat and general and administrative per FEU fell 0.5%. - Kirby Corporation FY2025: newbuild and heavy maintenance capacity for inland tank barges sits with a small number of yards; the operator runs its own yard at Channelview partly in response. Steel is disclosed as near historical highs and shipyard labour as expensive. This is the same structure on the capital line rather than the operating line, and thinner evidence than the Recorded as a second observation, not a second proof. - Already recorded for model 7 in operating models: Class I rail agreements with contractual minimum volume commitments, where volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers, evidenced through STB EP 770 and Ex Parte 711 dockets.

Root cause

The operator's cost base contains a block priced by a counterparty whose own fixed costs it must cover. When the operator's volume falls, the counterparty's cost per unit rises and is passed through. The operator holds an equity interest or a long contract, so it cannot re-tender. In the joint-venture case it also books its share of the counterparty's earnings inside its own cost line. The same relationship therefore flatters the income statement while inflating the cost per unit.

Falsified if

The purchased input's unit cost falls with the operator's volume, or the operator demonstrates it re-tendered the work, or the cost rise is explained by scope rather than by the counterparty's own cost base.

Dry-docking bunching removes revenue days

Symptom

Several scheduled dockings fall into the same period, reducing revenue days and utilisation before visible repair cost rises.

What it looks like from outside

Off-hire, dry-docking payments or deferred dry-docking assets rise. Revenue days fall in the affected fleet while market volume indices do not explain the fall.

Financial signature

Revenue days, shipping days or available days fall while owned fleet count is stable. Maintenance capex and deferred dry-docking assets rise. Algoma Central FY2025 Ocean Self-Unloaders had lower revenue days from five planned dry-dockings against two in 2024. cash dry-docking payments rose to $49.4m from $30.2m.

Cycle discriminator

A freight cycle changes rates and demand. This mechanism changes the denominator. It is usable only when revenue-day or off-hire loss exceeds the demand change implied by sector volume indices.

Root cause

Class, regulatory and yard windows were allowed to cluster. Deferral makes the later period look like poor utilisation even when demand is unchanged.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Danaos Management Consultants, ABS Nautical Systems

What operators do about it

Stagger dockings, reserve yard slots earlier, bundle repair scope, charter substitute tonnage, or delay non-critical work.

Falsified if

Utilisation falls with the market while docking days, off-hire days and dry-docking cash do not rise.

Off-hire and technical downtime convert owned fleet into idle capital

Symptom

Vessel count is stable, but available days, contracted days or revenue days fall because ships are technically unavailable.

What it looks like from outside

Off-hire days, utilisation, revenue days or availability fall faster than reported fleet count. Repair and maintenance cost may lag the lost days.

Financial signature

Vessel operating cost, depreciation and finance cost continue while revenue days fall. The result is lower TCE or lower operating earnings per vessel. Pangaea risk disclosure names vessel breakdowns and off-hires. Algoma attributes a revenue-day fall to planned dockings in Ocean Self-Unloaders.

Cycle discriminator

Market cycles reduce fixture rates and demand. This mechanism reduces physical availability. It requires off-hire or available-day evidence, not only lower revenue.

Root cause

Maintenance planning, technical reliability, class survey, spare-parts availability, crew availability or repair-yard access removes capacity from service.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Q88, Danaos Software, ABS Nautical Systems, Kalmar

What operators do about it

Preventive maintenance, condition monitoring, spare-parts pooling, technical-management audits, yard-slot reservation and temporary charter cover.

Falsified if

Available days are stable and the earnings move is fully explained by lower rates or weaker volumes.

Fleet age capex cliff raises maintenance before replacement is funded

Symptom

Ageing vessels show rising maintenance, dry-docking scope, off-hire and class-compliance capex before newbuild cash flow is ready.

What it looks like from outside

Fleet age rises; maintenance and docking cash rise; vessel sales remove older units; newbuild commitments appear after a period of high utilisation or regulatory pressure.

Financial signature

Repair cost, off-hire days and dry-docking cash rise while depreciation remains based on old book value. Capex later jumps when replacement is ordered. Kirby Corporation reports long-lived marine assets and a constrained US inland tank barge fleet. two 14,000 TEU vessels for 2028 after heavy fleet capex years.

Cycle discriminator

A cycle changes asset values and rates. The mechanism requires age, maintenance, off-hire or replacement-capex evidence moving against the company's own fleet history, not only lower vessel values.

Root cause

Replacement decisions were deferred while the old fleet still earned acceptable cash returns. Book value understated replacement cost, so reported returns looked better than economic returns.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Danaos Software, ABS Nautical Systems, Kalmar Insight

What operators do about it

Staged renewal, sale of older ships, life extension work, charter-in cover, speed limitation, or scrapping.

Falsified if

Fleet age is stable or falling, maintenance per vessel-day is stable, and capex is ordinary replacement rather than catch-up.

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Purchased-transportation accrual failure

Symptom

Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Visible from outside

  • Consequence evidence, not mechanism evidence: the Nasdaq listing deficiency notice (2026-08-20, Listing (c)(1)); securities litigation filings; and the Deutsche Bank impact estimate. These show that a failure occurred, that reports were delayed, and that the market repriced the company. They do not evidence the accrual-to-invoice locus.
  • Management remediation and supporting material: the disclosed Ernst & Young re-audit engagement, carrier settlement, freight payment audit and technical accounting roles in postings.

Financial signature

Reported operating margin overstated while cash is unaffected — the tell that separates this from a revenue or collections failure. approximately $77 million across the first three quarters of 2025, and management confirmed no impact on cash or operating cash flow in any period. Falsified if

The understatement is confined to a single acquired entity or a single period and does not widen backwards, which points to a conversion or purchase-accounting error rather than a standing reconciliation failure; or the restatement also moves cash or operating cash flow, which makes it a different failure; or the disclosed cause names revenue recognition rather than expense accrual.

Remediation

Automated three-way match between tender, accrual and carrier invoice; exception queues on unsupported accrual adjustments; continuous reconciliation rather than period-end reconciliation.

Forensic craft insights

Specific observations on how this model behaves in practice, the preconditions for automation, and operational failure modes.

A disclosed metric definition can exclude the component a value case rests on — Read the operator's own definition of a disclosed operational metric before building on it.

An attributed inventory increase does not characterise the balance — When a filing explains the movement in inventory, that explanation describes the delta, not the stock.

Depreciated book flatters return on capital in long-lived marine fleets — For fleets of 25-to-40-year assets built over decades, reported return on capital sits on a denominator well below replacement cost.

Margin ranks segments backwards where asset turnover differs — In a group running two operating models with materially different asset intensity, ranking the segments on operating margin can invert the ranking on return on the capital each employs.

Marine capacity is priced by vessel class, not by asset — In inland and coastal marine the sellable unit is a class of interchangeable vessel, not a named vessel.

Time charters make delay a cost event, not a revenue loss — In marine transport the contract form decides what a lost day of availability is worth.
