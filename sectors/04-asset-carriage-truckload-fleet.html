In 90 seconds

The business owns tractors, trailers, and employs drivers. Sells capacity directly to shippers. The unit of work is the loaded mile, or the linehaul trip, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on total revenue. Standard measure is operating ratio (operating expenses ÷ revenue), often quoted ex-fuel. The cost base is driver compensation (predominantly mileage-based), fuel, equipment depreciation, insurance, and maintenance.

The valid peer set is Heartland Express, Knight-Swift (One-Way OTR), Werner (One-Way Truckload), Schneider (Network Truckload), Marten (Truckload).

The common misreadTreating an OTR operating ratio improvement during a rate boom as operational progress. Spot rate inflation flatters OR without improving fleet execution.

The operating model

The model divides into 6 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little. It operates across Australia, Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand.

Architecture: Irregular-Route OTR vs. Dedicated Contract Carriage

Asset carriage is not a homogeneous operating model. It contains two distinct commercial and operational architectures that cannot be blended in analysis:

  1. Irregular-Route Over-the-Road (One-Way OTR): Sells capacity per mile or per load across an open, dynamic network. Dispatches depend on daily spot and annual contract tender volume. Network balance, empty miles percentage, and load-to-truck ratios govern profitability.
  2. Dedicated Contract Carriage: Replaces shipper private fleets under multi-year indexed contracts. Assigns dedicated tractors, trailers, and domiciled driver pools exclusively to specific customer facilities. Capacity is committed; revenues are governed by fixed weekly charges and indexed cost pass-throughs.

Architectural decision: why Dedicated remains under Model 4. Dedicated carriage is classified as a primary sub-sector under Model 4 rather than an independent operating model. Both operations share Class 8 tractors, trailers, terminal maintenance shops, FMCSA safety regulatory oversight, driver qualification files, and commercial auto casualty liability. Crucially, carrier CFOs actively flex rolling stock capital between Dedicated and OTR across the freight cycle. However, because their units of work, pricing structures, and margin cycles diverge, Dedicated cannot be evaluated against generic OTR benchmarks. They require separate metric definitions and peer distributions.

Scope note

U-Haul stays outside this model for now. Its workbook describes consumer self-moving equipment rental and self-storage, with customers driving the equipment themselves. The unit of work is a rental transaction, not a freight trip performed for a shipper.

Sub-sectors

The model holds, but the economics inside it are not uniform. Each sub-sector changes what the work costs and which numbers move.

  • Unit of work: the loaded mile, or the linehaul trip
  • Correct denominator: total revenue. Standard measure is operating ratio (operating expenses ÷ revenue), often quoted ex-fuel.
  • Key productivity metrics: revenue per tractor per week, empty miles %, cost per mile, driver turnover (80–95%+), seated tractor utilization
  • Cost structure: driver compensation (predominantly mileage-based), fuel, equipment depreciation, insurance, and maintenance.
  • Valid peers: Heartland Express, Knight-Swift (One-Way OTR), Werner (One-Way Truckload), Schneider (Network Truckload), Marten (Truckload).
  • Common outsider misread: treating an OTR operating ratio improvement during a rate boom as operational progress. Spot rate inflation flatters OR without improving fleet execution.

Financial signature

  • Capex intensity high and cyclical: gross capex approx 10-20% of revenue through a fleet replacement cycle, net capex approx 5-12% after tractor and trailer disposal proceeds. Depreciation runs approx 8-12% of revenue. Gains on equipment sales flow through operating results, so a soft used-truck market widens reported operating ratio with no change in freight operations - check the gain line before reading an OR movement.
  • Asset turnover low: approx 1.2-2.0x revenue / total assets, with PP&E typically the majority of assets. Capital employed, not headcount, is the binding constraint on growth.
  • Working capital light relative to fixed assets; the liabilities carry the story. DSO approx 35-45 days, minimal inventory (parts and fuel). What distinguishes the model is funding structure: equipment debt and lease obligations, plus self-insurance and claims reserves with a multi-year tail. Adverse reserve development is a cash and earnings event with no analogue in the non-asset models.
  • Operating margin counter-cyclicality between sub-sectors. Dedicated operating ratios remain range-bound (88%–95%) through freight downcycles due to fixed weekly charges. Irregular-route OTR margins compress violently (OR 98% to 105%), creating massive earnings divergence inside the same parent company.

Ranges are approximate directional norms for the operating model, verified against SEC 10-K segment filings (Knight-Swift, Werner, Schneider, Marten, Covenant).

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Revenue per Truck per Week

Definition

Total freight revenue (commonly net of fuel surcharge, or gross) ÷ average operating tractors ÷ weeks in period

Why it matters

The definitive productivity and asset yield metric for motor carriers. In dedicated carriage, it reflects the contractual two-part tariff (fixed weekly truck charge + variable mileage rate).

Moves up

Contractual minimum truck charge increases; higher weekly dispatched mileage per tractor; indexed rate escalations; accessorial capture

Moves down

Unseated tractors; shipper volume curtailment below contracted minimums; unbilled equipment dwell; escalator recovery lag

Where to find it

Disclosed in SEC Form 10-K and 10-Q MD&A tables (Werner, Schneider, Covenant, Marten, J.B. Hunt)

Common misread

Comparing gross revenue per truck against net-of-fuel figures, or comparing dedicated yield against irregular-route OTR. OTR moves with spot linehaul rates; dedicated reflects fixed asset recovery.

Diagnostic question

What percentage of your revenue per truck per week is guaranteed under fixed weekly equipment charges versus variable mileage dispatches?

Fleet Utilisation against Committed Capacity

Definition

Actively dispatched and revenue-generating tractors ÷ total contractually committed fleet size on a dedicated customer account

Why it matters

Dedicated accounts commit dedicated iron and drivers to a shipper. If shipper volume drops and tractors sit idle without minimum revenue guarantees, asset productivity collapses.

Moves up

Consistent shipper dispatch cadence; efficient slip-seating; rapid seated-driver replacement; enforceable minimum volume commitments (MVCs)

Moves down

Customer plant shutdowns or retail inventory cuts; localized driver recruitment deficits leaving trucks unseated; maintenance dwell

Where to find it

Internal TMS dispatch platforms (Trimble TMW, McLeod LoadMaster); referenced qualitatively in carrier 10-K fleet disclosures

Common misread

Evaluating dedicated utilization on miles per tractor per week. A dedicated store replenishment fleet with short hauls can show low weekly mileage but 100% committed asset utilization and high return on capital.

Diagnostic question

When a dedicated customer's dispatch volume drops 20% below forecast, how many tractors sit unseated, and does the contract enforce billing for the idle capacity?

Driver Turnover

Definition

Annualized driver separations (voluntary and involuntary) ÷ average active driver headcount

Why it matters

Driver recruitment and onboarding costs $5,000 to $10,000+ per seat. Dedicated fleets act as a structural retention mechanism, insulating carriers from irregular-route driver churn.

Moves up (worse)

Unpredictable OTR schedules; extended time away from home; local industrial wage competition; shipper detention

Moves down (better)

Regular dedicated routes; predictable daily or weekly home time; guaranteed minimum weekly pay; newer equipment

Where to find it

American Trucking Associations (ATA) quarterly driver turnover surveys; carrier annual reports and ESG disclosures

Common misread

Treating 90% turnover as an exodus from the industry. It is primarily inter-carrier churn chasing sign-on bonuses. Dedicated routes eliminate the churn trigger by providing predictable home time.

Diagnostic question

What is the turnover differential between your dedicated accounts and your irregular-route OTR fleet, and what is that gap costing in recruiting overhead and unseated equipment?

Contract Renewal Rate

Definition

Contracted revenue or tractor commitment retained at renewal ÷ contract value up for renewal in the period

Why it matters

Both contract logistics (warehousing) and dedicated contract carriage run on books of multi-year agreements (typically 3 to 5 years). Renewal is where accumulated inflation and service performance are repriced, and unrecovered capital (racking, custom trailers, daycabs) becomes an exposure if lost.

Moves up

High on-time delivery and service level compliance; deep systems integration (EDI, TMS, private yard dispatch); mutual operational dependency

Moves down

Service failures inside the term; shipper insourcing (converting to private fleet) or switching to irregular-route 3PLs; cost drift above market

Where to find it

Disclosed in investor presentations and annual reports (J.B. Hunt DCS discloses 95–98% renewal; Schneider, Werner, GXO)

Benchmark

90% to 98% in mature dedicated contract carriage and contract logistics accounts.

Common misread

Treating a high renewal rate as pure strength. Contracts renewed at concessions (conceding rate freezes or absorbing higher equipment costs to protect volume) count as retained while destroying account margin.

Diagnostic question

Of the dedicated accounts or warehouse contracts you renewed last year, which accepted lower rates or capped escalators, and what did the shipper point to when demanding the concession?

Operating Ratio

Definition

Operating expenses ÷ revenue. Often quoted ex-fuel-surcharge.

Why it matters

The standard measure for asset carriers. Lower is better.

Moves up (worse)

Driver wage inflation; utilisation decline; insurance; empty miles

Moves down (better)

Density; utilisation; fuel efficiency

Common misread

Comparing OR across carriers without normalising for fuel surcharge treatment and length of haul

Diagnostic question

Where do your empty miles come from - which lanes, which customers, and which planning decisions put them there?

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Contract vintage margin decay

Symptom

Blended margin declines while new-business margin holds

Financial signature

Revenue growth without gross margin growth; older contract cohorts underperform

Root cause

Fixed-price or inadequately indexed contracts signed in a different cost environment, with no productivity mechanism to offset labour inflation.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, SAP TM, MercuryGate

Falsified if

The operator discloses indexation and a quantified productivity offset holding cost per unit of work flat through wage inflation disclosed new-site start-up cost, which depresses blended margin without any cohort decaying; or the decay does not survive renewal, in which case it is the commercial variant and the operational remediation is mis-sold.

Remediation

Depends which side of the discriminator the target sits on cost-to-serve modelling per contract and priced indexation at renewal for the commercial variant; productivity instrumentation and hours per unit of work for the operational variant. Do not pitch both. - What has been tried (failure history): autonomous inventory auditing robots and aerial drones (computer vision autonomous inventory auditing, Bossa Nova Robotics / Walmart). - Why it failed: scanning robots detected inventory discrepancies but could not physically retrieve or replenish stock; direct warehouse labor hours remained unchanged, while robotics hardware depreciation and maintenance inflated facility operating costs. - Difficulty of remediation: operational productivity instrumentation is harder than it looks. Measuring hours per unit of work does not improve throughput if technology fails to eliminate physical touches; deploying sensing automation without physical material-handling automation adds overhead without offsetting wage inflation.

Diagnostic questions

  • "Which of your contracts were priced before 2022 and what indexation do they carry?"
  • "Take a contract you repriced at renewal — did the margin come back to book, and did it stay there?"

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation

Symptom

Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Visible from outside

The disclosed lag and covered share are grade ` from the 10-K; the analyst exchanges are gradeC`. Realised rates tracking the input series with a lag is the mechanism. Realised rates tracking the freight rate index instead is the cycle. That test runs on public data and has not been run.

Financial signature

Operating margin compresses in a period of rising input prices while volume and utilisation are stable, and recovers with a lag rather than with volume. The tell is the pairing of a disclosed lag with a disclosed uncovered share. escalation clauses, or the customer pays for the fuel," with "generally a 30 to 120 day delay before contracts are adjusted"; spot contracts carry no fuel escalator and spot was 30% of inland marine revenue. sell-side analysts across the 2026 Q2 and Q3 cycle and answered qualitatively both times, with management redirecting to a market-driven rather than fuel-driven pricing environment.

Root cause

The escalator is a contractual formula computed and invoiced on a cycle, not a price the commercial team sets. Recovery fails in three distinct places. The formula is indexed to a series that moves differently from the actual input. The cycle resets after the cost lands. The billing recalculates and rebills by hand. Only the third is addressable operationally, and no operator located discloses which of the three binds.

Operational systems where this surfaces

SAP TM, Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

The operator discloses realised escalator capture at or near 100% across a full inflation cycle, or the uncovered revenue share is immaterial, or realised rate movements track a freight rate index rather than the input series.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost

Symptom

The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Visible from outside

Models 8 and 9 rest on `` related-party and cost disclosure only.

Financial signature

Cost per unit of work on the purchased line rises during a volume decline, while the operator's own controllable cost lines hold flat per unit. The gap between the two is the mechanism. 34.2% of direct cargo expense and 12.5% of Ocean Transportation revenue. Terminal services cost $341.9m (2025), $320.9m (2024), $297.2m (2023): +15% over three years across which total container volume fell. Per FEU, $875 against $800, +9.4% in a year when volume fell 2.6%. Over the same period vessel operating expense per FEU was flat and general and administrative per FEU fell 0.5%. barges sits with a small number of yards; the operator runs its own yard at Channelview partly in response. Steel is disclosed as near historical highs and shipyard labour as expensive. This is the same structure on the capital line rather than the operating line, and thinner evidence than the Recorded as a second observation, not a second proof. - Already recorded for model 7 in operating models: Class I rail agreements with contractual minimum volume commitments, where volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers, evidenced through STB EP 770 and Ex Parte 711 dockets.

Root cause

The operator's cost base contains a block priced by a counterparty whose own fixed costs it must cover. When the operator's volume falls, the counterparty's cost per unit rises and is passed through. The operator holds an equity interest or a long contract, so it cannot re-tender. In the joint-venture case it also books its share of the counterparty's earnings inside its own cost line. The same relationship therefore flatters the income statement while inflating the cost per unit.

Falsified if

The purchased input's unit cost falls with the operator's volume, or the operator demonstrates it re-tendered the work, or the cost rise is explained by scope rather than by the counterparty's own cost base.

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Purchased-transportation accrual failure

Symptom

Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Visible from outside

  • Consequence evidence, not mechanism evidence: the Nasdaq listing deficiency notice (2026-08-20, Listing (c)(1)); securities litigation filings; and the Deutsche Bank impact estimate. These show that a failure occurred, that reports were delayed, and that the market repriced the company. They do not evidence the accrual-to-invoice locus.
  • Management remediation and supporting material: the disclosed Ernst & Young re-audit engagement, carrier settlement, freight payment audit and technical accounting roles in postings.

Financial signature

Reported operating margin overstated while cash is unaffected — the tell that separates this from a revenue or collections failure. Hub Group, Inc. understated both lines by approximately $77 million across the first three quarters of 2025, and management confirmed no impact on cash or operating cash flow in any period. The consequential costs are the larger part of the signature and are externally observable: delayed 10-K, two missed 10-Qs, shares −18% (2026-02-06) and −9.2% (2026-05-12), CFO and COO departures (2026-05-28), a securities litigation investigation, and a Nasdaq listing deficiency. See evidence of consequences does not evidence mechanism cause and accrual release inverts cost-ratio trend.

Root cause

Hub Group, Inc. 2026-Q1 disclosed that transactions were "recognized prematurely, booked incorrectly, or lacked adequate supporting documentation" in the historical ledgers, with an expected conclusion of ineffective disclosure controls and ineffective ICFR for FY2023 and FY2024. In operational terms this sits on the accrual-to-invoice reconciliation for purchased transportation: the accrued cost of a move and the carrier's eventual invoice are reconciled at period end, and the reconciliation was carrying an unsupported reduction to accounts payable. The company has not stated it that way. The mapping from "lacked adequate supporting documentation" to the PT accrual process is our inference and carries no better than.

Operational systems where this surfaces

TriumphPay, HubTran, Cass Information Systems, McLeod LoadMaster/PowerBroker, Trimble TMW

Falsified if

The understatement is confined to a single acquired entity or a single period and does not widen backwards, which points to a conversion or purchase-accounting error rather than a standing reconciliation failure; or the restatement also moves cash or operating cash flow, which makes it a different failure; or the disclosed cause names revenue recognition rather than expense accrual.

Remediation

Automated three-way match between tender, accrual and carrier invoice; exception queues on unsupported accrual adjustments; continuous reconciliation rather than period-end reconciliation.

Diagnostic question

"When a carrier's invoice arrives, what does it get matched against, and who clears the difference?"

Forensic craft insights

Specific observations on how this model behaves in practice, the preconditions for automation, and operational failure modes.

Exception resolution requires commercial authority not workflow software — Operational exception resolution cannot be automated purely through software workflows because clearing a transit deviation requires commercial authority to spend money or absorb capacity losses.

Management claims of deliberate volume rejection require yield proof — Management assertions of walking away from unprofitable volume are untestable anti-patterns without retained-book yield analysis, often disguising market share loss.

SIC codes fail to distinguish transport operating models — Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes bundle fundamentally different transport operating models under identical numbers, making code-derived peer sets invalid.

Tag priority ladders can return stale EDGAR data — Omitting modern revenue concepts in SEC EDGAR XBRL fallback ladders causes extraction scripts to return decade-old historical figures rather than reporting missing data.
