In 90 seconds

The business buys space from ocean/air carriers, sells to shippers, manages the documentation and customs chain. Owns no vessels or aircraft. The unit of work is the shipment file, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on gross profit / net revenue. The cost base is personnel plus systems. Document handling is the labour sink.

The valid peer set is Expeditors, Kuehne+Nagel, DSV, Kerry Logistics, Dimerco.

The common misreadTreating volume (TEU) growth as margin growth. Ocean and air yields move independently of volume.

The operating model

The model divides into 5 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little. It operates across Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Australia.

  • Key productivity metrics: files per operations FTE per month, touches per file, cost per file, gross profit per TEU or per tonne.

Sub-sectors

The model holds, but the economics inside it are not uniform. Each sub-sector changes what the work costs and which numbers move.

Financial signature

  • Working capital intensity: the highest of the non-asset models, and part of it is other people's money. Ocean and air carriers are paid on short terms and duty/VAT is frequently advanced on the customer's behalf, while shippers pay in approx 45-60 days. DSO approx 45-60 days; cash conversion cycle roughly +20 to +35 days. Duty and tax advancement inflates both receivables and payables without touching net revenue, so receivables-to-revenue ratios are not comparable across forwarders with different customs mixes.
  • Capex low: approx 1-3% of net revenue, concentrated in customs and TMS systems, customer integrations, and office footprint. No owned vessels, aircraft, or terminal capacity.
  • Balance sheet typically net cash, and rate spikes are the cash risk. Asset turnover is very high on gross revenue and close to meaningless as a measure; the balance sheet is cash, receivables, and acquisition goodwill. The distinguishing cash-flow behaviour is a sudden ocean or air rate spike: the gross-up must be funded before it is collected, so a forwarder can be cash-hungry in precisely its highest-margin periods.

Ranges are approximate directional norms for the operating model, not figures sourced to any company's filings.

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Files per Operations FTE per Month

Definition

Shipment files handled ÷ operations headcount ÷ months

Why it matters

Forwarding cost is document-handling labour. This is the productivity number the operating model actually turns on.

Moves up

Carrier and customs EDI coverage; automated document capture and classification; standing routing instructions removing per-file decisions

Moves down

Manual rekeying between customer, carrier and customs systems; exception-heavy trade lanes; multiple TMS instances after acquisition

Where to find it

Rarely disclosed. Model from segment headcount and volume, label.

Common misread

Comparing across forwarders with different trade lane and customs mix. A customs-heavy file is not the same unit of work as a port-to-port FCL file.

Diagnostic question

For a routine file on your busiest trade lane, which data is keyed by a person and which arrives by integration?

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

Gross Profit / Net Revenue

Definition

Gross revenue less the cost of carrier space, freight, and directly attributable third-party charges

Why it matters

The only revenue line for a forwarder that reflects company performance. Gross revenue moves with ocean and air rate cycles, which the forwarder neither sets nor controls.

Moves up

Buying space below the price sold; yield discipline on contracted allocations; mix toward customs brokerage and value-added services; spot exposure in a rising rate market

Moves down

Volume bought at market on uncontracted space; mix toward low-yield FCL port-to-port; loss of allocation forcing spot buys

Where to find it

Disclosed as "net revenue" or "gross profit" in forwarder reporting; European filers commonly present it as gross profit on the face of the income statement

Common misread

Reading gross revenue growth as performance. A freight rate spike inflates gross revenue and can leave gross profit flat or lower.

Diagnostic question

The last time ocean rates moved sharply, how long did it take to reprice your spot customers, and who did that repricing?

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Detention and demurrage under-recovery

Symptom

Charges absorbed rather than contested or passed through, in periods when port and terminal dwell is rising

Financial signature

Gross profit per file falls as dwell rises, and the fall concentrates in the trade lanes and ports where dwell moved

Root cause

The charge is contestable, but contesting it requires assembling appointment, gate, and equipment evidence across systems inside a short window. No single owner holds the window, so claims lapse and the charge is booked as cost.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Navis N4, Tideworks (Mainsail / GateVision), Octopi, Turvo, Veson IMOS, CargoWise, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

Gross profit per file holds through a dwell increase, or the company's docket activity is in line with comparable-volume peers

Remediation

Automated evidence assembly and claim generation inside the dispute window. - What has been tried (failure history): consortium blockchain container milestone logging (consortium blockchain trade documentation, TradeLens) and real-time container visibility platforms (passive visibility control towers). - Why it failed: D&D disputes do not fail from a lack of timestamp tracking; they fail because ocean carriers and terminal operators reject claims lacking official equipment interchange receipts (EIR) and appointment cancellation logs under 46 CFR Part 541. Knowing a container is dwelling does not provide legal leverage to compel fee waivers. - Difficulty of remediation: automated claim generation is harder than it looks. Terminal operating systems actively restrict external API access to gate records and appointment logs; assembling admissible dispute evidence requires cross-system extraction from uncooperative counterparties inside short statutory windows.

Diagnostic question

"Who decides whether a demurrage charge gets contested, and how long do they have?"

Purchased-transportation accrual failure

Symptom

Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Visible from outside

  • Consequence evidence, not mechanism evidence: the Nasdaq listing deficiency notice (2026-08-20, Listing (c)(1)); securities litigation filings; and the Deutsche Bank impact estimate. These show that a failure occurred, that reports were delayed, and that the market repriced the company. They do not evidence the accrual-to-invoice locus.
  • Management remediation and supporting material: the disclosed Ernst & Young re-audit engagement, carrier settlement, freight payment audit and technical accounting roles in postings.

Financial signature

Reported operating margin overstated while cash is unaffected — the tell that separates this from a revenue or collections failure. approximately $77 million across the first three quarters of 2025, and management confirmed no impact on cash or operating cash flow in any period. The consequential costs are the larger part of the signature and are externally observable: delayed 10-K, two missed 10-Qs, shares −18% (2026-02-06) and −9.2% (2026-05-12), CFO and COO departures (2026-05-28), a securities litigation investigation, and a Nasdaq listing deficiency. See evidence of consequences does not evidence mechanism cause and accrual release inverts cost-ratio trend.

Root cause

(for the company's own words; for our operational mapping of them — and the mapping is the first thing to prematurely, booked incorrectly, or lacked adequate supporting documentation" in the historical ledgers, with an expected conclusion of ineffective disclosure controls and ineffective ICFR for FY2023 and FY2024. In operational terms this sits on the accrual-to-invoice reconciliation for purchased transportation: the accrued cost of a move and the carrier's eventual invoice are reconciled at period end, and the reconciliation was carrying an unsupported reduction to accounts payable. The company has not stated it that way. The mapping from "lacked adequate supporting documentation" to the PT accrual process is our inference and carries no better than.

Operational systems where this surfaces

TriumphPay, HubTran, Cass Information Systems, McLeod LoadMaster/PowerBroker, Trimble TMW

Falsified if

The understatement is confined to a single acquired entity or a single period and does not widen backwards, which points to a conversion or purchase-accounting error rather than a standing reconciliation failure; or the restatement also moves cash or operating cash flow, which makes it a different failure; or the disclosed cause names revenue recognition rather than expense accrual.

Remediation

Automated three-way match between tender, accrual and carrier invoice; exception queues on unsupported accrual adjustments; continuous reconciliation rather than period-end reconciliation.

Diagnostic question

"When a carrier's invoice arrives, what does it get matched against, and who clears the difference?"

Fragmented carrier master after acquisition

Symptom

Onboarding cycle time grows after an acquisition closes; duplicate carrier records; compliance exceptions rise

Financial signature

SG&A per load rises after close and has not reverted by the company's own stated synergy timetable; synergy targets restated, deferred, or dropped from later disclosure. Use the timetable the company published — do not assume a recovery period.

Root cause

Each acquired entity brought its own TMS and carrier master. There is no golden record, so carrier onboarding, compliance checking, and payment run separately per brand and the cost of each is duplicated.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Trimble Transportation (TMW.Suite / TruckMate / Innovative), McLeod Software

Falsified if

Authorities and bonds were consolidated on the company's stated schedule and SG&A per load reverts to the pre-close trend

Remediation

Entity resolution, golden record, single onboarding workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): multi-year enterprise ERP and master data management (MDM) consolidations (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: enterprise software suites treated carrier master consolidation as database deduplication rather than reconciling distinct operating authorities, insurance policies, and settlement terms.

Diagnostic question

"How many carrier masters do you have today?"

Quote-to-book manual handoff

Symptom

loads per rep per day flat or falling while volume grows

Financial signature

Personnel expense grows in line with volume rather than sub-linearly; SG&A as % of net revenue does not improve as volume scales

Root cause

Quoting, booking, tendering, and confirmation live in separate systems with no record passing between them, so scale is bought with headcount rather than throughput.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Revenova, McLeod PowerBroker, MercuryGate, Turvo

Falsified if

Loads per head improves as volume grows, or the flat ratio is explained by a disclosed mix shift into more complex freight

Remediation

quote-to-book workflow automation removing the re-keys. - What has been tried (failure history): automated two-sided digital matching (digital freight brokerage matching algorithms), maritime electronic documentation platforms (consortium blockchain trade documentation), and enterprise ERP re-platforming (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: automating standard booking touches saved only 3 to 5 minutes on the happy path, while operational exceptions (missed appointments, equipment failures, detention) required 30 to 45 minutes of manual rep intervention. - Difficulty of remediation: workflow automation removing re-keys is harder than it looks. Removing the initial data re-key leaves 15% to 25% of loads stranded in manual exception loops; unless exception handling itself is automated, personnel expense per load fails to decline with volume.

Diagnostic question

"How many systems does a rep touch between a customer asking for a rate and the load being tendered?"

Why manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility is not the explanation

manual carrier sourcing under rate volatility moves the price at which capacity is bought and shows up in net revenue per load. quote-to-book manual handoff leaves the buy price alone and moves the cost of processing the load. If net revenue per load is compressing while loads per head holds, the problem is sourcing, not the handoff.

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation

Symptom

Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Visible from outside

The disclosed lag and covered share are grade ` from the 10-K; the analyst exchanges are gradeC`. Realised rates tracking the input series with a lag is the mechanism. Realised rates tracking the freight rate index instead is the cycle. That test runs on public data and has not been run.

Financial signature

Operating margin compresses in a period of rising input prices while volume and utilisation are stable, and recovers with a lag rather than with volume. The tell is the pairing of a disclosed lag with a disclosed uncovered share. escalation clauses, or the customer pays for the fuel," with "generally a 30 to 120 day delay before contracts are adjusted"; spot contracts carry no fuel escalator and spot was 30% of inland marine revenue. sell-side analysts across the 2026 Q2 and Q3 cycle and answered qualitatively both times, with management redirecting to a market-driven rather than fuel-driven pricing environment.

Root cause

The escalator is a contractual formula computed and invoiced on a cycle, not a price the commercial team sets. Recovery fails in three distinct places. The formula is indexed to a series that moves differently from the actual input. The cycle resets after the cost lands. The billing recalculates and rebills by hand. Only the third is addressable operationally, and no operator located discloses which of the three binds.

Operational systems where this surfaces

SAP TM, Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

The operator discloses realised escalator capture at or near 100% across a full inflation cycle, or the uncovered revenue share is immaterial, or realised rate movements track a freight rate index rather than the input series.

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost

Symptom

The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Visible from outside

Models 8 and 9 rest on `` related-party and cost disclosure only.

Financial signature

Cost per unit of work on the purchased line rises during a volume decline, while the operator's own controllable cost lines hold flat per unit. The gap between the two is the mechanism. 34.2% of direct cargo expense and 12.5% of Ocean Transportation revenue. Terminal services cost $341.9m (2025), $320.9m (2024), $297.2m (2023): +15% over three years across which total container volume fell. Per FEU, $875 against $800, +9.4% in a year when volume fell 2.6%. Over the same period vessel operating expense per FEU was flat and general and administrative per FEU fell 0.5%. barges sits with a small number of yards; the operator runs its own yard at Channelview partly in response. Steel is disclosed as near historical highs and shipyard labour as expensive. This is the same structure on the capital line rather than the operating line, and thinner evidence than the Recorded as a second observation, not a second proof. - Already recorded for model 7 in operating models: Class I rail agreements with contractual minimum volume commitments, where volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers, evidenced through STB EP 770 and Ex Parte 711 dockets.

Root cause

The operator's cost base contains a block priced by a counterparty whose own fixed costs it must cover. When the operator's volume falls, the counterparty's cost per unit rises and is passed through. The operator holds an equity interest or a long contract, so it cannot re-tender. In the joint-venture case it also books its share of the counterparty's earnings inside its own cost line. The same relationship therefore flatters the income statement while inflating the cost per unit.

Falsified if

The purchased input's unit cost falls with the operator's volume, or the operator demonstrates it re-tendered the work, or the cost rise is explained by scope rather than by the counterparty's own cost base.

Build-operate-transfer commercial arrangements — A multi-year commercial structure where a provider designs, builds, and operates a specialized technology and operational capability under strict SLAs, subsequently transferring full ownership, staff, and intellectual property to the client

Forward-deployed engineer and embedded technical arrangements — A commercial engagement embedding senior software and data engineers directly inside client operations to build, deploy, and iterate production automation workflows in live operational environments

Per-transaction managed service or BPO — A commercial model where the provider takes operational ownership of a discrete process, charging a fixed fee per completed unit of work rather than selling software licenses or consulting hours

Recovery fee and contingency commercial structures — A purely performance-contingent commercial model where provider compensation is paid as a percentage of actual cash recovered or billing credits issued from disputed transportation charges

What has already failed

Consortium blockchain for global trade documentation and shipping — Centralized consortium blockchains failed in global container shipping because trade document friction is caused by maritime jurisdiction law and commercial carrier distrust, not by the absence of cryptographic ledgers

Passive visibility control towers without operational execution — Real-time transportation visibility platforms and control towers stalled across enterprise logistics because displaying shipment location data does not provide the contractual authority or physical capacity to resolve transit delays

Forensic craft insights

Specific observations on how this model behaves in practice, the preconditions for automation, and operational failure modes.

Accessorial dispute recovery requires contemporaneous operational evidence — Contesting logistics accessorials, detention, and demurrage cannot be solved retroactively because evidentiary standards demand data captured at the exact moment the operational failure occurs.

Accrual release inverts cost-ratio trend — A disclosed freight accrual release inflates base-year gross profit and can invert an operating cost ratio, turning an operational improver into an apparent deteriorator.

Capitalised software without process change inflates overhead — Capitalising internal software development inflates fixed administrative overhead and amortisation when operational exception handling and data entry remain manual.

Charges registers provide external records of forwarder receivables financing — Public charges registers provide dated, independent evidence of forwarder invoice discounting and factoring facilities, enabling external verification of management working-capital claims.

Cohort-wide ratio movement diagnoses the cycle not the operator — When an operational cost or margin ratio moves uniformly across an entire peer cohort, the movement diagnoses the market freight cycle, not an individual operator's failure.

Consortium governance deadlocks inter-competitor data sharing — Centralized platform consortia in logistics fail from commercial distrust over proprietary customer and rate data, not from technical limitations of the data-sharing protocol.

Deliberate mix migration renders consolidated gross margin uninterpretable — When an operator intentionally migrates customer or mode mix, consolidated gross margin percentage movements reflect portfolio transition rather than core operational execution.

Exception resolution requires commercial authority not workflow software — Operational exception resolution cannot be automated purely through software workflows because clearing a transit deviation requires commercial authority to spend money or absorb capacity losses.

Interest payable against receivables prices working capital extension — Rising interest payable alongside expanding debtor days provides the external financial signature that proves a forwarder's receivable extension is being actively financed.

Mixed turnover denominators mask forwarder margin compression — Mixing statutory turnover and ex-duty turnover across disclosure tables masks core margin compression and invalidates peer margin comparisons.

SIC codes fail to distinguish transport operating models — Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes bundle fundamentally different transport operating models under identical numbers, making code-derived peer sets invalid.

Total debtors overstates forwarder collection period — Computing debtor days from total balance-sheet debtors heavily overstates the collection period for customs-active freight forwarders.

Trade creditor totals conceal purchased transportation payables — In UK statutory filed accounts, trade creditors are bundled into aggregate short-term liabilities, preventing external calculation of days payable outstanding or supplier terms.

Visibility platforms fail at carrier data capture not software layer — Real-time supply chain visibility platforms stall because long-tail carrier telematics and batch EDI feeds fail to provide clean, timely data, not because tracking software or predictive algorithms are defective.
