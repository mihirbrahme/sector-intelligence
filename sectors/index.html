Sectors

Thirteen operating models. The model determines the unit of work, the valid peer set and the denominator on which a margin means anything. Establish it before anything else.

Brokers & Forwarders (Non-Asset)

Non-asset capacity intermediaries. Evaluated strictly on net revenue spread and loads or shipment files per operator. Personnel is the dominant cost.

  • Truckload Brokeragethe loadbuys capacity from carriers, sells it to shippers, keeps the spread.
  • Freight Forwarding / NVOCCthe shipment filebuys space from ocean/air carriers, sells to shippers, manages the documentation and customs chain.

Trucking & Rail Fleets (Inland Asset)

Motor carriers, LTL terminal networks, and rail intermodal. Capital is in rolling stock and cross-docks. Evaluated on operating ratio (OR) and loaded asset productivity.

  • Asset Carriage / Truckload Fleetthe loaded mile, or the linehaul tripowns tractors, trailers, and employs drivers.
  • LTLthe shipment, or the hundredweightconsolidates partial loads through a terminal network.
  • Domestic Intermodalthe intermodal load — one container moved end to endbuys rail linehaul in bulk under multi-year contracts with the Class I railroads, owns or leases the container and chassis fleet, and arranges drayage at both ends.

Warehouses & Port Terminals (Facilities)

Dedicated facilities and gateway infrastructure. Multi-year contracts or concessions. Evaluated on cost per order line or crane moves per hour.

  • Contract Logistics / 3PL Warehousingthe order lineoperates warehouses and fulfilment under multi-year contracts, usually cost-plus or open-book with productivity commitments
  • Ports & Terminalsthe box movecharges for moves, storage, and berth access.

Ships & Barges (Marine)

Vessels, tank barges, and offshore fleets. Multi-decade balance sheet assets. Economics turn on revenue-days, time charter rates, and dry-dock management.

  • Inland liquid-bulk marine / tank bargethe crewed towboat-dayowns tank barges and the towboats that push them, crews both, and sells liquid-bulk capacity on the US inland waterway system and coastwise.
  • Protected liner shipping / Jones Act non-contiguous tradesthe container liftedoperates US-flagged, US-built, US-crewed vessels on fixed day-of-week schedules between the mainland and the non-contiguous states and territories, where cabotage law bars foreign-flag competition.
  • International container liner and feeder shippingthe loaded container carried, normally TEUoperates container vessels on scheduled international strings.
  • Dry bulk ocean logistics and charteringthe loaded voyagecontracts to move dry bulk cargoes, then performs the voyage with owned vessels, chartered-in vessels, or spot tonnage.
  • Energy shipping: LNG, crude, product, offshore FPSO/FSOthe contracted vessel-day or offshore asset-dayowns or operates specialised energy marine assets and sells availability under long-term charters, contracts of affreightment, or offshore production and storage contracts.
