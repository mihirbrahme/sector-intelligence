In 90 seconds

The business operates scheduled all-cargo aircraft fleets carrying express parcels, freight pallets, and mail. Revenue is earned on gross and dimensional weight under published cargo tariffs and corporate service contracts. The unit of work is the Available Ton-Mile, and every cost and every margin on this page is read against it.

Performance is measured on freight and cargo revenue. The cost base is flight crew salaries, aviation fuel, central hub sort personnel, aircraft heavy maintenance, aircraft depreciation and lease expense.

The valid peer set is FedEx Express, UPS Airlines, Cargolux, Polar Air Cargo. It is never compared to passenger belly-cargo operations. Dedicated freighters must cover full aircraft operating costs, whereas belly cargo treats freight as marginal revenue over passenger payload.

The common misreadTreating air freight as homogeneous weight. Volumetric cube constraints frequently cube out an aircraft before gross payload weight limits are reached.

The operating model

The model divides into 3 sub-sectors, set out below. They share a unit of work and diverge on what it costs to produce, which is why a single margin for the model as a whole says little.

  • Primary margin metric: cargo operating margin, paired with freight yield per Revenue Ton-Mile and cost per ATM.

  • Key productivity metrics: freight yield per RTM, cost per ATM, cargo load factor (weight and volume), block hours per freighter, on-time sort dispatch rate.

Night sort hub mechanics and payload density physics

Express air cargo carriers operate around overnight hub sorting windows. Aircraft fly into centralized sort hubs (e.g. Memphis for FedEx, Louisville for UPS) between 22:00 and 01:00. Shipments are mechanically sorted and reloaded for outbound flights departing before 04:00.

Air cargo economics balance gross weight against volumetric cubic capacity. Low-density e-commerce freight fills an aircraft's internal volume before maximum structural payload weight is reached. Cargo carriers charge dimensional weight tariffs to protect yield against low-density freight.

Financial signature

  • Aircraft daily utilization is structurally lower than passenger jets: approx 4–7 block hours/day. Flying is concentrated into overnight express windows. Freighters sit parked during daytime hours.
  • Fleet strategy relies on converted passenger airframes (P2F). Because daily flight hours are low, carriers avoid new-build aircraft capital costs. They convert depreciated passenger widebodies into freighters.
  • High exposure to jet fuel price volatility. Fuel represents 25–35% of flight operating expenses. Exposure is managed through indexed customer fuel surcharges.
  • Heavy balance sheet capitalisation in aircraft and automated sorting infrastructure. FedEx FY2026 total revenue reached $94,720m, with massive asset commitments in freighter fleets and automated air hubs.

The numbers that carry meaning

These are the measures that survive contact with this model. Read against any other model they mean something different, or nothing at all.

Freight Yield per Revenue Ton-Mile

Definition

Cargo freight revenue divided by Revenue Ton-Miles (RTMs) flown.

Why it matters

The clean unit revenue metric for dedicated freighters and air cargo networks.

Moves up

Global manufacturing supply chain bottlenecks; semiconductor or automotive emergency shipments; high fuel surcharges.

Moves down

Return of passenger international belly capacity; soft global industrial demand; modal shift to container shipping.

Where to find it

Form 10-K freight segment disclosures; IATA Air Cargo Market Analysis monthly reports.

Common misread

Treating ton-mile yields as uniform across routes. Eastbound trans-Pacific routes command multiples of westbound return yields due to directional trade imbalances.

Diagnostic question

"What is your freight yield on backhaul return flights compared to primary export trade lanes?"

Block Hour Operating Cost

Definition

Total aircraft operating expenses divided by revenue block hours flown (from gate pushback to destination gate arrival).

Why it matters

The fundamental operating transaction cost in regional capacity agreements and ACMI charter contracts.

Moves up

Pilot wage rate step-ups; engine shop visit overhauls; unscheduled line maintenance; ground handling fee inflation.

Moves down

Operational scale; engine durability improvements; streamlined line maintenance rotations.

Where to find it

US DOT BTS Form 41 Schedule P-5.2; ACMI carrier 10-K filings.

Common misread

Comparing widebody block hour costs to regional jet block hour costs. Fuel burn and crew complement differ by an order of magnitude.

Diagnostic question

"What is your hourly cost variance when maintenance shop visits exceed scheduled turn times?"

Aircraft Daily Utilization

Definition

Average block hours flown per aircraft per day across the active operating fleet.

Why it matters

The primary driver of capital asset turnover. Higher utilization dilutes fixed aircraft depreciation and lease payments over more seat miles.

Moves up

Quick turnarounds at gates; red-eye flights; point-to-point network routing without hub bank waits.

Moves down

Congested hub delays; maintenance groundings; pilot availability shortages; winter weather cancellations.

Where to find it

Form 10-K fleet operating statistics tables; US DOT Form 41 reports.

Common misread

Maximizing aircraft utilization without tracking yield. Running aircraft on marginal off-peak routes lowers unit cost but destroys network PRASM.

Diagnostic question

"How many daily block hours does your fleet lose to hub turnaround banking buffers and airport taxi dwell?"

Days Sales Outstanding

Definition

Receivables ÷ revenue × 365

Why it matters

Brokers pay carriers fast and collect from shippers slowly. The gap is why freight factoring exists. A widening gap is a cash mechanism.

Moves up

Customer mix to large shippers with long terms; billing exceptions and disputes; invoice error rates

Moves down

Invoice accuracy; automated freight audit; collections discipline

Where to find it

Balance sheet and income statement, computable for any filer

Common misread

Treating it as a finance problem. It is usually a billing data quality problem.

Diagnostic question

Of the invoices you send in a week, how many come back queried, and what are the top three things they are queried about?

Cost per Available Seat Mile excluding fuel / CASM ex-fuel

Definition

Total airline operating expenses less aircraft fuel and related taxes, divided by total Available Seat Miles (ASMs).

Why it matters

Isolates controllable operational cost performance from jet fuel commodity price swings. The baseline diagnostic metric for airline operational efficiency.

Moves up

Pilot and flight attendant wage increases; maintenance check cost inflation; reduced aircraft utilization; weather disruption re-accommodations.

Moves down

Labor productivity gains; seat density increases (cabin densification); fixed asset dilution over expanded flight schedules.

Where to find it

Form 10-K MD&A non-GAAP reconciliations; US DOT Form 41 Schedule P-1.2.

Common misread

Comparing CASM ex-fuel across carriers with different stage lengths. Short flights concentrate takeoff and landing costs over fewer miles.

Diagnostic question

"How much of your CASM ex-fuel increase this year was driven by collective bargaining wage step-ups versus operational schedule degradation?"

Stage-Length Adjusted CASM

Definition

Unit operating cost normalized to a standard industry flight distance (typically 1,000 miles) using an empirical stage-length square-root adjustment curve.

Why it matters

Removes distance distortion from cost comparisons. Fixed departure expenses (landing fees, pushback, takeoff fuel) dilute naturally over longer flights.

Moves up

Inefficient ground operations or bloated station staffing relative to peer flight distances.

Moves down

Optimized turnaround processes; efficient secondary airport operations.

Where to find it

Modelled from Form 10-K stage length and CASM disclosures, or MIT Airline Data Project / Bureau of Transportation Statistics analyses.

Common misread

Using unadjusted CASM to claim a long-haul carrier is inherently more efficient than a short-haul regional operator.

Diagnostic question

"When benchmarked on a stage-length adjusted curve, does your cost advantage hold against point-to-point operators?"

Passenger Revenue per Available Seat Mile / PRASM

Definition

Passenger revenue divided by total Available Seat Miles (ASMs), equivalent to passenger yield multiplied by passenger load factor.

Why it matters

The clean measure of passenger unit pricing and seat monetization.

Moves up

Strong business travel demand; effective revenue management yield optimization; capacity discipline in competitive markets.

Moves down

Competitive fare wars; overcapacity in leisure routes; macroeconomic travel slowdown.

Where to find it

Form 10-K operating statistics tables; quarterly investor presentations.

Common misread

Confusing PRASM with TRASM. PRASM excludes cargo and high-margin co-brand loyalty cash remuneration.

Diagnostic question

"Did your PRASM improvement reflect higher average fares paid per mile, or simply fuller seats at discounted yields?"

Total Revenue per Available Seat Mile / TRASM

Definition

Total airline operating revenue (including passenger tickets, cargo, and loyalty partner remuneration) divided by total Available Seat Miles.

Why it matters

Reflects total commercial revenue production per unit of flying capacity deployed.

Moves up

Growth in high-margin co-branded credit card spend; belly cargo yield spikes; premium cabin product mix migration.

Moves down

Discounting on economy cabin fares; lower loyalty remuneration; cargo rate softness.

Where to find it

Form 10-K operating statistics tables; investor updates.

Common misread

Assuming TRASM increases are driven solely by ticket pricing. Premium seating and loyalty programs represent over half of total revenue for network carriers.

Diagnostic question

"What portion of your TRASM spread over competitors originates from bank co-brand partner payments rather than ticket revenue?"

Completion Factor

Definition

Percentage of scheduled flights completed without cancellation, calculated as completed flights divided by scheduled flights.

Why it matters

The definitive metric of operational network integrity. Sub-98% completion factors cause exponential re-accommodation and crew positioning costs.

Moves up

Spare aircraft availability; buffer crew reserves; favorable seasonal weather.

Moves down

Severe weather events; FAA ground stops; crew duty-time legal expirations; maintenance unserviceability.

Where to find it

US DOT Air Travel Consumer Reports (ATCR); company monthly operational releases.

Common misread

Assuming a 99% completion factor means operations ran smoothly. Canceling 1% of flights at a major hub stranded tens of thousands of connecting passengers.

Diagnostic question

"What was the fully loaded cost per cancellation when factoring in crew re-pairing and DOT passenger compensation?"

On-Time Arrival / A14

Definition

Percentage of scheduled flight arrivals reaching the gate within 14 minutes of published schedule arrival time.

Why it matters

The federal regulatory standard for airline punctuality. Chronic delays incur passenger compensation and hub gate gridlock.

Moves up

Schedule block time padding; favorable runway conditions; early morning departure discipline.

Moves down

Airport ground delay programs; gate congestion; maintenance deferral paperwork delays.

Where to find it

US DOT Air Travel Consumer Reports monthly tables.

Common misread

Achieving high A14 by inflating scheduled block time. Schedule padding increases crew pay and reduces aircraft utilization.

Diagnostic question

"How many minutes of schedule block padding did your planners add last quarter to protect on-time arrival metrics?"

What recurs

Failures that show up repeatedly in this model, each with a financial signature that can be read from the outside.

Escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation

Symptom

Input costs move in double digits while contract renewals and realised rates move in low single digits or negative, and the operator attributes the margin movement to timing rather than to price. Two disclosed sub-conditions distinguish it from ordinary rate weakness: a stated reset lag, and a share of revenue carrying no escalator at all.

Visible from outside

The disclosed lag and covered share are grade ` from the 10-K; the analyst exchanges are gradeC`. Realised rates tracking the input series with a lag is the mechanism. Realised rates tracking the freight rate index instead is the cycle. That test runs on public data and has not been run.

Financial signature

Operating margin compresses in a period of rising input prices while volume and utilisation are stable, and recovers with a lag rather than with volume. The tell is the pairing of a disclosed lag with a disclosed uncovered share. escalation clauses, or the customer pays for the fuel," with "generally a 30 to 120 day delay before contracts are adjusted"; spot contracts carry no fuel escalator and spot was 30% of inland marine revenue. sell-side analysts across the 2026 Q2 and Q3 cycle and answered qualitatively both times, with management redirecting to a market-driven rather than fuel-driven pricing environment.

Root cause

The escalator is a contractual formula computed and invoiced on a cycle, not a price the commercial team sets. Recovery fails in three distinct places. The formula is indexed to a series that moves differently from the actual input. The cycle resets after the cost lands. The billing recalculates and rebills by hand. Only the third is addressable operationally, and no operator located discloses which of the three binds.

Operational systems where this surfaces

SAP TM, Blue Yonder, Oracle OTM, Cass Information Systems

Falsified if

The operator discloses realised escalator capture at or near 100% across a full inflation cycle, or the uncovered revenue share is immaterial, or realised rate movements track a freight rate index rather than the input series.

Belly cargo capacity volatility from passenger load preemption

Symptom

Commercial belly-cargo shipments bumped or rejected at origin gates despite confirmed booking allocations.

Visible from outside

Airline cargo revenue volatility on stable passenger ASMs; freight forwarder service dispute records.

Financial signature

High rate of freight credit notes, claims for offloaded cargo, and loss of premium forwarder cargo contracts.

Root cause

Passenger baggage, extra fuel reserves for weather, and high passenger load factors consume aircraft maximum takeoff weight and cargo hold volume. Belly cargo takes lowest operational payload priority.

Falsified if

Cargo rejections correlate strictly with forwarder late gate deliveries or documentation defects.

Remediation

Dynamic payload prediction algorithms incorporating real-time passenger baggage check weights; dedicated cargo capacity buffers on long-haul routes.

Diagnostic question

"What percentage of booked belly cargo was offloaded due to passenger baggage or fuel weight limits last quarter?"

Discriminating question

"How accurately does your dispatch system forecast available cargo payload weight prior to passenger gate check-in?"

Billing exception leakage

Symptom

DSO widens without a change in customer mix or payment terms

Financial signature

Receivables grow faster than revenue; credit notes and write-offs rise; the gap between delivery and a clean invoice widens, which pushes cash out before any dispute has occurred. Take DSO per load, or net it against disclosed revenue per load, before reading it — see the rate-level falsifier below.

Root cause

Two distinct breaks, both upstream of finance. First, proof of delivery is captured late or incompletely, so the invoice cannot be raised on time. Second, the invoice that is raised carries data errors from the operational system, so a proportion are disputed and each dispute is worked by hand. Both are treated as collections problems when they are data capture and data quality problems.

Operational systems where this surfaces

Cass Information Systems, TriumphPay, HubTran, Denim, SAP TM, Oracle OTM, Blue Yonder, CargoWise, UK Customs Software Layer

Falsified if

  • The DSO movement is explained by a disclosed change in customer mix, payment terms, or a factoring facility being unwound; or
  • the DSO movement is explained by the rate level — receivables inflating with revenue per load rather than with volume or with any billing failure. In an inflecting rate environment this is the normal case, not the exception. Any billing exception leakage diagnosis made in 2026 without first netting out the rate move is unsafe. See rate inflation inflates working capital without billing leakage.

Remediation

POD capture automation; upstream data validation so invoices go out first-time-right; freight audit and dispute workflow. - What has been tried (failure history): geofenced automated arrival/departure tracking (passive visibility control towers) and monolithic ERP automated billing engines (monolithic ERP and WMS rip-and-replace). - Why it failed: GPS departure pings cannot substitute for signed delivery receipts, bill-of-lading exception stamps, or lumper receipts required by customer AP departments; corrupted upstream master data caused automated ERP billing engines to generate thousands of disputed invoices daily. - Difficulty of remediation: upstream validation is substantially harder than it looks. Physical proof of delivery is controlled by third-party drivers and dock receiving clerks outside the operator's systems; automated invoicing workflows stall whenever physical paperwork carries handwritten accessorial annotations.

Diagnostic questions

  • "What proportion of your invoices go out right the first time?"
  • "How many days between delivery and a clean invoice going out?"
  • "How much of your receivables growth is rate and how much is days?"

Post-restructuring capability gap

Symptom

Layoffs announced, then volume recovers, then service metrics deteriorate. All three, in that order. The third is the one that makes it this mechanism, and it is the one most often missing from disclosure.

Financial signature

SG&A falls then rises; overtime and contractor spend grows. Net of severance — see the counterexample.

Root cause

Headcount removed without the process change that would let the remaining team absorb the work. Institutional knowledge left with them.

Falsified if

The reduction is accompanied by a disclosed process change that removes the work rather than redistributing it, and productivity per head rises through the volume recovery; or the SG&A rebound is fully explained by disclosed severance; or the volume recovery is too small to test the remaining capacity.

Remediation

Workflow automation targeted at the removed capacity

Diagnostic question

"What work did you stop doing after the reduction, and what came back?"

Purchased-transportation accrual failure

Symptom

Purchased transportation expense and accounts payable are both understated, discovered at a year-end close rather than at any period close during the year, and the restatement then widens backwards into prior years rather than staying confined to the year in which it was found.

Visible from outside

  • Consequence evidence, not mechanism evidence: the Nasdaq listing deficiency notice (2026-08-20, Listing (c)(1)); securities litigation filings; and the Deutsche Bank impact estimate. These show that a failure occurred, that reports were delayed, and that the market repriced the company. They do not evidence the accrual-to-invoice locus.

Operational systems where this surfaces

TriumphPay, HubTran, Cass Information Systems, McLeod LoadMaster/PowerBroker, Trimble TMW

Falsified if

The understatement is confined to a single acquired entity or a single period and does not widen backwards, which points to a conversion or purchase-accounting error rather than a standing reconciliation failure; or the restatement also moves cash or operating cash flow, which makes it a different failure; or the disclosed cause names revenue recognition rather than expense accrual.

Diagnostic question

"When a carrier's invoice arrives, what does it get matched against, and who clears the difference?"

Uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost

Symptom

The operator's largest purchased cost line fails to flex with its own volume, because the supplier is a monopoly, a duopoly, or a joint venture the operator does not control. Unit cost of the input rises while the operator's volume falls.

Visible from outside

Models 8 and 9 rest on `` related-party and cost disclosure only.

Financial signature

Cost per unit of work on the purchased line rises during a volume decline, while the operator's own controllable cost lines hold flat per unit. The gap between the two is the mechanism. 34.2% of direct cargo expense and 12.5% of Ocean Transportation revenue. Terminal services cost $341.9m (2025), $320.9m (2024), $297.2m (2023): +15% over three years across which total container volume fell. Per FEU, $875 against $800, +9.4% in a year when volume fell 2.6%. Over the same period vessel operating expense per FEU was flat and general and administrative per FEU fell 0.5%. barges sits with a small number of yards; the operator runs its own yard at Channelview partly in response. Steel is disclosed as near historical highs and shipyard labour as expensive. This is the same structure on the capital line rather than the operating line, and thinner evidence than the Recorded as a second observation, not a second proof. - Already recorded for model 7 in operating models: Class I rail agreements with contractual minimum volume commitments, where volume shortfalls trigger higher rate tiers, evidenced through STB EP 770 and Ex Parte 711 dockets.

Root cause

The operator's cost base contains a block priced by a counterparty whose own fixed costs it must cover. When the operator's volume falls, the counterparty's cost per unit rises and is passed through. The operator holds an equity interest or a long contract, so it cannot re-tender. In the joint-venture case it also books its share of the counterparty's earnings inside its own cost line. The same relationship therefore flatters the income statement while inflating the cost per unit.

Falsified if

The purchased input's unit cost falls with the operator's volume, or the operator demonstrates it re-tendered the work, or the cost rise is explained by scope rather than by the counterparty's own cost base.

Crew legality cascade under irregular operations

Symptom

Flight cancellations multiply non-linearly on the second and third days of an irregular operations (IROPS) weather event, continuing after weather has cleared.

Visible from outside

US DOT Air Travel Consumer Reports monthly cancellation rates and tarmac delay reports; Form 10-K disclosures of IROPS disruption costs. Second class is FAA Part 121 / 117 compliance reporting.

Financial signature

Surge in premium crew overtime pay, hotel accommodations, passenger vouchers, and quarterly CASM ex-fuel escalation.

Root cause

FAA Part 117 flight duty period limits expire during initial weather holds. Downstream flight pairings break when crew rest clocks reset away from base. Legacy pairing re-optimization software fails to solve large-scale disruption recovery in real time.

Falsified if

Cancellations correlate strictly with runway closures and recover within 12 hours of weather clearing without crew displacement.

Remediation

Automated real-time crew recovery solvers; pre-positioned reserve crew buffers at secondary hubs; decoupling long duty rotations during storm windows.

Diagnostic question

"How many hours after a major weather event clears does your flight completion factor return to 99%?"

Discriminating question

"What proportion of second-day cancellations were caused by missing flight crew rather than out-of-position aircraft?"

Stage-length dilution distorting unit cost benchmarking

Symptom

Short-haul carrier appears structurally uncompetitive against long-haul peers on unadjusted Cost per Available Seat Mile (CASM).

Visible from outside

Form 10-K CASM disclosures plotted against average stage length in miles. Second class is BTS Form 41 Schedule P-1.2 segment reporting.

Financial signature

Reported CASM ex-fuel sits 20–30% above peer averages, while route-level operating margins remain healthy or superior.

Root cause

Fixed departure costs (landing fees, terminal gate pushback, climb fuel, gate staffing) amortize across fewer miles on short stage lengths. Linear CASM comparisons penalize short-haul networks.

Falsified if

The carrier's unit cost penalty persists after applying the empirical stage-length square-root adjustment curve.

Remediation

Stage-length adjustment of internal and peer benchmarks; route portfolio profitability evaluation on trip contribution rather than unadjusted CASM.

Diagnostic question

"Do your internal unit cost targets adjust for changes in network average stage length?"

Discriminating question

"How much of your CASM differential against competitors disappears when normalized to a 1,000-mile flight length?"
