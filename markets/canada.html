What the regulator controls

Canadian transport regulation splits three ways: federal for extra-provincial carriage and for rail and marine, provincial for intra-provincial carriage, and bilateral for anything crossing into the US. A Canadian operator is usually subject to all three at once.

Where the cost differs

The freight is north-south before it is east-west. The population sits within a few hundred kilometres of the US border, so the highest-value lanes cross a customs frontier rather than running the 5,000 km domestic corridor.

Rail is a duopoly, not an oligopoly. Two Class I railways serve the whole country. In Model 7 that removes the substitute that makes a purchased input behave like a purchased input.

Private companies file nothing. Canada has neither US-style quarterly disclosure for private firms nor the UK obligation to file a profit and loss account. What is not listed is invisible.

The freight task

Trucking dominates by value, and most of that value crosses the border. Rail dominates by tonne-kilometre on the domestic east-west and Prairie-to-port corridors. The two rarely compete for the same freight.

Freight task Typical mode Why
Ontario and Quebec to the US Midwest and Northeast Transborder truckload and LTL Short lanes, high frequency, manufacturing and automotive. Customs is the constraint, not distance.
Prairie grain, potash, coal to port Class I unit train Captive origins, regulated western grain revenue, no road alternative.
Domestic east-west general freight Rail intermodal, with truck at both ends 4,000-plus km between Toronto and Vancouver. Rail wins on cost; there is no third railway to price against.
Intra-provincial distribution Road, multi-drop Provincially regulated, short haul, urban.
Iron ore, grain, salt, aggregate on the Lakes and St. Lawrence Great Lakes and Seaway bulk Seasonal, lock-constrained, binational. Algoma.

Northern and remote resupply Winter road, barge, air Winter roads operate roughly January to March. There is no year-round alternative.

The St. Lawrence Seaway navigation season runs roughly from late March to late December. Ice dates set the season, the season sets revenue days, and revenue days set the year for a Model 13 operator. That is why lay-up and fit-out timing is the first thing to read in an Algoma disclosure, and it is already the stated watch in the monitoring plan.

The concentration reading. Rail is a duopoly. LTL is concentrated in two listed groups plus a handful of private networks. Truckload is fragmented, as everywhere. The unusual case is Model 7: a Canadian intermodal operator faces two possible suppliers nationally and typically one on a given lane, which is a tighter constraint than the US six-railway position and much tighter than anything in Asia-Pacific.

Regulation

Canadian transport regulation splits three ways: federal for extra-provincial carriage and for rail and marine, provincial for intra-provincial carriage, and bilateral for anything crossing into the US. A Canadian operator is usually subject to all three at once.

CBSA and CARM

The Canada Border Services Agency administers the Customs Act. The material change of the last decade is CARM.

CARM — the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management system — became the system of record for commercial imports with its Release 2 launch on 2024-10-21. What it changed is not the declaration; it is who carries the money and the liability.

  • Every commercial importer must register on the CARM Client Portal and hold a business number with an import-export program account.
  • Release Prior to Payment now requires the importer's own financial security — a surety bond or a cash deposit — posted on the importer's own account.
  • Before CARM, a customs broker could extend its own bond to any importer that wanted it. From 2025-05-20 that is no longer permitted. An importer without its own security does not get release prior to payment.
  1. A revenue line disappeared from Canadian customs brokerage. Lending a bond was a service, and it was priced. Its removal is a structural change to the Model 2 revenue mix in this market, not a cycle event.
  2. It is the mirror image of the UK duty deferment position. In the UK the forwarder still advances the importer's duty and carries 70 to 80 debtor days for it — see United Kingdom and forwarding receivable extension funded on gross turnover. In Canada the regulator moved that exposure back onto the importer. Same underlying question, opposite regulatory answer, and the difference shows up directly in a forwarder's receivable days and in whether it needs a facility at all.

Other CBSA mechanics that shape operations:

  • Advance Commercial Information / eManifest. Cargo and conveyance data must reach CBSA before arrival: at least one hour ahead for highway, two hours for rail, and 24 hours before loading for marine containers. A late or rejected transmission is a border delay, not a paperwork query.
  • Harmonised billing. CARM moved importers onto a monthly statement of account with a single payment due date, which shifts the working-capital cycle to a fixed monthly rhythm.
  • Trusted trader programs. Partners in Protection on the Canadian side, CTPAT on the US side, and FAST lanes for approved carriers and drivers. FAST eligibility is a real throughput asset on a high-frequency lane and a real cost to maintain.
  • CUSMA, and the joint review that has already happened. In force since 2020-07-01. Certification of origin has no prescribed form but requires nine specified data elements. The first joint review under Article 34.7 was held on 2026-07-01, and the United States declined to confirm its intention to extend the agreement for the further sixteen years, saying it would not renew in its current form.

What that does and does not mean. The agreement has not lapsed. It remains fully in force and now runs to 2036-07-01 unless extended. What changed is the review cadence: Article 34.7.4 triggers a joint review every year until the parties agree an extension or the agreement expires. The sixteen-year extension stays available at any time on written confirmation by all three parties.

For this record the consequence is structural, not immediate. Canadian transborder freight now sits on an agreement with a defined and shortening horizon and an annual political checkpoint, rather than on a settled long-term framework. Tariff and rules-of-origin risk becomes a recurring annual event to monitor rather than a once-in-six-years one. US tariff actions in 2025 already disrupted these flows independently of the review.

The Transportation Modernization Act

Bill C-49, in force from 2018, amended the Canada Transportation Act, the Railway Safety Act and the Canada Marine Act. Its rail provisions are the reason a Canadian shipper has any bargaining position against a two-railway market.

  • Long-haul interswitching. A shipper with access to only one railway could obtain a regulated rate to an interchange with a competing carrier, up to 1,200 km or 50% of the total haul, whichever was greater. This provision sunset in 2021.
  • The extended interswitching pilot has also expired. A 160 km limit for Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta was enacted through the Budget Implementation Act, 2023, came into force 2023-09-20, and sunset on 2025-03-20 after its eighteen-month term. An earlier 160 km extension ran from 2014 to 2016 and was allowed to lapse the same way.

So the position today is the statutory 30 km radius, and nothing else. A captive Prairie shipper has no extended remedy in force. The Canadian Transportation Agency issued a determination on 2026-03-09 from its statutory review of the Railway Interswitching Regulations, which sets the regulated rates within that 30 km radius but does not extend it. Reviving or making permanent a longer radius is under active political contest, with grain shippers pressing for a permanent measure at up to 500 km and the railways opposing.

This is the single most important regulatory fact in Canadian rail freight and it is easy to get wrong, because the pilot is widely written about in the present tense in material published before March 2025. applies directly: a lapsed remedy described as current is a wrong answer wearing a source. - Reciprocal financial penalties in service level agreements. Before C-49 a shipper could be penalised for missing a commitment and the railway could not. Now both directions are available, which changes what a service agreement is worth. - A statutory definition of adequate and suitable service, against which the Canadian Transportation Agency adjudicates. - Data and performance reporting. Class I railways file service and performance data with the CTA and Transport Canada. This is Canada's analogue of the US STB Docket EP 770, and it is the external series against which a Model 7 operator's purchased linehaul performance can be read. - Maximum Revenue Entitlement for western grain was retained, with modifications intended to incentivise hopper car investment. - Locomotive voice and video recorders were mandated under the Railway Safety Act amendments. - Marine. C-49 relaxed the Coasting Trade Act to permit vessels of any registry to reposition empty containers between Canadian ports, and to allow vessels registered in CETA states to perform certain feeder and dredging services. That is a narrow, specific carve-out with a direct Model 10 and Model 13 consequence, and it has no US equivalent — the Jones Act admits nothing like it.

Cross-border filing and MJDS

This decides what a Canadian public company's disclosure actually looks like, and it is easy to get wrong.

  • SEDAR+ replaced SEDAR in 2023 and is the filing system for every Canadian reporting issuer, run by the Canadian Securities Administrators.
  • National Instrument 51-102 sets continuous disclosure: an annual information form, annual and interim financial statements with MD&A, and material change reports within ten days of the change.
  • The Multijurisdictional Disclosure System lets an eligible Canadian issuer satisfy US registration and reporting using its Canadian documents. An MJDS filer lodges a Form 40-F on EDGAR — containing the Canadian AIF, MD&A and IFRS financial statements — rather than a 10-K or a 20-F.

What follows for analysis:

  1. Do not expect US-format segment tables from a Canadian dual-listed operator. The EDGAR document is the Canadian one. Segment presentation follows IFRS 8, not US GAAP, and margin and lease lines are not directly comparable to a 10-K filer's without restatement — see IFRS taxonomy is mandatory for international EDGAR filers.
  2. Interim cadence is Canadian. MJDS filers do not file 10-Qs. Quarterly interim statements and MD&A go to SEDAR+ and are furnished to the SEC.
  3. Ownership movement is visible later and less often. The Canadian early warning threshold under NI 62-104 is 10%, against 5% for a Schedule 13D or 13G in the US. A position that would be disclosed in the US can be built invisibly in Canada.
  4. Private companies disclose nothing. No filed accounts, no turnover, no headcount. This is the sharpest contrast with United Kingdom, where a £60m private forwarder files audited revenue. Day & Ross, Bison and Canada Cartage are structurally unreadable.

Trucking: safety, hours and labour

  • Jurisdiction. Extra-provincial carriers are federally regulated but administered provincially through the National Safety Code. A carrier holds an NSC safety fitness certificate issued by its base province. Intra-provincial operations are provincial throughout.
  • Hours of service differ from the US and the difference is material. Canadian federal rules allow 13 hours driving within a 14-hour on-duty window after 10 hours off duty, with cycle limits of 70 hours in 7 days or 120 hours in 14. The US allows 11 driving in a 14-hour window, 60 in 7 or 70 in 8. A transborder driver changes rule sets at the border. Planning a transborder lane on Canadian hours produces a schedule that is illegal on the US side.
  • ELDs are third-party certified. The mandate has applied to federally regulated carriers since 2021-06-12, with full enforcement from 2023-01-01. Unlike the US self-certification regime, hardware and software must be tested and certified by a certification body accredited by the Minister of Transport before it can be listed and used. The Canadian device register is therefore a stronger signal than the US one, and a fleet running a US-only self-certified device is not compliant north of the border.
  • Mandatory Entry-Level Training for Class 1 licences is required across the provinces that have adopted it, which raises the cost of putting a new driver in a seat.
  • "Driver Inc." is the Canadian labour-cost distortion with no direct US or UK equivalent at this scale. Carriers pay drivers through incorporated personal service businesses, avoiding source deductions, CPP and EI contributions and overtime obligations. Federal enforcement by CRA and by Employment and Social Development Canada has been stepped up, with dedicated inspection funding. The analytical consequence is that a compliant carrier's driver cost per mile and a non-compliant competitor's are not the same quantity, and the market rate is set partly by the non-compliant base. Any Canadian driver-cost benchmark carries this contamination and should say so.

Carbon, fuel and environmental cost

  • The federal fuel charge was set to zero effective 2025-04-01, by regulations registered in March 2025 (SOR/2025-107). Rates for all fuel types and combustible waste went to zero after 2025-03-31, and existing fuel-charge registrations were cancelled on 2025-11-01. The charge had applied in Newfoundland and Labrador, Prince Edward Island, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, Nunavut and Yukon. The industrial output-based pricing system for large emitters remains.
  • Clean Fuel Regulations, in force since 2023, impose carbon-intensity reduction obligations on fuel producers and importers. The cost is embedded in the diesel price rather than itemised.

Why this matters for escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. A step removal of a per-litre charge is a reset event for every fuel escalator clause in the country. A contract whose surcharge formula was written around the charge either reprices — passing the saving to the customer — or does not, and keeps it. Which one happened is a contract question with a P&L answer, and it is dated: 2025-04-01.

Where the cost differs

  • Seasonal axle-weight restrictions. Spring thaw road bans cut allowable axle weights on secondary highways for several weeks each year, mostly in the Prairies and the North. Payload falls; tractor count, driver cost and insurance do not. That is a seasonal revenue-per-trip compression against a fixed cost base, and no US or UK benchmark contains it.
  • Winter roads. Northern resupply runs on ice roads for roughly a January-to-March window. Outside it the only options are air and barge, at multiples of the cost.
  • Weights and dimensions are higher domestically than transborder. Canadian configurations, particularly B-trains in the West, carry gross weights well above the US Interstate limit of 80,000 lb. A transborder load is sized to the foreign limit. Domestic equipment productivity therefore does not transfer to transborder lanes, and a blended cost per mile across both hides it.
  • Currency. Transborder revenue is frequently priced in US dollars against a Canadian dollar cost base. That is unhedged currency exposure inside file margin — written for forwarding file margin — transferring cleanly to trucking lane margin. Whether it is hedged, and at what point in the quote-to-settlement cycle, is usually undisclosed.
  • Length of haul is bimodal. Short, dense, high-frequency transborder lanes and very long, thin domestic ones. A single revenue-per-mile average across both describes neither. Read them as separate lane classes.
  • Two railways. Purchased linehaul in Model 7 has no competitive alternative on most lanes. See the mechanism table below.

Candidate missing mechanisms:

  • Duopoly purchased linehaul converting a variable input into an administered price. The financial signature is intermodal cost per container rising independently of volume, mix or fuel, with no lane-level alternative and regulated relief available only inside a 30 km interswitching radius — the 160 km pilot having sunset on 2025-03-20. This is a Canada-specific sharpening of uncontrolled purchased input behaving as a fixed cost and may warrant its own entry. The precondition the earlier draft treated as unresolved is now settled, and settled in the direction that strengthens the mechanism: there is no extended remedy. Evidence would come from CTA rail performance data against a Model 7 operator's disclosed cost per unit.
  • Seasonal payload restriction compressing revenue per trip against a fixed cost base. The signature is revenue per trip and tonnes per trip falling in the spring quarter with tractor count, driver cost and insurance flat. Testable against Mullen's monthly segment revenue, which is the only monthly series in this record.
  • Labour classification arbitrage depressing the market rate a compliant carrier must meet. The signature is a persistent driver cost per mile gap between carriers of similar model and geography, unexplained by wage rate or utilisation. Hard to evidence from filings alone; CRA and ESDC enforcement

What is sold here

What sells here follows the border and the seasonality.

Patterns that sell:

  • Document processing and customs entry automation CARM registration, portal maintenance, statement of account reconciliation and eManifest transmission are all recurring, deadline-driven and manual.
  • Carrier onboarding and compliance automation NSC safety fitness plus FAST and PIP status, and US authority checks for any carrier used on a transborder lane.
  • Post-acquisition logistics integration and carrier and customer master data consolidation the direct response to a serial-acquirer structure.
  • Freight audit and payment automation cross-border charges, duty disbursements, fuel surcharge verification after the 2025 carbon reset.
  • Fleet scheduling and dry-dock planning for Model 13, where the constraint is scheduling maintenance outside a fixed navigation season.
  • Transport workforce administration driver qualification files across two hours-of-service regimes.

Providers: Descartes is Canadian, listed on both the TSX and NASDAQ, and is a serial acquirer of customs and compliance software. That gives Canada the same shape Australia has with CargoWise — a domestic software champion in the customs and logistics layer, which raises local maturity in that layer while road and warehouse stacks stay mixed. applies to anything either vendor publishes about outcomes.

Harder preconditions:

  • No open Canadian road rate series. Loadlink's truck-to-load ratio is the nearest published Canadian market signal, and it measures balance, not price.
  • The private carrier population is unreadable, so a market-level business case cannot be built bottom-up from filings the way it can in the UK.
  • Bilingual operation is a real requirement in Quebec and federally, and it is a cost line in any workflow or service desk build.
