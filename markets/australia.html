The freight task

BITRE estimates the 2024-25 domestic freight task at 786 billion tonne kilometres. Rail carried about 447 billion tonne kilometres and road about 253 billion tonne kilometres. The highest-volume domestic flow is Pilbara iron ore rail. The next largest are Queensland and Hunter Valley coal rail, then bauxite by coastal shipping.

This makes the headline modal split misleading for general freight. Rail dominates tonnes-km because iron ore and coal are long-distance bulk flows. Road remains the dominant non-bulk mode. Infrastructure Australia states road carried almost 80% of non-bulk freight in 2024-25.

The practical split is corridor-based:

Freight task Typical mode Why
Pilbara iron ore, coal systems Heavy rail Dedicated corridors, high axle loads, mine-to-port density.
East coast capital-city general freight Road Distances are short enough that rail terminal time erodes linehaul cost advantage.
East-west interstate general freight Intermodal rail plus road drayage Perth lanes are long enough for rail labour and fuel efficiency to dominate.
Tasmania-mainland freight Coastal ro-ro and container shipping Sea leg is unavoidable; schedules and port windows set service.
Fresh produce and chilled food Refrigerated road, with some rail linehaul Pickup and delivery windows matter more than pure tonne-km. Lindsay is the client-set evidence.

Coastal shipping is important for Tasmania, aluminium, steel, petroleum and some primary-product flows. National Freight Data Hub projections keep coastal freight broadly stable at 105-110 billion tonne kilometres a year under the central scenario.

Regulation

The National Heavy Vehicle Regulator administers the Heavy Vehicle National Law for vehicles over 4.5 tonnes GVM across participating states. Western Australia and the Northern Territory remain separate in important respects. Chain of Responsibility makes consignors, loaders, schedulers and executives part of the safety duty.

Fair Work awards set minimum terms for transport labour. The Road Transport and Distribution Award covers road transport, tanker distribution, milk and cream, quarry materials and vehicle distribution. Long-distance operations have a separate award.

Coastal shipping is cabotage-shaped. Foreign-flagged ships need a temporary licence to carry domestic cargo between Australian ports. That keeps coastal shipping closer to protected liner logic than open international liner logic, but it is less closed than the Jones Act.

Ports are monitored by the ACCC. In 2024-25, the ACCC said stevedoring total real revenue per lift reached A$423.11 and landside charges were 49.5% of industry revenue. Terminal access charges alone were more than A$642m.

Where the cost differs

Labour and distance change the metrics.

  • Road labour: award wages, fatigue rules and distance make driver hours a binding cost. Read OR with linehaul distance and HPFV access.
  • Fuel: fuel cost per trip is more volatile on long interstate legs. Escalator timing matters where contracts reset slower than diesel.
  • Tolls: east coast metro collection and delivery legs can carry toll leakage that a US linehaul benchmark will miss.
  • Land: warehouse and port-adjacent industrial land is expensive near Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane. JLL reported national industrial vacancy at 4.8% in Q2 2026, with Perth tightest at 2.2%.
  • Ports: landside fees are a cost line imposed on road and rail operators, then passed to cargo owners. Treat them as accessorial pass-through and margin risk, not as a normal linehaul cost.

Candidate missing mechanisms:

  • Terminal access charge escalation without user bargaining power. The financial signature is rising port accessorial revenue per lift at stable productivity and spare capacity.
  • HPFV route-access fragmentation. The signature is excess trip count or subcontracted legs where vehicle productivity is lost at permit boundaries.
  • Weather and corridor outage concentration. Flood, fire and cyclone events can close sparse national corridors and force expensive mode substitution.

What is sold here

Australia buys practical systems when the prerequisite data work is funded. High labour cost makes warehouse labour instrumentation and transport workflow automation easier to justify than in lower-wage markets.

Patterns that sell:

  • Warehouse labour productivity instrumentation in grocery, retail and cold-chain DCs.
  • Event-driven visibility data pipeline where carriers accept telematics and CoR evidence requirements.
  • Freight audit and payment automation for port charges, rail accessorials, tolls and fuel.
  • Document processing and customs entry automation in import/export forwarding.
  • Quote-to-book workflow automation for repeat road and intermodal freight.

Harder preconditions:

  • There is no open road spot-rate series equal to DAT.
  • Private operators often do not disclose shipment counts or unit costs.
  • Port and rail contracts can include confidential access and terminal terms.

WiseTech makes Australia unusual. The country has a global forwarding software champion in CargoWise. That raises local software maturity for forwarding, while road and warehouse operators still run mixed TMS, WMS and telematics stacks.
