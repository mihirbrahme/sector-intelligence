The freight task

The freight task is port-led. MPA reported 2024 vessel arrival tonnage of 3.11 billion gross tons, cargo throughput of 622.67 million tonnes and container throughput of 41.12 million TEU. Around 90% of Singapore's container throughput is transhipment.

That changes the economics. A large share of the work is ship-to-ship container transfer, yard planning, berth windows, bunker coordination, feeder connectivity and documentation. Domestic trucking exists, but it is short-haul drayage between port, airport, warehouse, factory and border.

Singapore is also an air-cargo and high-value inventory market, though this file does not yet have a Changi cargo benchmark. The cross-border road task is mainly Malaysia-linked. MOT stated that an average 77,000 foreign-registered vehicles entered Singapore daily in 2024; 82% were motorcycles. ASEAN goods vehicles need an LTA goods vehicle permit.

Regulation

Singapore Customs makes the free trade zone regime central to logistics economics. Goods may sit in FTZs with duty and GST suspended pending transhipment or re-export. If goods enter customs territory, duty and GST become payable. TradeNet permits and document retention requirements shape forwarder workflow.

For transhipment, Singapore Customs distinguishes recognised transhipment cargo from goods requiring import or export permits even in an FTZ. Dutiable liquor and tobacco can generally stay in an FTZ for up to 30 days unless held under a through bill of lading or airway bill.

Labour rules shape warehouse, port and transport cost. MOM's services-sector foreign-worker regime uses a 35% dependency ratio ceiling. In 2026, monthly levies range from S$300 to S$800 by skill tier and dependency tier. From 2028, the first two tiers merge, with S$400 for higher-skilled and S$600 for basic-skilled workers.

Cross-border road freight is permit-shaped. ASEAN goods vehicles must hold an LTA ASEAN Goods Vehicle Permit tied to road tax and insurance, valid for up to six months.

Where the cost differs

Singapore compresses distance and inflates land and labour.

  • Road: miles per tractor is the wrong primary denominator. Use driver hours, turns per day, queue time, gate dwell and border delay.
  • Warehouse: land and rent push the model toward higher throughput per square metre, vertical space use, tighter slotting and automation.
  • Port: berth productivity, yard density, crane intensity and rotation integrity matter more than inland haul length.
  • Labour: foreign-worker quotas and levies make staffing a regulated input, especially in warehouses, port services and value-added logistics.
  • Fuel: marine bunkering is a hub economy. Fuel-cost exposure depends on vessel operator, bunker clause and contract form, not Singapore location alone.

JTC reported all-industrial occupancy of 89.1% in 2Q 2026. Treat that as land tightness context, not a logistics margin benchmark.

Candidate missing mechanisms:

  • FTZ permit exception leakage. The signature is rising rework, permit amendment cost or storage delay when cargo fails recognised transhipment criteria.
  • Foreign-worker quota constraint. The signature is rising overtime, subcontract labour, missed value-added service levels or slower warehouse turns when quota headroom tightens.
  • Hub bunching from external disruption. The signature is vessel arrivals, yard occupancy and overtime rising together after upstream route disruption. MPA's 2024 Red Sea response is evidence that this is a hub operating risk.

What is sold here

Singapore buys logistics systems where they protect hub velocity, compliance and space productivity.

Patterns that fit:

  • Document processing and customs entry automation for TradeNet permits, transhipment criteria and retained documents.
  • Event-driven visibility data pipeline for carrier, terminal, warehouse and customer milestones.
  • Exception triage and resolution workflow for missed connections, rolled cargo and permit exceptions.
  • Freight audit and payment automation for accessorials, port charges, bunker clauses and drayage invoices.
  • Warehouse labour productivity instrumentation where labour quotas make engineered standards worth maintaining.
  • Quote-to-book workflow automation in NVOCC, feeder and forwarding desks.

Harder preconditions:

  • Terminal and carrier operational data may be proprietary.
  • Public company accounts rarely isolate Singapore local P&L.
  • Local drayage is fragmented and private.

CargoWise maturity matters here. Forwarders and NVOCCs in Singapore are more likely to have process systems than small domestic road operators. The heavier white space is maritime operating software: voyage management, port-call workflow, bunker procurement and terminal planning providers are not yet mapped in provider landscape.
