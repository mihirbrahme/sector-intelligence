The freight task

Thailand's freight task is driven by industrial manufacturing, agricultural exports, and cross-border transit trade into the Greater Mekong Subregion (GMS).

Manufacturing clusters in the Eastern Economic Corridor (EEC), comprising Chonburi, Rayong, and Chachoengsao, and Greater Bangkok.

Automotive production dominates industrial logistics. Known as the "Detroit of Asia", Thailand produces commercial pickup trucks, passenger cars, and electric vehicles for global export.

Automotive assembly requires dedicated, just-in-time road transport linking tier-one parts suppliers in industrial estates to assembly plants.

Electronics and consumer appliances generate high-value air cargo through Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) and containerized sea freight.

Primary agricultural commodities originate in Northern, Northeastern (Isan), and Southern provinces. Processed poultry, canned tuna, rice, rubber, and cassava move southward to Bangkok and deepwater ports.

Port of Laem Chabang handles over 80% of Thailand's international container volume, processing approximately 9.5 million TEUs annually.

Laem Chabang accommodates mainline transpacific and European container vessels, bypassing river draft limitations.

Bangkok Port (Klong Toey), situated upriver on the Chao Phraya River, is draft-restricted to vessels under 12,000 DWT and approximately 1,500 TEUs. It handles intra-Asia regional feeder trade.

Overland transport is overwhelmingly road-dominated, carrying over 80% of domestic freight tonnage.

Thailand acts as the overland gateway to Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar, and southern China. The southern corridor connects through the Sadao border into Peninsular Malaysia.

The State Railway of Thailand (SRT) holds under 3% of domestic freight share. However, SRT operates a vital container shuttle linking Ladkrabang Inland Container Depot (ICD) to Laem Chabang.

State enterprises maintain conservative concession models. PAT derives stable stevedoring royalties while terminal concessionaires absorb volume volatility.

Domestic conglomerates benefit from industrial parentage. SCGJWD benefits from captive cement and building material freight generated by Siam Cement Group.

Japanese logistics providers control captive tier-one automotive supply chain flows, creating high barriers for independent competitors.

Regulation

Foreign equity limits

The Foreign Business Act B.E. 2542 (1999) restricts foreign ownership in domestic road transport, coastal shipping, and freight forwarding to 49%.

Foreign operators must establish majority Thai joint ventures or secure Board of Investment (BOI) logistics promotion privileges.

BOI privileges provide corporate tax exemptions and allow majority foreign ownership for specialized logistics distribution centers.

Road transport and telematics mandates

The Department of Land Transport (DLT) regulates commercial trucking under the Land Transport Act B.E. 2522.

DLT mandates real-time GPS tracking installation on all commercial freight trucks. GPS data feeds continuously into DLT monitoring centers to enforce speed limits and driving hours.

Cabotage restrictions

The Thai Vessel Act B.E. 2481 restricts domestic coastal shipping to Thai-flagged vessels.

Shipowning entities must maintain at least 70% Thai equity ownership to register vessels for domestic coastal trade.

Customs and National Single Window

Customs clearance is administered by the Thai Customs Department through electronic customs (e-Customs) and the Thailand National Single Window (Thai NSW).

Customs audits and retrospective tariff reclassifications represent a major compliance risk for multinational shippers and forwarders.

Cross-border GMS frameworks

Cross-border trucking into Laos, Cambodia, and Myanmar is governed by the Greater Mekong Subregion Cross-Border Transport Agreement (CBTA).

Despite bilateral agreements, domestic regulatory friction often requires cargo transloading or trailer-swapping at border checkpoints such as Mae Sot and Sadao.

Labour regulation

The Labour Protection Act sets employment conditions. Logistics, warehousing, and port loading operations rely on registered migrant workers from Myanmar, Cambodia, and Laos.

Daily minimum wages vary by province, averaging THB 330 to THB 400 per day ($10 to $12 USD per day).

Where the cost differs

Thailand combines moderate labor rates with efficient industrial road infrastructure along export corridors.

  • Labour: Warehouse base wages average THB 10,000 to THB 14,000 monthly ($300 to $400 USD). Wages are higher than Vietnam or Indonesia, supporting selective warehouse automation.
  • Fuel: Diesel prices have historically been stabilized near THB 30 to THB 33 per litre through the State Oil Fuel Fund. Subsidy phase-outs create fuel price volatility.
  • Highway tolls: Motorway 7 provides high-speed transit between Bangkok and Laem Chabang. Tolls represent a predictable operating cost.
  • Urban congestion: Severe traffic congestion in the Bangkok Metropolitan Region increases fuel burn and limits delivery vehicle turns per day.
  • Cross-border border dwell: Border customs clearance queues and mandatory cargo transloading add 24 to 48 hours to regional road transit times.
  • Metric impact: Truck utilization along the EEC corridor is high. However, revenue per employee in metric dictionary reflects lower domestic tariff yields compared to US and European baselines.

Candidate missing mechanisms

  • Cross-border border transloading breakage and dwell delay: Mandatory border cargo transloading causes freight damage, manual labor expense, and equipment idle days.
  • Thai customs retrospective tariff reclassification penalty: Retrospective customs valuation audits penalize forwarders and importers on previously cleared HS codes.
  • Automotive OEM JIT sequencing penalty: Contractual penalties imposed by vehicle assemblers when highway congestion delays component deliveries to assembly lines.

What is sold here

What buyers purchase

Automotive 3PLs and multinational forwarders purchase enterprise WMS, TMS, and visibility systems. Domestic trucking companies invest primarily in mandatory GPS telematics and fuel monitoring.

Key solution fits: - Document processing and customs entry automation: Automates e-Customs submissions and Thai NSW declaration workflows. - Event-driven visibility data pipeline: Integrates DLT-mandated truck GPS data with warehouse arrival milestones. - Detention and demurrage dispute recovery: Audits container dwell and disputed demurrage charges at Laem Chabang. - Warehouse labour productivity instrumentation: Optimizes picking and packing labor in large EEC contract logistics distribution centers.

Harder preconditions

  • Small family-run transport operators lack structured TMS platforms.
  • Border transloading practices create visibility blind spots where freight changes vehicles.
  • Port Authority legacy systems limit real-time direct container status API feeds.
