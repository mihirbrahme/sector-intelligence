The freight task

The defining number is not tonnage. It is haul length. DfT put the average length of haul for GB-registered HGVs at 105 km in 2025, down from 106 km in 2024 — 134 km for articulated vehicles and 58 km for rigids. A US transcontinental truckload runs 2,000-plus miles. Almost all UK domestic freight is a single-shift, out-and-back or multi-drop movement.

The rest of the 2025 picture, from the same series: 1.53 billion tonnes lifted (down 3% on 2024) and 162 billion tonne-kilometres moved (down 4%). GB HGVs ran 5,897 million kilometres empty, 31% of the 18,975 million total vehicle kilometres.

Do not set that 31% against the US empty-mile figure. The US series measures empty miles for truckload carriers; the DfT series covers all GB-registered HGVs including rigids on multi-drop work, where a vehicle returns empty by design. The denominators describe different jobs. Read the UK figure against itself over time.

That kills the truckload linehaul margin. There is not enough cost inside one load to support a broker's spread between a shipper price and a carrier price. It is why Model 1 has no UK market, and why comparing a UK haulier to a US truckload carrier on revenue per mile is meaningless — the denominators describe different jobs.

Freight task Typical mode Why
Retail and grocery replenishment Road, from a regional DC, multi-drop Population density puts most of GB within a day's return run of a DC.
Palletised industrial groupage Pallet network hub-and-spoke Overnight trunking into a central hub, sorted, trunked out. The UK's own answer to LTL.
Deep-sea import containers Ocean into Felixstowe, Southampton, London Gateway, then road or rail Import-dominated flows. Empty repositioning is a structural cost.
EU trade Accompanied and unaccompanied road trailers via Dover, Eurotunnel, and the North Sea ports Since 2021 every one of these movements is a customs movement.
Bulk aggregates, fuel, cement Road tanker and tipper; some rail Short haul, high density, dedicated fleets.
Long-distance intermodal Rail from the deep-sea ports to inland terminals Works on the Felixstowe and Southampton corridors, marginal elsewhere.

Rail freight is a small share of the task. Its economics work on the deep-sea port corridors and on bulk, and nowhere much else, because the island is not long enough. Coastal shipping is minor outside bulk and the Northern Isles.

Pallet networks are the structural fact most easily missed. Palletways, Pall-Ex, Fortec and the rest are member-owned or franchise networks in which independent hauliers trunk into a shared hub. Economically they perform the LTL function — consolidating part-loads over a network — but the network is a cooperative, not a single balance sheet. So the UK has the LTL job without an LTL company, and no operator publishes shipments, weight per shipment or operating ratio the way Old Dominion does.

The operator landscape

Road haulage is fragmented. Tens of thousands of operator licences are in issue, and the median holder runs a handful of vehicles. Contract logistics is concentrated among a small number of large groups. Ports are privately owned and concentrated by corridor. Forwarding has a very long tail — the population evidence is unusually good here and is set out under Data Availability.

Regulation

HMRC CDS — the Customs Declaration Service

CDS replaced CHIEF as the UK customs declaration platform. Import declarations migrated on 2022-09-30 and exports completed in 2024, after repeated extensions. CHIEF is closed.

CDS is built on the Union Customs Code data model, and it requires materially more data elements per declaration than CHIEF did. That is the origin of most of what follows commercially. A declaration is no longer a form a clerk fills; it is a structured dataset assembled from the commercial invoice, the packing list, the transport document and the client's own master data. Where that data is incomplete, someone chases it, and the chasing is the cost.

Practical mechanics that shape a forwarder's month:

  • EORI registration is required to move goods, and CDS access runs through a Government Gateway subscription.
  • Simplified procedures. A trader authorised for simplified declarations files a reduced frontier declaration at the border and a supplementary declaration afterwards, due by the fourth working day of the following month. That creates a hard monthly processing peak with a statutory deadline — a predictable, recurring overtime and agency-labour spike.
  • Declaration volume is the unit of work for a UK customs operation, not shipment value and not tonnage.

This is the substrate for the UK customs software layer and the direct cause of systems programme trailing the forwarding process it was bought to fix where a systems programme trails the process it was bought to fix.

Duty deferment accounts and customs guarantees

This is the single most important commercial mechanic in UK forwarding, and it is invisible in a revenue line.

  • A Duty Deferment Account lets a trader defer customs duty and, where applicable, import VAT to the 15th of the following month, collected by direct debit. Without one, duty is payable at the frontier before release.
  • A DDA normally requires a Customs Comprehensive Guarantee from a bank or insurer. HMRC permits guarantee waivers, approved on financial standing, which removes the bank line but not the exposure.
  • Postponed VAT Accounting, available since 2021-01-01, lets an importer account for import VAT on its VAT return rather than paying it at the frontier. That took import VAT out of the cash cycle for the importer. It did not remove the duty leg, and it did not change who advances it.

What actually happens. A forwarder lends its DDA to its clients. It funds their duty on its own account for up to roughly 45 days between the movement and the deferment collection, then recovers on commercial terms that are usually slower still. the record has already measured the result: UK forwarder customs duty advance debtor days of 70 to 80 on accounts carrying duty and VAT disbursement, against 30-day commercial freight terms, from Companies House statutory filings, registered in benchmark register.

That is forwarding receivable extension funded on gross turnover with a UK-specific driver. A forwarder that grows file count grows a duty advance it funds from working capital, and the funding requirement scales with duty value, which moves with tariff policy and commodity mix rather than with its own service revenue. Charges registered against the company are the external evidence that the facility exists — see charges registers provide external records of forwarder receivables financing.

Port community systems and GVMS

The UK has no single national port system. It has two operating regimes, and which one applies decides whether a truck can board.

  • Inventory-linked ports. Container and general cargo ports run inventory systems — Destin8 and CNS are the two long-standing community systems. Goods cannot be released until the customs declaration is matched against the port's inventory record. The match is the choke point, and a mismatch is a physical delay, not a paperwork query.
  • Non-inventory-linked roll-on roll-off locations. Dover, Eurotunnel and the ferry ports run on the Goods Vehicle Movement Service. The haulier must hold a goods movement reference linking every declaration on the vehicle before it can board. No GMR, no boarding.

GVMS is the hardest single constraint on UK–EU road freight, and it is where the customs failure becomes a transport failure. One missing declaration among thirty on a groupage trailer stops the trailer, not the consignment. That asymmetry — one consignment's defect stopping a whole vehicle's revenue — is the specific UK

The Border Target Operating Model

Published August 2023, phased through 2024:

  • 2024-01-31 — export health certificates required for medium-risk animal products, plants and plant products imported from the EU.
  • 2024-04-30 — documentary, identity and physical checks begin at Border Control Posts, and the Common User Charge starts at Sevington for goods entering via Dover and Eurotunnel.
  • 2024-10-31 — safety and security declarations required on EU imports, on a reduced dataset.

The Common User Charge is levied per commodity line on a common health entry document, for goods entering Great Britain through Dover or Eurotunnel and using the Sevington border control post. The rates:

Category Per commodity line Cap per CHED
Animal products, medium and high risk — imports £29 £145
Plants and plant products, medium risk A and high risk — imports £29 £145
High-risk food and feed of non-animal origin — imports £29 £145
Animal products, low risk — imports £10 £50
Animal product transits, all risk categories £10 £50

A maximum of five commodity lines is charged per CHED, however many the document carries.

The structure matters more than the rate. The charge scales with lines and documents, not with consignment value or weight. A full load of one commodity on one CHED pays £29. A groupage trailer carrying many consignments carries many CHEDs, each charged separately up to its own cap, so the same vehicle can carry a multiple of the cost while carrying no more freight. It taxes exactly the business model a small forwarder runs, and it is a fixed cost per movement.

Read together with forwarding operations establishment fixed against falling file count. BTOM raised the fixed establishment cost of running a UK forwarding operation — declarants, BCP scheduling, veterinary documentation — in a period when file counts were not growing. That is the mechanism's precondition, delivered by regulation rather than by the cycle.

The Windsor Framework governs GB to Northern Ireland movements through the UK Internal Market Scheme, with separate green and red lane arrangements and a distinct parcels regime. It is a different rule set for a domestic movement, and it is the reason a GB–NI trailer is not a domestic trailer.

A UK–EU sanitary and phytosanitary agreement is under negotiation and is the largest single live variable in UK freight regulation. The UK government published its first substantive update on 2026-03-09. The proposal is a shared SPS zone with the UK dynamically aligning to a wide body of EU SPS law. If concluded, export health certificates, phytosanitary certificates and routine border checks would be removed for most agrifood goods, with checks retained on live animals and germinal products.

It is not in force and the detail is not settled. What it would do to this geography is unusually large: it removes most of the Common User Charge base, reverses the establishment-cost increase that BTOM imposed, and takes out a whole category of documentary work that UK forwarders currently sell. Anything in 05-solutions/ priced against SPS documentation volume carries that risk.

Operator licensing

The Goods Vehicle (Licensing of Operators) Act 1995 governs. Any operator using vehicles over 3.5 tonnes for hire and reward, or in connection with a trade, needs an O-licence: restricted, standard national, or standard international.

Four requirements, each of which can end a business:

  • Financial standing. For a standard licence, available capital and reserves of £8,000 for the first authorised vehicle and £4,500 for each additional one; for a restricted licence, £3,100 and £1,700. The sums are reset annually by the Senior Traffic Commissioner. It is a continuous test, not a test at grant: a Traffic Commissioner can demand evidence at any time, and usually does at renewal and after a compliance failure. For a ten-vehicle haulier that is roughly £48,500 of demonstrable liquidity held against nothing productive.
  • Good repute of the operator and of the transport manager.
  • Professional competence — a CPC-qualified transport manager exercising continuous and effective responsibility.
  • A suitable operating centre, advertised locally and open to objection.

Traffic Commissioners — eight regional office-holders — grant, curtail, suspend and revoke licences, and can disqualify a transport manager personally. Their decisions are published in Notices and Proceedings.

A public inquiry decision is externally generated, dated, and describes what actually went wrong inside a haulier — maintenance, drivers' hours falsification, financial standing failure. It is, and it is the closest thing the UK has to the US litigation record. Nothing in the US registry system produces a narrative account of an operator's failure the way a UK public inquiry decision does.

Since May 2022, vans and light goods vehicles between 2.5 and 3.5 tonnes used for hire and reward on international journeys require a standard international O-licence. That brought a previously unlicensed fleet class into the regime.

DVSA enforces at roadside and through the Operator Compliance Risk Score, which sets stop probability, and runs the Earned Recognition scheme for operators sharing compliance data.

Driver CPC

Professional HGV drivers must hold a Driver Qualification Card, maintained by 35 hours of periodic training every five years. DVSA reforms took effect on 2024-12-03, splitting the qualification in two. The National Driver CPC, for UK-only driving, allows training in blocks of 3.5 hours rather than 7. The International Driver CPC, required to drive in the EU, keeps the 7-hour course. Both now allow up to 12 of the 35 hours by eLearning. The 35-hour total did not change.

Two operational consequences:

  • A fixed per-driver annual training cost, plus the wage cost of the training day itself.
  • A cliff, not a slope. An expired DQC removes the driver from service immediately. It is a scheduled, foreseeable capacity loss that nonetheless routinely surprises operators, which makes it exactly the kind of thing transport workforce administration is sold against.

This section determines the ceiling on UK company analysis, and it is the opposite of the US position.

The obligation. Under the Companies Act 2006, sections 381 to 444, medium and large companies filing full, medium or group accounts must file a profit and loss account disclosing turnover, cost of sales, operating profit and average employee headcount. Small companies and micro-entities are exempt under section 444 and file balance sheets with minimal notes. They disclose no turnover.

What this means in practice.

  1. The UK gives audited revenue and headcount for private companies that the US never gives at any size. A £60m private forwarder is readable. A $2bn US private carrier is not. That is why the forwarding mechanisms forwarding cost pass-through lag on the open shipment file to volume-tender dilution of forwarding portfolio yield were written on UK evidence.
  2. It gives it late and once a year. A private company files within nine months of its accounting reference date, so the data can be nearly two years old on arrival. There is no interim disclosure and no equivalent of the 8-K. Movement between filings is invisible. See annual filing cadence sweeps lag accounting disclosures.
  3. iXBRL decides whether it is machine-readable. Electronic filings tag core:TurnoverRevenue and can be extracted directly. Scanned paper filings cannot, and 1,055 of the 1,892 are scans.
  4. SIC codes do not identify the operating model. See SIC codes fail to distinguish transport operating models. Classification must be settled from the accounts and the description, never from the code — root.

What is changing, and what is not. The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 requires identity verification for directors and persons with significant control. Two accounts changes follow, both commencing April 2028, and the difference between them decides what this record gains.

  • Small companies and micro-entities will have to file a profit and loss account — but may opt out of publishing it on the public register. The P&L goes to Companies House and is available to HMRC and law enforcement. The government has said the opt-out addresses privacy and commercial-risk concerns from smaller businesses, and that the mechanics will be confirmed later. Assume the opt-out is taken. Filing is not disclosure, and the roughly 56,000 micro and small audit-exempt T&L companies should not be planned for as a future readable population.
  • All accounts must be filed in iXBRL using commercial software. Web and paper filing for accounts closes. This is the change that actually helps. Of the 1,892 companies that already disclose turnover, 1,055 file scanned paper that cannot be parsed. From April 2028 that population is machine- readable, and the extractable share of the disclosing cohort roughly triples without a single new disclosure obligation.

The lesson generalises: an obligation to file is not an obligation to publish, and only the second one reaches this record.

Other Companies House filings that carry signal: the charges register, which dates a facility and names the lender; officer changes, where an en bloc resignation usually precedes something; a change of accounting reference date, which usually precedes a transaction; and overdue accounts, which are a fact about the company rather than about our search.

Where the cost differs

  • Fuel carries a large fixed tax wedge. Duty on standard diesel is 52.95p per litre, frozen in cash terms since 2011-12 and including the temporary 5p cut introduced in 2022-23. The cut has been extended repeatedly; the Prime Minister confirmed on 2026-05-20 that it runs to 2026-12-31, scrapping the staged rises pencilled in for autumn and winter. Changes due in December 2026 now take effect 2027-01-01, and from April 2027 duty is due to rise with inflation annually, ending the freeze. VAT sits on top. A UK haulier's diesel line and a US carrier's are not the same quantity, and a US fuel surcharge benchmark does not transfer. The pass-through mechanics base peg, reset frequency — do transfer, which is escalator and surcharge recovery lag under input inflation. April 2027 is a scheduled escalator test for every UK haulage contract.
  • The HGV levy was reintroduced in August 2023, banded by weight and emissions class, and applies to UK and foreign vehicles.
  • Employer National Insurance rose to 15% with the secondary threshold cut to £5,000 from April 2025. In a business where labour is the dominant cost and the workforce is large and low-to-mid paid, that is a direct, non-recoverable margin hit in the year it landed. Contract logistics operators with indexed contracts recovered it; hauliers on spot work largely did not.
  • National Living Wage rose to £12.21 an hour for those aged 21 and over from April 2025, which sets the floor for warehouse and yard labour.
  • Business rates on warehouse property are a UK-specific fixed occupancy cost with no direct US or Australian analogue, and they do not fall when throughput does.
  • Urban access charges. London's ULEZ, Clean Air Zones in several English cities, and the Direct Vision Standard, which from October 2024 requires a three-star rating or a Progressive Safe System retrofit for HGVs entering Greater London. That is a capex on the London-serving fleet specifically, not on the fleet as a whole.

What this does to the metrics. Cost per mile is the wrong unit. Use cost per vehicle per week, cost per drop, cost per pallet and drops per shift for road. For forwarding, use gross-to-net spread per file, files per operator per day, and debtor days — with the duty advance separated out, because blending it makes the receivable look like a collections failure when it is a funding decision.

What is sold here

What sells here is customs and compliance work, because that is where the regulation put the cost. What does not sell is anything priced against a rate index, because there is not one.

Patterns that sell:

  • Document processing and customs entry automation the core UK play. CDS data elements, supplementary declaration cadence, commercial invoice extraction.
  • Port community system integration Destin8 and CNS inventory matching, and GVMS reference generation.
  • Freight audit and payment automation port charges, ferry and Eurotunnel charges, demurrage, disbursements.
  • Transport finance and working capital the duty advance is a funding problem before it is a collections problem.
  • Transport workforce administration Driver CPC and DQC expiry, tachograph analysis, O-licence compliance evidence.
  • Order management and booking desk and shipper and consignee service desk the labour that the customs layer created.

Providers concentrated here: the UK customs software layer, Descartes, CargoWise — WiseTech has acquired regional customs software repeatedly and UK declaration software is exactly the asset class it buys — and forwarding platforms generally.

Harder preconditions:

  • No open road rate series. Nothing can be priced against a published buy rate.
  • Small forwarders buy declarations software, not platforms. Enterprise TMS implementation timelines of 12 to 24 months, with system integrator cost at two to four times licence, are outside what a £30m forwarder can fund — see benchmark register and systems programme trailing the forwarding process it was bought to fix.
  • Pallet network economics are split across members, so a network-level business case has no single balance sheet to land on.
